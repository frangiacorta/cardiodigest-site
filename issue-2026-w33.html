Tier 1

Practice-changing

CLEAN — Intravenous Nicorandil in STEMI Undergoing Primary PCI
Huang D et al. · J Am Coll Cardiol 2026 · primary source ↗
Structured abstract
Background
Nicorandil, an ATP-sensitive potassium-channel opener with nitrate-like properties, may reduce reperfusion injury and microvascular obstruction in STEMI, but large-scale randomised evidence on long-term clinical outcomes has been inconclusive.
Methods
Multicentre, double-blind, placebo-controlled RCT at 49 hospitals in China. Patients aged 18–80 with STEMI within 12 hours of symptom onset were randomised 1:1 to IV nicorandil (6 mg bolus before reperfusion, then 6 mg/h for 48 h) or matching placebo; oral nicorandil was prohibited during follow-up. Primary outcome: composite of cardiovascular death, nonfatal MI, target-vessel revascularisation, or unplanned heart-failure hospitalisation at 12 months.
Key results
1,503 patients were randomised (nicorandil n=748, placebo n=755). The primary composite occurred in 98 patients (13.1%) with nicorandil and 99 (13.1%) with placebo (rate ratio 0.869; 95% CI 0.650–1.162; P=0.3429). Among secondary outcomes there were nominal reductions in cardiovascular death (1.9% vs 3.6%; HR 0.515, 95% CI 0.269–0.983) and target-vessel revascularisation (1.1% vs 3.0%; HR 0.322, 95% CI 0.143–0.727), while nonfatal MI and unplanned HF hospitalisation were similar. Adverse events did not differ.
Conclusion
Adjunctive IV nicorandil did not significantly reduce the 12-month primary composite; the findings do not support routine use in unselected STEMI.
Critical appraisal
Study design
Adequately sized, double-blind, placebo-controlled multicentre RCT with a patient-relevant composite — a substantial upgrade over prior surrogate-endpoint nicorandil studies.
Clinical relevance
Answers a recurring cath-lab question directly: routine IV nicorandil peri-primary-PCI does not move hard 12-month outcomes in an all-comer STEMI population.
Context
Consistent with the broader disappointment of pharmacological cardioprotection adjuncts in reperfusion; the nominal signals on cardiovascular death and TVR are hypothesis-generating only.
Limitations
Single-country (China) enrolment limits generalisability; the secondary reductions are unadjusted for multiplicity and were not accompanied by a primary-endpoint benefit.
Bottom line: Do not adopt routine IV nicorandil for unselected STEMI on the basis of a neutral primary endpoint — reserve any interest for hypothesis-testing of the mortality/TVR signals.
PROVISION — Angiography-Derived FFR to Guide Revascularisation
Tanigaki T et al. · JACC Cardiovasc Interv 2026 · primary source ↗
Structured abstract
Background
Pressure-wire FFR is the reference standard for physiologic lesion assessment but is underused because of the need for hyperaemia, procedural complexity, and wire-related risk. Angiography-derived FFR offers a wire- and hyperaemia-free alternative, but its impact on treatment decisions is uncertain.
Methods
Prospective, multicentre RCT randomising patients with stable coronary disease 1:1 to angiography-derived FFR-guided or pressure-wire FFR-guided revascularisation. Primary endpoint: noninferiority of angiography-derived FFR for the revascularisation rate (proportion undergoing PCI or CABG based on physiologic assessment); secondary endpoint: 1-year MACE (cardiac death, any MI, or ischaemia-driven target-vessel revascularisation).
Key results
401 patients (483 vessels) at 13 Japanese centres. Revascularisation was performed in 41.4% (angiography-derived FFR) vs 37.9% (pressure-wire FFR); absolute difference 3.5% (95% CI −6.0 to 13.1; P for noninferiority=0.049). At 1 year, MACE occurred in 8.4% vs 10.1% (HR 0.84; 95% CI 0.44–1.60; P=0.591).
Conclusion
Angiography-derived FFR-guided revascularisation was noninferior to a pressure-wire FFR strategy for determining revascularisation in stable coronary disease, warranting confirmation in larger outcome-driven trials.
Critical appraisal
Study design
Randomised head-to-head of two physiology strategies with a clinically meaningful decision endpoint, rather than a diagnostic-accuracy comparison alone.
Clinical relevance
Supports angiography-derived FFR as a practical, wire-free option for physiologic guidance where wire FFR is impractical or declined.
Context
Adds a decision-and-outcome layer to prior accuracy studies of angio-derived indices; fits the field's move toward image-/angiography-based physiology.
Limitations
The noninferiority P value (0.049) sits at the margin; single-country, modest sample, and the study is underpowered for hard clinical outcomes.
Bottom line: Angiography-derived FFR is a reasonable wire-free alternative for revascularisation decisions in stable disease, but a larger outcomes trial is needed before it replaces the wire.
CHOICE-MI — Transfemoral Transcatheter Mitral Valve Replacement
Strotmann L et al. · JACC Cardiovasc Interv 2026 · primary source ↗
Structured abstract
Background
Transcatheter mitral valve replacement (TMVR) is an option for mitral regurgitation (MR), but data on transfemoral (TF) devices are limited.
Methods
Analysis of TF TMVR within the international CHOICE-MI registry (41 centres), with outcomes assessed by Mitral Valve Academic Research Consortium criteria to 2 years. Primary endpoint: composite of all-cause mortality or heart-failure hospitalisation at 2 years.
Key results
124 patients (median age 79 years, 50% women, median EuroSCORE II 5.0%) underwent TF TMVR with 9 different devices; primary MR was the leading aetiology (39.5%) and mitral annular calcification was present in 12.9%. Technical success was 91.1% (113/124) with procedural mortality of 0.8% (1/124); residual MR ≤1+ was achieved in 94.9% at discharge. Cardiovascular mortality was 14.7% and 20.3%, and the primary composite 44.1% and 52.4%, at 1 and 2 years; NYHA class improved significantly, atrial fibrillation independently predicted the primary endpoint, and technical success was associated with survival.
Conclusion
Dedicated TF TMVR devices delivered favourable safety, durable MR reduction, and sustained functional improvement in high-risk patients.
Critical appraisal
Study design
Large multicentre registry of a genuinely novel therapy, with MVARC-defined endpoints and 2-year follow-up.
Clinical relevance
Documents that a transfemoral (rather than transapical) route is technically achievable with low procedural mortality — an important step for TMVR access.
Context
Positions TF TMVR alongside M-TEER for anatomies unsuitable for repair; the high late event rate mirrors the comorbid, high-risk populations these devices currently reach.
Limitations
Non-randomised registry pooling 9 devices, modest sample, and a >50% 2-year death/HF rate underline that patient selection and residual risk remain unresolved.
Bottom line: Transfemoral TMVR is feasible with high technical success and durable MR reduction, but the steep late event curve means it remains a last-resort option pending randomised data.
J-PRIDE — Definite vs Marginal FFR/NHPR Discordance
Shiono Y et al. · JACC Cardiovasc Interv 2026 · source ↗

This registry analysis stratified 4,304 lesions from 3,200 patients into six groups by the magnitude of discordance between FFR and nonhyperaemic pressure ratios (NHPR). Compared with concordant-negative lesions, groups with definite discordance carried higher 1-year MACE (FFR+/NHPR− aHR 1.98, 95% CI 1.07–3.68; FFR−/NHPR+ aHR 2.18, 95% CI 1.18–4.04), and the FFR−/NHPR+ group also had higher all-cause mortality (aHR 2.57), whereas marginal discordance did not. The clinical message is that how far apart the two indices sit matters — definite discordance flags prognostically important lesions, while marginal disagreement can be interpreted more calmly.

ASCoP — Complete vs Incomplete Revascularisation in Severe AS Undergoing CHIP + TAVI
Tartaglia F et al. · Struct Heart 2026 · source ↗

This 14-centre registry analysed 550 patients who underwent complex/high-risk indicated PCI (CHIP) and transfemoral TAVI between 2013 and 2023, stratified by completeness of revascularisation (CR achieved in 54.4%). The 1-year composite of all-cause death or unplanned cardiovascular hospitalisation did not differ significantly (20.1% CR vs 27.3% IR; HR 0.72, 95% CI 0.49–1.07), but CR was associated with fewer cardiovascular rehospitalisations (14.8% vs 22.8%; HR 0.61, 95% CI 0.38–0.97) even after adjustment. The signal favours aiming for complete revascularisation in this comorbid CHIP-plus-TAVI population, though confirmation is needed.

— — Off-Label Microaxial LVAD Use and Mortality
Murphy SJ et al. · Cardiovasc Revasc Med 2026 · source ↗

Using the National Inpatient Sample (2015–2022), this study classified 31,263 Impella CP hospitalisations as on-label (cardiogenic shock, COVID-19 with ECMO, or high-risk PCI) or off-label, finding 6.4% — roughly 1 in 15 — were off-label. Off-label recipients were younger (61.0 vs 65.6 years) with fewer comorbidities, yet had higher in-hospital mortality (37.5% vs 27.9%; P<0.0001), longer stays, and lower rates of discharge home. The finding is a cautionary flag against device use outside approved indications, though residual confounding in claims data limits causal inference.

Tier 2

Notable

— — VExUS Score and Hepatorenal Congestion After Tricuspid Intervention
Rodríguez-Pérez Á et al. · JACC Cardiovasc Interv 2026 · source ↗

This prospective multicentre cohort of 64 patients (mean age 76.7 years, 76.6% women) undergoing transcatheter tricuspid valve intervention (edge-to-edge repair 60.9%, annuloplasty 18.7%, orthotopic replacement 14.1%) measured venous congestion by the Venous Excess Ultrasound (VExUS) score before and after the procedure. At baseline 70% had VExUS grade 3, and within 24 hours 66% were reclassified to VExUS ≤1 despite no change in diuretic dose or weight. Rapid decongestion after tricuspid intervention offers a bedside prognostic and physiologic readout, though the cohort is small.

OCEAN-Mitral — M-TEER in Functional MR With Very Severe LV Dysfunction
Omote K et al. · JACC Adv 2026 · source ↗

From the OCEAN-Mitral registry, patients with functional MR and LVEF ≤40% (n=1,538) were compared by LVEF <20% (n=103) versus 20–40% (n=1,435) — a group largely excluded from M-TEER trials. Acute procedural success was similarly high (100% vs 97%) and in-hospital mortality did not differ (5% vs 4%), with the 1-year composite of death or HF hospitalisation not significantly different after adjustment. M-TEER appears feasible and safe even at the extreme low end of ejection fraction, though functional status remained worse in the <20% group.

— — Early vs Delayed M-TEER in Degenerative MR
Shimura T et al. · Am J Cardiol 2026 · source ↗

This analysis of 1,061 patients with degenerative MR undergoing M-TEER compared an early group (≤1 year after the DMR-heart-failure diagnosis, n=667) with a delayed group (>1 year, n=394). The early group had less cardiac damage at baseline, and at 3 years had lower mortality (25.1% vs 32.7%; P=0.013) and a lower death/HF-hospitalisation composite (34.1% vs 45.8%), with earlier intervention independently associated with lower mortality (HR 0.70, 95% CI 0.52–0.95). The data argue against watchful waiting once degenerative MR causes heart failure, acknowledging the non-randomised design.

— — Selective vs Routine Femoral Angiography After TF-TAVI
von Ehr A et al. · JACC Adv 2026 · source ↗

This two-site study compared routine (n=674) versus selective (n=473) post-procedural iliofemoral angiography across 1,147 transfemoral TAVI cases, using VARC-3 definitions. Overall vascular/access-site complications were similar (selective 6.6% vs routine 8.0%; OR 0.76, 95% CI 0.48–1.19), and routine angiography detected more access-site bleeds (13.9% vs 5.2%) without differences in haemoglobin drop, transfusion, or bleeding-related death. Routine angiographic surveillance mostly surfaces minor bleeds of uncertain clinical import, supporting a selective, suspicion-driven approach.

— — DCB-Only vs DES for Large-Vessel B2/C Lesions
Bando R et al. · Cardiovasc Revasc Med 2026 · source ↗

This single-centre, propensity-matched study compared a drug-coated-balloon-only strategy (after successful lesion preparation) with drug-eluting stents in de novo ACC/AHA type B2/C lesions in large vessels (≥3.0 mm), analysing 113 matched pairs. Over a mean follow-up of 1,344.5 days, target-lesion failure occurred in 5 DCB vs 11 DES patients (3-year cumulative 6.0% vs 11.3%; HR 0.48, 95% CI 0.17–1.36; P=0.159), and DCB was not independently associated with TLF. The authors frame this as hypothesis-generating support for a "leave-nothing-behind" option in carefully selected large-vessel lesions, not proof of equivalence.

REVIVAL — Complete vs Incomplete Revascularisation for PCI After TAVI
Pivato CA et al. · Open Heart 2026 · source ↗

The multicentre REVIVAL registry analysed 447 patients undergoing PCI after TAVI (2008–2023), classifying them as complete (70.5%) or incomplete (29.5%) revascularisation. The 4-year incidence of MACE (cardiovascular death, MI, or stroke) was essentially identical (35.0% ICR vs 34.7% CR; HR 0.93, 95% CI 0.62–1.39), with adjusted analysis confirming no significant difference. In this post-TAVI setting, pursuing anatomical completeness did not translate into fewer hard events, contrasting with the CHIP-plus-TAVI rehospitalisation signal seen in ASCoP.

— — Predicting THV Underexpansion and Balloon Postdilatation Effect
Akodad M et al. · Struct Heart 2026 · source ↗

This study used patient-specific finite-element simulation from pre-TAVI CT to predict transcatheter heart valve (THV) underexpansion and the effect of staged balloon postdilatation, in patients who underwent postdilatation for symptomatic THV dysfunction. For balloon-expandable valves, differences between CT-measured and simulated expansion were small (e.g. −3.27% ± 4.67% at the outflow, P=0.03; other regions non-significant), and postdilatation effects were likewise well predicted. Computational modelling could help anticipate underexpansion before the index procedure, though the analysed cohort was selected and retrospective.

— — Conduction Recovery and Permanent Pacing in Bicuspid AV Post-TAVR
Alhallak I et al. · Cardiovasc Revasc Med 2026 · source ↗

This single-centre retrospective study followed 307 bicuspid-aortic-valve patients without prior pacemaker who underwent TAVR (2014–2025) to characterise recovery from intra-procedural conduction injury. Temporary pacing was required in 60 patients, of whom 28 (46.7%) recovered without a permanent pacemaker; peripheral vascular disease was the strongest predictor of non-recovery, and left-right coronary-fusion morphology (OR 7.18) and self-expanding valves (OR 4.41) increased temporary-pacing risk. Nearly half of conduction injuries in bicuspid anatomy resolve, supporting a period of observation before committing to permanent pacing.

VISION-FFR — Vessel FFR vs OCT in Chronic Coronary Syndromes
Baruś P et al. · Clin Res Cardiol 2026 · source ↗

This single-centre prospective study enrolled 106 patients with 120 intermediate lesions (40–80% stenosis) undergoing FFR, angiography-derived vessel FFR (vFFR), and OCT. Against wire FFR ≤0.8, vFFR ≤0.8 had 88.5% sensitivity and 92.5% specificity (AUC 0.905), and vFFR-positive lesions had smaller OCT minimal lumen area (1.83 vs 2.53 mm²) and greater length. vFFR tracked both physiologic significance and OCT-defined lumen anatomy, supporting angiography-derived physiology in chronic coronary syndromes.

— — CTO PCI in Cancer Patients: National Trends
Nubla A et al. · Catheter Cardiovasc Interv 2026 · source ↗

Using the National Inpatient Sample (2016–2022), this study examined 214,365 weighted chronic-total-occlusion PCI admissions, of which 4,135 (1.9%) involved active cancer, a proportion that rose over time (OR/year 1.065). Despite higher unadjusted mortality (5.2% vs 3.7%), active cancer was not independently associated with in-hospital mortality after adjustment (aOR 0.91, 95% CI 0.63–1.32), though myelosuppression flagged a >2-fold mortality risk in an exploratory subgroup. Selected cancer patients can undergo CTO PCI without a clear independent mortality penalty, with myelosuppression as a caution.

Tier 3

Worth knowing

OCEAN-TAVI — Decade of Early TAVR Outcomes in Asian Patients
Hioki H et al. · Struct Heart 2026 · source ↗

This multicentre Asian registry analysed temporal trends in 30-day outcomes among 13,245 TAVR patients at 18 centres (2013–2023). Risk-adjusted 30-day mortality stayed low and unchanged over the decade, while pacemaker implantation and paravalvular leak ≥mild declined significantly (especially with balloon-expandable valves), and periprocedural stroke did not improve. Device-related complications have fallen substantially, but stroke remains the key unmet challenge.

— — Frailty and Five-Year Mortality After TAVR
Huet J et al. · J Am Geriatr Soc 2026 · source ↗

This single-centre study of 976 patients aged ≥75 (median 84) with standardised geriatric assessment examined 5-year mortality, which was 46%. Frailty by the Rockwood Clinical Frailty Scale was the only geriatric variable independently associated with 5-year mortality (HR 1.12, 95% CI 1.01–1.26), with mortality rising from 38% in robust to 55% in severely frail patients. Frailty scoring adds long-term prognostic value and argues for combined cardiology–geriatric follow-up after TAVR.

— — M-TEER in Patients Above 85 Years
Jürgens F et al. · Catheter Cardiovasc Interv 2026 · source ↗

This retrospective series compared PASCAL M-TEER outcomes in patients ≥85 (n=71) versus <85 (n=304) years. No device-related complications occurred in either group, residual MR at 90 days was comparable, and mid-term mortality did not differ (5.6% vs 7.6%; P=0.76) despite the older group having more severe MR and higher STS scores. M-TEER appears as safe and effective in the very elderly as in younger patients.

— — Left Atrial Reservoir Strain Improvement After M-TEER
Takahari K et al. · JACC Asia 2026 · source ↗

This single-centre study measured left atrial reservoir strain (LARS) at baseline and 1 month in 128 patients with functional MR undergoing M-TEER, defining improvement as a ≥15% relative increase (seen in 33%). Early LARS improvement — predicted by absence of persistent AF, lower baseline LARS, and better renal function — independently forecast lower all-cause death and HF hospitalisation over a median 14 months. Serial LARS may serve as an early marker of favourable reverse remodelling after M-TEER.

CREDENCE — Sex Differences in Plaque Burden and Ischaemia
Ding Y et al. · Eur Heart J Cardiovasc Imaging 2026 · source ↗

This post-hoc analysis of the CREDENCE trial studied 612 symptomatic patients (184 women) who underwent CCTA and invasive FFR. Women had lower overall plaque burden yet a stronger association between plaque burden and ischaemia (total plaque OR 1.28 per 5% increase; significant sex interaction P=0.015), whereas diameter stenosis showed no sex interaction. For the same plaque burden, ischaemia is more likely in women — a reminder that stenosis severity alone under-reads risk in women.

— — Gender, Subclinical Atherosclerosis and MI Risk
Larsen AF et al. · Eur Heart J 2026 · source ↗

This Danish population cohort imaged 10,003 asymptomatic adults (57% women) with CCTA and followed them a median 6 years. Obstructive subclinical coronary atherosclerosis conferred an adjusted MI hazard ratio of 4.1 in women versus 12 in men (P for interaction 0.24), suggesting that for the same disease burden women may carry lower MI risk. The findings inform how screening-detected atherosclerosis should be weighted differently by sex.

— — Angiographic Lumen Assessment After DCB Angioplasty
Hande ŞUB et al. · Catheter Cardiovasc Interv 2026 · source ↗

This study performed serial quantitative coronary angiography in 70 patients treated with drug-coated balloons across de novo, in-stent restenosis, and CTO lesions. Binary restenosis at follow-up occurred in 25/70 (35.7%), late lumen loss was seen in 72.9% and late lumen enlargement in 27.1%, and active smoking independently predicted restenosis (OR 9.23) while female sex was protective (OR 0.07). The mixed lesion set explains the restenosis rate, with smoking emerging as a key modifiable predictor.

— — Ultrasonic Flow Ratio vs μQFR After IVUS-Guided PCI
Jiang J et al. · Front Cardiovasc Med 2026 · source ↗

This retrospective single-centre study of 127 patients with follow-up angiography after IVUS-guided PCI compared the ultrasonic flow ratio (UFR, derived from IVUS) with Murray-law-based quantitative flow ratio (μQFR, from angiography) for predicting target-vessel failure (19 events). Post-PCI UFR showed the highest discrimination and significantly outperformed μQFR, and only UFR remained independently associated with TVF in multivariable analysis. IVUS-derived anatomical-functional assessment may add to post-PCI risk stratification.

— — Complete vs Culprit-Only PCI in NSTEMI With Multivessel Disease
Elkasaby MH et al. · Cardiol Rev 2026 · source ↗

Using the National Inpatient Sample (2016–2022), this analysis compared complete versus culprit-only PCI in NSTEMI with multivessel disease, yielding 37,048 well-matched pairs (complete revascularisation in 27.5%). Complete revascularisation was associated with more in-hospital major bleeding (12.22% vs 10.52%; OR 1.18) and similar mortality, while MACE trended lower (14.61% vs 15.10%) driven by less recurrent MI. The in-hospital trade-off is more bleeding for a modest ischaemic benefit, greatest in patients under 65.

— — In-Stent vs De Novo CTO PCI
Ozturk S et al. · Kardiol Pol 2026 · source ↗

This three-centre registry analysed 2,375 CTO PCI procedures, of which 296 (12.5%) were in-stent restenotic CTOs (ISR-CTO). Technical success was comparable (92.9% ISR-CTO vs 91.7% de novo; P=0.49) with similar procedural success and complication rates, and adjustment confirmed no penalty for ISR-CTO. The differences were procedural rather than in outcome — less retrograde crossing, more IVUS/DCB use, and lower DES implantation.

— — Linking the TVT Registry to Medicare Advantage Claims
O'Brien SM et al. · Circ Cardiovasc Interv 2026 · source ↗

This study linked 364,573 STS/ACC TVT Registry TAVR procedures to combined Medicare fee-for-service and Medicare Advantage (MA) claims. Adding MA data lifted the combined linkage rate to 87% (a 54% increase in linked procedures) and improved Hispanic (2.26-fold) and Black (1.91-fold) representation, with 1-year mortality similar between FFS (12.6%) and MA (11.8%) cohorts. Incorporating MA claims materially strengthens the real-world surveillance infrastructure for structural devices.

Landmark Revisited

Disrupt CAD III — Intravascular Lithotripsy for Treatment of Severely Calcified Coronary Artery Disease
Hill JM et al. · J Am Coll Cardiol 2020 · primary source ↗ · NCT03595176
Structured abstract
Background
Coronary calcification hinders stent delivery and expansion and is associated with adverse outcomes; intravascular lithotripsy (IVL) delivers acoustic pressure waves to modify calcium, enhancing vessel compliance and optimising stent deployment.
Methods
Prospective, single-arm, multicentre study (47 sites, 4 countries) designed for regulatory approval, enrolling 431 patients with severely calcified de novo lesions. The primary safety endpoint was 30-day freedom from MACE (cardiac death, MI, or target-vessel revascularisation) versus a pre-specified performance goal; the primary effectiveness endpoint was procedural success versus a performance goal; an OCT substudy assessed the mechanism of calcium modification.
Key results
30-day freedom from MACE was 92.2% (lower bound of 95% CI 89.9%, exceeding the 84.4% performance goal; P<0.0001) and procedural success was 92.4% (lower bound 90.2%, exceeding the 83.4% goal; P<0.0001). Mean calcified-segment length was 47.9 ± 18.8 mm, calcium angle 292.5 ± 76.5°, and thickness 0.96 ± 0.25 mm; OCT demonstrated multiplane and longitudinal calcium fractures in 67.4% of lesions, and minimum stent area (6.5 ± 2.1 mm²) was similar regardless of demonstrable fractures.
Conclusion
Coronary IVL safely and effectively facilitated stent implantation in severely calcified lesions.
The question & the verdict

Before IVL, severely calcified lesions depended on rotational or orbital atherectomy and specialty balloons — effective but operator-dependent, with real dissection and perforation risk. Disrupt CAD III was the pivotal single-arm study asking whether acoustic pressure-wave lithotripsy could prepare such lesions safely and effectively against pre-specified performance goals. The verdict was unambiguous: both goals were comfortably exceeded, delivering the evidence for regulatory approval and a new, low-trauma calcium-modification tool.

Statistical appraisal

The single-arm, performance-goal design is the study's central limitation: outcomes were benchmarked against pre-specified thresholds rather than a randomised comparator such as rotational atherectomy, so no causal head-to-head claim is possible. The endpoints are procedural and short-term surrogates (30-day MACE and procedural success), not long-term hard outcomes, and the enriched, experienced-operator population limits generalisability to eccentric or nodular calcium and to bailout scenarios. The OCT mechanism substudy is a genuine strength, giving biological plausibility (calcium fracture) to the clinical signal.

Subsequent substudies & follow-ups

· Patient-level pooled analysis of the Disrupt CAD studies (Kereiakes DJ et al., JACC Cardiovasc Interv 2021): across 628 patients at 72 sites, the primary safety and effectiveness endpoints were met in 92.7% and 92.4%, with 30-day target-lesion failure 7.2%, cardiac death 0.5%, and stent thrombosis 0.8%, and no IVL-associated perforation, abrupt closure, or no-reflow. source ↗

· IVUS mechanism substudy (Cardiovasc Revasc Med 2023): in 18 patients with serial imaging, minimum lumen area rose from 2.75 ± 0.84 mm² pre-IVL to 4.06 ± 1.41 mm² post-IVL and 6.84 ± 2.18 mm² post-stenting, confirming improved vessel compliance as the mechanism. source ↗

· Two-year outcomes of Disrupt CAD III have since been reported (Kereiakes DJ et al., JACC Cardiovasc Interv 2023), addressing durability. source ↗

Why it still matters: IVL is now a first-line calcium-modification option embedded in contemporary algorithms — echoed in this week's EuroIntervention "crack or ablate" debate and calcified-nodule imaging reviews — but the enduring lesson is that intravascular imaging, not reflex device choice, should decide when to reach for lithotripsy versus atherectomy.
