Practice-changing

CHIP-BCIS3 — Impact of Left Ventricular Unloading on Hemodynamics and Stunning in Complex PCI
Ezad SM et al. · J Am Coll Cardiol 2026 · primary source ↗
Structured abstract
Background
Elective LV unloading during high-risk PCI is hypothesized to blunt hemodynamic instability and myocardial stunning, but had not been tested in a randomized trial.
Methods
Prespecified substudy of the CHIP-BCIS3 randomized trial. Patients with severe LV systolic dysfunction undergoing complex PCI were randomized to elective unloading with a microaxial flow pump (mAFP) or standard care, with invasive hemodynamics by pulmonary-artery catheter at baseline and post-PCI. Coprimary outcomes were systolic blood-pressure reduction, loss of pulse pressure (LOPP), and inotrope requirement.
Key results
Of 125 eligible patients, 97 were enrolled (50 mAFP; 47 standard care); baseline cardiac index was 2.10 ± 0.55 L/min/m². Systolic BP reduction was similar (RR 0.86; 95% CI 0.58–1.29), while inotrope use was lower with mAFP (RR 0.57; 95% CI 0.34–0.97). LOPP was markedly more frequent with mAFP (RR 7.83; 95% CI 2.53–24.24) and was associated with peri-procedural myocardial injury (OR 3.00; 95% CI 1.00–9.03). Changes in cardiac index (−0.25 vs −0.24 L/min/m²; P = 0.20) and pulmonary capillary wedge pressure (2 vs 3 mm Hg; P = 0.79) did not differ.
Conclusion
Elective mAFP unloading reduced inotrope requirements but increased loss of pulse pressure and did not prevent myocardial stunning.
Critical appraisal
Study design
A prespecified randomized substudy with invasive, catheter-based hemodynamic endpoints — the strongest available design for a mechanistic question that registries cannot settle, though the mechanistic endpoints are surrogates rather than clinical outcomes.
Clinical relevance
Speaks directly to the CHIP-lab decision of whether to place prophylactic support before a complex, low-EF case: the mixed signal (less inotrope but more pulse-pressure loss and unchanged stunning) argues against reflexive elective unloading purely to protect the myocardium.
Context
Fits within a hemodynamic-support literature that has repeatedly shown better device haemodynamics without a clean clinical payoff in non-shock high-risk PCI; the finding that loss of pulse pressure tracked with myocardial injury is hypothesis-generating for how continuous-flow support may itself perturb coronary perfusion.
Limitations
Small (97 patients), enrolment stopped short of the 125 eligible, open-label with surrogate coprimary endpoints, and a single microaxial platform — none of which supports conclusions about hard clinical outcomes.
Bottom line: In complex PCI with severe LV dysfunction, elective microaxial unloading spared inotropes but did not abolish stunning and introduced frequent loss of pulse pressure — reserve it for a haemodynamic indication, not routine myocardial protection.
ILUMIEN IV — OCT-Defined Plaque Morphology and Clinical Outcomes After PCI
Lopez JJ et al. · JACC Cardiovasc Imaging 2026 · primary source ↗
Structured abstract
Background
OCT can classify predominant plaque morphology (fibrous, lipidic, calcified), but the prognostic impact of predominant plaque type after PCI was unclear.
Methods
Substudy of the OCT-guided cohort of the ILUMIEN IV trial (n = 1,233 assigned to OCT guidance). Core-lab analysis classified each lesion as predominantly lipidic (arc ≥180° or thin-cap fibroatheroma), calcific (arc ≥180°), or fibrotic. The primary endpoint was target-vessel failure (TVF: cardiac death, target-vessel MI, or ischemia-driven target-vessel revascularization).
Key results
Single de novo lesion PCI was performed in 926 patients; plaque was predominantly lipidic in 309 (33%), calcific in 252 (27%), and fibrotic in 365 (39%). After adjustment, PCI of a predominantly calcific lesion carried higher TVF than a fibrotic lesion (10.7% vs 4.0%; adjusted HR 2.20; 95% CI 1.12–4.33; P = 0.02), driven by more ischemia-driven target-vessel revascularization (8.2% vs 3.1%; adjusted HR 2.31; 95% CI 1.08–4.96; P = 0.03). Lipidic lesions were intermediate (TVF 5.6%; ID-TVR 4.0%).
Conclusion
Baseline OCT plaque morphology was prognostic after PCI: calcific lesions had the highest and fibrotic the lowest 2-year TVF — the first large-trial demonstration of this relationship.
Critical appraisal
Study design
Core-lab–adjudicated substudy nested in a randomized imaging trial — robust image analysis and adjudicated events, but the plaque-morphology comparison is observational and non-randomized.
Clinical relevance
Reframes pre-PCI OCT as a prognostic, not just procedural, tool: a predominantly calcific lesion flags a patient who may warrant more aggressive lesion preparation and closer surveillance.
Context
Reinforces a recurrent theme this week — calcium as the dominant residual risk after modern PCI — echoing the IVUS calcium-angle and lithotripsy-prediction work elsewhere in this issue.
Limitations
Restricted to single de novo lesions in the OCT arm, modest absolute event counts, and residual confounding by lesion complexity that adjustment cannot fully remove.
Bottom line: Predominant calcific plaque on baseline OCT more than doubled 2-year target-vessel failure — read plaque type as a prognostic signal, and prepare calcified lesions accordingly.
— — Computed Tomography Versus Angiography for Coronary Stenoses Before TAVI
Garzon S et al. · Am J Cardiol 2026 · primary source ↗
Structured abstract
Background
Coronary artery disease is common before TAVI, but the optimal pre-procedural coronary assessment strategy — CT angiography first versus invasive angiography first — is uncertain.
Methods
Single-center comparison of 307 consecutive TAVI patients managed with an invasive-angiography-first (ICA-first, n = 150) or CT-angiography-first (CTA-first, n = 157) strategy. The primary outcome was all-cause mortality.
Key results
Over a median 61.7 months, mortality did not differ between strategies (unadjusted HR 0.88; 95% CI 0.66–1.16), and assessment strategy was not an independent predictor of death after adjustment (HR 0.95; 95% CI 0.67–1.34). A CTA-first strategy avoided invasive angiography in 73% of patients, with a non-significant trend toward more late post-TAVI catheterization (HR 2.47; 95% CI 0.94–6.45). Pre-TAVI PCI was not associated with lower mortality, though CAD itself marked a higher-risk phenotype.
Conclusion
A CTA-first strategy yielded equivalent long-term survival while sparing most patients an invasive study, supporting it as the default pre-TAVI coronary assessment.
Critical appraisal
Study design
Single-center observational cohort with long follow-up and a hard primary endpoint — pragmatic and clinically framed, but non-randomized and vulnerable to allocation and era effects.
Clinical relevance
The pre-TAVI work-up already includes gated CT for annular sizing; leveraging it to triage coronary disease avoids an extra invasive procedure in a frail, largely elderly population.
Context
Consistent with the broader move toward CT-first coronary gatekeeping before structural intervention; the neutral signal on pre-TAVI PCI adds to ongoing equipoise about revascularising bystander CAD before valve work.
Limitations
Single center, modest sample, no core-lab CT adjudication, and a trend toward more late catheterization in the CTA arm that a larger study might render significant.
Bottom line: Defaulting to a CT-first coronary assessment before TAVI spared roughly three of four patients an invasive angiogram without any survival penalty over five years.
— — Tirzepatide and the Risk of Atherosclerotic Cardiovascular Events: Population-Based Cohort Study
Krüger N et al. · BMJ 2026 · source ↗

Using two US claims databases with a trial-benchmarked emulation design, this cohort compared 35,353 patients with type 2 diabetes and established ASCVD who initiated tirzepatide against 17,618 who initiated sitagliptin (a cardiovascular-neutral placebo proxy), with propensity-overlap weighting. At one year the weighted MACE risk was 2.9% with tirzepatide versus 4.4% with sitagliptin (risk difference −1.4%; HR 0.68, 95% CI 0.58–0.80; NNT 70), with lower MI (HR 0.67) but no meaningful difference in ischaemic stroke. Reductions also spanned all-cause mortality (HR 0.55) and infection-related outcomes, hinting at mechanisms beyond atherosclerosis — though this is observational data, not a randomized cardiovascular outcomes trial.

— — IVUS-Guided Versus Angiography-Guided PCI for Complex Coronary Lesions: Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis
Ye Z et al. · Cardiovasc Revasc Med 2026 · source ↗

This updated meta-analysis pooled 12 randomized trials (7,089 patients, weighted mean follow-up 18.0 months) comparing IVUS-guided versus angiography-guided PCI in complex coronary anatomy, with cardiac death as the primary outcome. IVUS did not reduce cardiac death (RR 1.02, 95% CI 0.76–1.38), all-cause death, or MI, but it did lower definite stent thrombosis (RR 0.31, 95% CI 0.12–0.77) and repeat revascularization (any RR 0.67; TLR RR 0.73; TVR RR 0.70). These revascularization benefits were attenuated to non-significance after trim-and-fill correction for publication bias, supporting selective — not universal — IVUS use in complex lesions.

— — National Impact of Optimal Implementation of Quadruple GDMT in HFrEF
Keykhaei M et al. · JACC Heart Fail 2026 · source ↗

This population-level decision-analytic model estimated the eligible-but-untreated HFrEF population and the deaths and hospitalizations preventable with full quadruple GDMT (ARNI, beta-blocker, MRA, SGLT2 inhibitor) using contemporary US epidemiologic data. An estimated 2.76 million US adults with chronic symptomatic HFrEF were eligible, yet only 18.2% received all four classes; optimal implementation was projected to prevent 113,747 deaths (95% UI 90,173–149,875) over 12 months and 357,332 HF hospitalizations annually. The modelling underscores an enormous, modifiable implementation gap directly relevant to post-MI and post-PCI patients with reduced ejection fraction.

Notable

NTCVR — Ascending Aorta and Pure Aortic Regurgitation TAVR
Hong N et al. · Heart 2026 · source ↗

This national registry analysed 890 patients with pure native aortic regurgitation treated with self-expandable transfemoral TAVR at 72 Chinese centres (2017–2023), with 30-day all-cause mortality as the primary endpoint. In-hospital and 30-day mortality were 2.0% and 2.7%; ascending-aorta diameter (OR 1.103 per mm) and renal insufficiency (OR 4.602) independently predicted 30-day death, and patients with an ascending aorta >41.6 mm had markedly higher mortality (OR 5.027, 95% CI 2.042–12.696). Ascending-aorta size emerges as a key gatekeeper when considering off-label TAVR for pure regurgitation.

AGENT IDE — Predictors of Target Lesion Failure in In-Stent Restenosis
Abbott JD et al. · Circ Cardiovasc Interv 2026 · source ↗

This post hoc analysis of the AGENT IDE trial examined 406 patients with in-stent restenosis randomized to a paclitaxel-coated balloon, using Cox modelling to find predictors of 2-year target lesion failure. TLF occurred in 27.0%; independent risks were multiple stent layers (HR 1.80), White race (HR 1.71), and greater post-preparation residual stenosis (HR 1.16 per 10%), while LAD location was protective (HR 0.56). The data spotlight residual stenosis after lesion preparation as a modifiable lever before drug-coated balloon therapy for ISR.

SCA-DEB — Scoring-Balloon plus Sirolimus-Eluting Balloon in Diffuse Small-Vessel Disease
Vassilev D et al. · Catheter Cardiovasc Interv 2026 · source ↗

This prospective single-arm study treated 167 patients (194 vessels) with diffuse (lesion length ≥20 mm), small-vessel (1.5–2.75 mm) coronary disease using systematic scoring-balloon angioplasty followed by sirolimus-eluting balloon, with mean lesion length 35 ± 15 mm. Over a median 15 months, MACE was 5.4%, target-lesion revascularization 4.8%, and patient-oriented composite events 10.2%. A fully "leave-nothing-behind" scoring-plus-drug-balloon strategy delivered promising results in an anatomy where drug-eluting stents perform poorly, though a single-arm design limits inference.

Gulf CTO — Outcomes Across Management Strategies for Chronic Total Occlusion
Daoulah A et al. · JACC Adv 2026 · source ↗

This 7-centre registry of 741 angiographically confirmed CTO patients compared an initial strategy of medical therapy (n = 151), PCI (n = 441), or CABG (n = 149) with adjustment for baseline risk. After adjustment, neither PCI nor CABG differed from medical therapy for in-hospital major adverse events or 36-month event-free survival, but angina control at last follow-up favoured revascularization (CCS class I in 86.2% medical, 91.7% PCI, 98.0% CABG; significant only for CABG). In a non-randomized cohort with strong allocation bias, revascularization — particularly CABG — bought symptom relief rather than a demonstrable hard-outcome benefit.

— — Incomplete Device Apposition and Stroke Recurrence After PFO Closure
Yan C et al. · Heart 2026 · source ↗

This prospective four-centre cohort used 6-month cardiac CT angiography to assess incomplete device apposition (IDA) in 233 patients after PFO closure for secondary stroke prevention. IDA was present in 55.8% (predominantly at the anterior-inferior rim), associated with larger/longer PFOs and larger devices, and during a median 20 months carried higher risks of recurrent stroke (log-rank P = 0.022) and new-onset symptoms (P < 0.001). CTA-detected malapposition may explain a subset of post-closure recurrences and warrants prospective study of preventive device strategies.

— — Left Atrial Appendage Closure After Prior Mitral Valve Interventions
Velazquez G et al. · Clin Res Cardiol 2026 · source ↗

This multicentre study of 1,213 LAAC patients compared 142 with prior mitral valve interventions (PMVI) against those without, with a 2-year composite of death and ischaemic events. Procedural success was very high in both groups (99.3% vs 99.4%) and device-related thrombus similar (7.0% vs 6.8%); the 2-year composite did not differ (21.3% vs 21.8%), though PMVI independently predicted higher all-cause mortality (adjusted OR 1.76) while no ischaemic strokes occurred in the PMVI group. LAAC is feasible and safe after mitral intervention, with excess mortality reflecting baseline comorbidity rather than thromboembolic failure.

— — Early Haptoglobin Decline During Impella CP Versus IABP in Cardiogenic Shock
Kawakami S et al. · J Am Heart Assoc 2026 · source ↗

This single-centre registry of 91 cardiogenic-shock patients (IABP 67; Impella CP 24) tracked serial serum haptoglobin every 12 hours to characterise subclinical haemolysis. Low haptoglobin (<19 mg/dL) occurred more often with Impella than IABP (67% vs 31%, P = 0.004), and Impella use independently predicted low haptoglobin (OR 2.82), though overt clinical haemolysis was uncommon in both. Serial haptoglobin may offer an early, inexpensive readout of device-related haemolysis to guide microaxial-pump management — a theme echoed by this issue's flow-pump AKI data.

— — Pulmonary Artery Catheter Use in Heart Failure-Related Cardiogenic Shock
Alahmad A et al. · Cardiovasc Revasc Med 2026 · source ↗

Using the National Inpatient Sample (2016–2022), this analysis of 17,480 weighted HF-related cardiogenic shock hospitalizations (excluding ACS) examined pulmonary-artery catheter (PAC) use and in-hospital mortality. PAC utilization more than doubled from 9.9% to 21.3%, and after adjustment PAC was associated with lower in-hospital mortality (aOR 0.67) — persisting across four sensitivity analyses including exclusion of LVAD/transplant patients (aOR 0.74) — at the cost of longer, costlier stays. The consistent survival signal strengthens the case for prospective testing of PAC-guided management in HF-CS.

COMBINE-AF — Extracranial Bleeding on Oral Anticoagulants in Atrial Fibrillation
Siegal DM et al. · Circulation 2026 · source ↗

This pooled analysis of 73,737 anticoagulated AF patients from five pivotal trials characterised extracranial clinically relevant bleeding by ISTH severity over a mean 705 days. Clinically relevant extracranial bleeding occurred in 10,634 patients (cumulative incidence 26%; 7.6 per 100 person-years), including a 7% cumulative incidence of major bleeds, with gastrointestinal sites comprising 49% of major bleeds. Baseline covariates explained only about two-thirds of the population-attributable risk, indicating substantial unmeasured drivers of bleeding — directly relevant to peri-procedural antithrombotic decisions.

— — AI-ECG for Long-Term Mortality Stratification After TAVR
Shi W et al. · JACC Asia 2026 · source ↗

This two-centre study trained and externally validated an AI model on 711 preoperative ECG images to predict 3-year all-cause death after TAVR. The model discriminated well internally and externally (AUC 0.767 vs 0.712), and AI-flagged high-risk patients (15.5%) had a 61.5% 3-year mortality versus 16.5% in low-risk patients (adjusted HR 3.49). A routine pre-procedural ECG, read by AI, could sharpen individualized follow-up intensity after TAVR — pending prospective validation.

SBLP — Small-Balloon Low-Pressure Pullback for Coronary Intramural Hematoma
Chen C et al. · Catheter Cardiovasc Interv 2026 · source ↗

This retrospective cohort compared three strategies for PCI-related coronary intramural hematoma in 181 patients: additional stenting ± cutting balloon (n = 109), conservative management (n = 20), and small-balloon low-pressure pullback (SBLP, n = 52). Over 15.1 months, MACE was lowest with SBLP (7.7%) versus stenting (29.4%) and conservative care (50%), with adjusted HR 0.060 for SBLP versus conservative management, plus shorter stays and fewer stents. SBLP is a promising stent-sparing bailout for intramural hematoma, though the retrospective, single-centre design invites confirmation.

Tier 3

Worth knowing

— — IVUS Distance-Averaged Calcium Angles Predict Stent Underexpansion
Wang JJ et al. · Catheter Cardiovasc Interv 2026 · source ↗

This single-centre analysis of 292 IVUS-guided DES cases tested whether averaging calcium angles measured every 1 mm within 5 mm of the maximum-calcium site predicts stent underexpansion (percent expansion <70%). Distance-averaged angles improved discrimination over the maximum calcium angle, with AUC 0.75–0.77, and the 2 mm-averaged angle was as accurate as wider windows. A simple refinement to IVUS calcium assessment may better flag lesions needing dedicated calcium modification before stenting.

— — Deep Learning to Predict Intravascular Lithotripsy Responsiveness
Liu Y et al. · JACC Adv 2026 · source ↗

This study applied deep learning to pre-IVL and post-stent OCT in 134 patients to predict post-stenting luminal expansion after intravascular lithotripsy for calcified lesions. Incorporating complex morphological features raised predictive performance well above conventional geometry (adjusted R² 0.72 vs 0.58 at lesion level), with lumen/calcium circularity, wall eccentricity, and calcium-to-lumen balance as key predictors. AI-derived plaque morphology could help select which calcified lesions will actually respond to lithotripsy, though prospective validation is needed.

CROWN — OCT-Guided Orbital Atherectomy for Calcified Lesions (Design)
Ntantou E et al. · Neth Heart J 2026 · source ↗

CROWN is a prospective, multicentre, single-arm observational study enrolling 100 patients with de novo severely calcified coronary lesions undergoing OCT-guided orbital atherectomy before stenting across sites in the Netherlands, Germany, and Italy. Peri-procedural OCT is acquired before and after atherectomy and after stenting, with the primary endpoint the proportion achieving an OCT-derived minimum stent area ≥5.5 mm². The study aims to clarify how orbital atherectomy modifies calcium and to refine device selection for the calcified lesions that increasingly dominate residual PCI risk.

— — CTO PCI in Patients Ineligible for Surgical Revascularization
Park J et al. · Int J Cardiol 2026 · source ↗

This single-centre cohort compared 75 surgically ineligible patients (6.6%) against all other patients among 1,135 undergoing CTO PCI, with all-cause mortality at 30 days and 1 year and overlap weighting for baseline differences. Despite greater comorbidity, lower ejection fraction, and more extensive disease, surgically ineligible patients had similar technical success (94.7% vs 91.1%) and no adjusted excess mortality at 30 days or 1 year. CTO PCI appears a reasonable option for carefully selected patients turned down for surgery, with the caveat of few mortality events.

— — Long-Term Renal Function After CTO PCI and Contrast Exposure
Testerini FBB et al. · Am J Cardiol 2026 · source ↗

This retrospective cohort of 275 CTO-PCI patients (median contrast 215 mL) examined whether procedural contrast volume drives long-term renal deterioration over a median 3.3 years. Persistent kidney dysfunction (≥25% eGFR decline) occurred in 52.4%, but contrast volume was not independently associated with it (P = 0.267) and showed no dose-response across quartiles; patient factors (age, male sex, hypertension, diabetes) predicted decline. The data challenge contrast volume as the main culprit for late renal decline after CTO PCI.

— — Microaxial Flow Pump Level, Haemolysis, and Kidney Injury in Shock
Nakashima T et al. · Eur Heart J Acute Cardiovasc Care 2026 · source ↗

This retrospective study of 160 cardiogenic-shock patients on a microaxial flow pump (49 pump-alone; 111 with VA-ECMO) linked pump level, cumulative plasma-free haemoglobin exposure, and acute kidney injury. In the pump-alone group, higher pump levels tracked with higher plasma-free haemoglobin and AKI, and a level ≥P8 sustained ≥24 h independently predicted AKI (adjusted RR 3.31, 95% CI 1.67–6.58). Running a microaxial pump at high levels for prolonged periods carries a measurable haemolysis-mediated renal cost — a practical titration signal for the shock team.

— — Transcatheter Tricuspid Valve Replacement After Failed T-TEER
Voß F et al. · Catheter Cardiovasc Interv 2026 · source ↗

This prospective single-centre series analysed 21 TTVR patients, including 5 with residual/recurrent severe-to-torrential tricuspid regurgitation after prior tricuspid edge-to-edge repair, describing a "valve-after-clip" algorithm. Procedural success was 100% in both the post-T-TEER and primary-TTVR groups with no periprocedural complications after T-TEER, though wire-based intentional clip excision was needed in 60% and procedure times were longer. The valve-after-clip concept is feasible and offers a bail-out for the ~12% of T-TEER patients with recurrent TR.

— — Baseline Invasive Hemodynamics and Response After ASD Closure
Hashimoto G et al. · Catheter Cardiovasc Interv 2026 · source ↗

This retrospective study of 204 adults undergoing percutaneous secundum ASD closure tested whether pre-closure invasive haemodynamics predict a suboptimal clinical response (persistent dyspnoea and/or new diuretic need). Suboptimal response occurred in 14.2%; age (OR 1.05/year) and mean pulmonary artery pressure (OR 1.10/mmHg) were independently associated, with mPAP showing moderate discrimination (AUC 0.794). Baseline pulmonary-pressure burden may help counsel patients about the likelihood of symptomatic benefit before ASD closure.

— — Pacemaker Need After Balloon-Expandable TAVR in Pre-Existing Bundle Branch Block
Rajendran J et al. · Struct Heart 2026 · source ↗

This retrospective study of 380 patients with pre-existing bundle branch block undergoing balloon-expandable TAVR compared 30-day permanent pacemaker rates between right (n = 269) and left (n = 111) bundle branch block. Pacemaker implantation was more frequent with RBBB than LBBB (18.59% vs 7.21%), and baseline RBBB (OR 3.56) and prior AF/flutter (OR 4.13) independently predicted 30-day pacing, while deeper right-sided membranous-septum implantation depth was protective. Pre-existing RBBB flags a patient for heightened conduction vigilance and higher valve implantation.

— — Coronary Microvascular Dysfunction and Sublingual Microcirculation in INOCA
Smilowitz NR et al. · JACC Adv 2026 · source ↗

This study examined whether noninvasive sublingual sidestream darkfield microscopy detects systemic microvascular abnormalities in 135 INOCA patients undergoing invasive coronary function testing. Coronary microvascular dysfunction was present in 46.7%, but sublingual perfused-boundary region, red-cell filling, and microvascular density did not differ by CMD status nor correlate with coronary flow reserve or index of microcirculatory resistance. The negative result argues that coronary microvascular dysfunction in INOCA is not simply a readout of a generalised systemic microcirculopathy.

— — Operator-Patient Sex and Outcomes After PCI
Ayayo SA et al. · Catheter Cardiovasc Interv 2026 · source ↗

This analysis of 138,112 ACS PCI procedures in England and Wales (2017–2020) evaluated in-hospital MACCE and mortality by patient sex and operator-patient sex concordance. Female patients had 10% higher MACCE and 14% higher mortality overall, differences that were statistically significant among the male operators performing 94.6% of procedures but not among female operators; operator-patient sex discordance was associated with less radial access, less prasugrel use, and higher MACCE (RR 1.12). The persistent female-patient disadvantage warrants scrutiny, though limited female-operator numbers demand cautious interpretation.

Landmark Revisited

RENOVATE-COMPLEX-PCI — Intravascular Imaging-Guided or Angiography-Guided Complex PCI
Lee JM et al. · N Engl J Med 2023 · primary source ↗
Structured abstract

Background: Data on clinical outcomes after intravascular imaging-guided PCI for complex coronary lesions, versus angiography-guided PCI, were limited.

Methods: Prospective, multicentre, open-label trial in South Korea. Patients with complex coronary-artery lesions were randomized 2:1 to intravascular imaging-guided PCI (IVUS or OCT at operator discretion) or angiography-guided PCI. The primary endpoint was a composite of death from cardiac causes, target-vessel-related MI, or clinically driven target-vessel revascularization.

Key results: Of 1,639 patients randomized, 1,092 were assigned to imaging-guided and 547 to angiography-guided PCI. At a median 2.1 years, a primary-endpoint event occurred in 76 patients (cumulative incidence 7.7%) with imaging guidance versus 60 (12.3%) with angiography (HR 0.64; 95% CI 0.45–0.89; P = 0.008). Cardiac death occurred in 1.7% vs 3.8%, target-vessel MI in 3.7% vs 5.6%, and clinically driven target-vessel revascularization in 3.4% vs 5.5%. Procedure-related safety events did not differ.

Conclusion: Among patients with complex coronary lesions, intravascular imaging-guided PCI reduced the composite of cardiac death, target-vessel MI, or target-vessel revascularization compared with angiography guidance.

The question & the verdict

By the early 2020s, IVUS and OCT were widely available yet inconsistently used, and older imaging trials were criticised for surrogate endpoints, modest size, or enrolment of simple lesions. RENOVATE-COMPLEX-PCI asked the practical question that mattered: in genuinely complex anatomy — long lesions, bifurcations, chronic total occlusions, heavy calcification, left main, in-stent restenosis — does routinely guiding PCI with intravascular imaging change hard clinical outcomes? The verdict was affirmative and clinically meaningful: a 36% relative reduction in target-vessel failure, driven across all three components of the composite, establishing imaging guidance as the preferred default for complex PCI and underpinning its elevation in subsequent revascularization guidance.

Statistical appraisal

The 2:1 randomization maximised experience in the imaging arm while preserving a randomized control, and the composite of cardiac death, target-vessel MI, and clinically driven revascularization is appropriately vessel-specific — tying events to the treated lesion rather than diluting the estimate with unrelated events. The absolute risk difference (7.7% vs 12.3%; ~4.6 percentage points; number-needed-to-treat ~22 over 2.1 years) is clinically substantial, and the effect was directionally consistent across all components, strengthening causal confidence. Key caveats temper enthusiasm: the trial was open-label, so the decision to perform clinically driven revascularization — a soft, operator-dependent endpoint — could be influenced by knowledge of imaging findings; the choice between IVUS and OCT was left to operators, so the trial validates a strategy rather than a single modality; and the South Korean, high-volume single-health-system setting may limit generalisability to labs with less imaging expertise. Enrolment (1,639) was powered for the composite, not for cardiac death alone, so component-level differences are supportive but not definitive.

Subsequent substudies & follow-ups

Kwon W et al., JACC Cardiovasc Interv 2024 (PMID 38267144): comparing imaging-guided PCI before versus after standardized optimization protocols, target-vessel failure was lower with contemporary (present) imaging practice than with historical imaging (5.1% vs 8.5%; HR 0.63; 95% CI 0.42–0.94), showing that structured optimization criteria — not merely acquiring images — drive the benefit.

Hong D et al., Rev Esp Cardiol 2025 (PMID 39643207): in a post hoc analysis stratified by atherothrombotic risk (TRS-2P), imaging guidance reduced target-vessel failure similarly in low-risk (5.6% vs 10.4%; HR 0.56) and high-risk (14.1% vs 18.5%; HR 0.71) patients, with no significant interaction (P = 0.496) — the relative benefit held across the risk spectrum even as absolute risk rose.

Why it still matters: RENOVATE-COMPLEX-PCI is the modern anchor for imaging-guided complex PCI — the reference every new imaging dataset in this issue implicitly extends, from ILUMIEN IV's plaque-morphology prognostics to the IVUS-vs-angiography meta-analysis debating where the benefit is real. Its lasting teaching point is that intravascular imaging earns its outcome benefit only when the images are acted on through disciplined optimization, not when they are merely acquired.
