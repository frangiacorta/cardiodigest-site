Congress Highlights — GISE 2026

The 47th GISE National Congress (Milan, Centro Congressi MiCo; four days closing Friday 16 October 2026) ran a Late-Breaking Clinical Science session of eight studies across five journals. Six are already peer-reviewed and appear in the tiers above/below — M-TEER with and without on-site surgery (Marchisio, Tier 2), anomalous coronary origins by continuous thermodilution (Agnifili/Lo Rito, Tier 3), renal denervation (Vetrano, Quick hits), prolonged-balloon-inflation TAVI (Nai Fovino/Cardaioli, Tier 2), PROPHET-FFR post-PCI physiology (Leone/Galante, Tier 2) and MATRIX occlusion-MI (Zito/Milzi, Tier 2). The two below have no peer-reviewed publication yet, so they sit here, exempt from the PubMed gate and verified only against the GISE congress recap.

BIOVASC substudy — non-culprit lesion complexity and the timing of complete revascularisation
G. Scherillo · GISE 2026 late-breaker (Circulation: Cardiovascular Interventions session) · source ↗

[GISE 2026 — late-breaker, pre-publication] This substudy of BIOVASC — the randomised trial comparing immediate versus staged complete revascularisation in patients with acute coronary syndromes and multivessel disease — stratified non-culprit disease by lesion complexity. As reported by the congress, greater non-culprit lesion complexity was associated with a worse prognosis but did not modify the effect of an immediate versus a staged (deferred) revascularisation strategy (the recap gives no effect sizes, so none are quoted here). These are congress-reported findings from the Late-Breaking Clinical Science session and are not yet peer-reviewed.

— — Emergency TAVI on veno-arterial ECMO: left-ventricular unloading to restore spontaneous circulation
T. Simone · GISE 2026 late-breaker (The American Journal of Cardiology session) · source ↗

[GISE 2026 — late-breaker, pre-publication] This single case described an emergency transcatheter aortic valve implantation performed on veno-arterial ECMO support. As reported by the congress, spontaneous circulation returned only after the left ventricle was unloaded with a microaxial pump, leading the presenter to suggest planning left-ventricular venting in advance. As a single congress-reported case that is not yet peer-reviewed, it is hypothesis-generating only.

Tier 1

Practice-changing

— — Immediate versus staged complete revascularisation in multivessel disease and STEMI: a meta-analysis of randomised trials
O'Callaghan D et al. · JACC Cardiovasc Interv 2026 · primary source ↗
Structured abstract
Background
Complete revascularisation is recommended for STEMI with multivessel disease, but whether non-culprit lesions should be treated immediately during the index procedure or staged at a later time is unsettled.
Methods
A frequentist, pairwise meta-analysis pooled multicentre randomised trials that compared immediate versus staged non-culprit revascularisation in STEMI, used only second-generation (or newer) drug-eluting stents and had ≥1-year follow-up; the primary outcome was all-cause mortality, with random-effects, inverse-variance weighting of trial-level hazard ratios (PROSPERO CRD420251124207).
Key results
Four randomised trials comprising 2,651 patients were included; 1-year all-cause death was higher with immediate complete revascularisation (HR 1.50; 95% CI 1.02–2.21; P = 0.04; prediction interval 0.80–2.81; I² = 0%). Immediate revascularisation reduced recurrent MI when protocol definitions including procedural MI were used (HR 0.58; 95% CI 0.36–0.94) but not spontaneous MI (HR 0.91; 95% CI 0.55–1.48), while cardiac death and unplanned revascularisation were comparable between strategies.
Conclusion
In STEMI with multivessel disease, immediate complete revascularisation may be associated with higher 1-year all-cause death than a staged approach; the data do not support a routine immediate strategy.
Critical appraisal
Study design
Trial-level random-effects meta-analysis restricted to contemporary-stent randomised trials with harmonised follow-up — a rigorous synthesis, but one dependent on only four trials and aggregate (not patient-level) data.
Clinical relevance
Timing of non-culprit PCI is a daily decision; a mortality signal favouring staging, if real, would reshape index-admission workflow and catheter-laboratory scheduling.
Context
Complete revascularisation itself is guideline-endorsed (COMPLETE, DANAMI-3-PRIMULTI — revisited below); this analysis addresses only the sequencing question and inverts earlier assumptions that doing everything at once is simply more efficient.
Limitations
The mortality finding rests on a small absolute number of deaths across four trials, is statistically fragile by the authors' own account, and the MI benefit depends on whether procedural MI is counted.
Bottom line: Absent a compelling reason to finish in one sitting, default to staging non-culprit PCI in STEMI — the signal of harm with an immediate strategy is worth respecting even while its fragility is acknowledged.
SPIRARE II — Vertex Pulmonary Embolectomy System in acute intermediate-risk pulmonary embolism
Sayfo S et al. · EuroIntervention 2026 (online first) · primary source ↗
Structured abstract
Background
Large-bore aspiration thrombectomy can rapidly unload the right ventricle in acute pulmonary embolism (PE), but catheter stiffness and instability may limit safe access; the Vertex system uses a Transforming Fixation (TFX) mechanism that separates navigational flexibility from the fixed support needed for aspiration.
Methods
SPIRARE II was a prospective, single-arm, multicentre pivotal investigation at 19 sites in the United States and Europe; eligible patients had acute PE (≤14 days), a CT-assessed RV/LV ratio >0.9 and systolic blood pressure ≥90 mmHg, with co-primary endpoints of a 48-hour composite major adverse event (MAE) rate against a 25% performance goal and core-laboratory RV/LV change from baseline to 48 hours against a >0.20 goal.
Key results
Among 123 patients (mean age 61 ± 14 years, 49% female, 85.4% ESC intermediate-high risk), the 48-hour composite MAE rate was 2.4% (3/123; one-sided P<0.0001) and the core-laboratory RV/LV ratio fell from 1.44 ± 0.33 to 1.05 ± 0.21, a mean reduction of 0.39 ± 0.33 (P<0.0001); secondary haemodynamics — mean pulmonary artery pressure, systolic pulmonary artery pressure and total pulmonary vascular resistance — improved with preserved cardiac index.
Conclusion
The Vertex Pulmonary Embolectomy System met both prespecified safety and effectiveness endpoints, delivering stable access and consistent right-ventricular unloading in intermediate-risk PE.
Critical appraisal
Study design
Prospective, multinational, single-arm pivotal device study with blinded core-laboratory imaging, independent clinical-events adjudication, and endpoints benchmarked against published performance goals rather than a randomised comparator.
Clinical relevance
Adds another mechanical-thrombectomy option to a crowded intermediate-risk PE field and pairs the RV/LV surrogate with systematic pre/post right-heart-catheterisation haemodynamics in every patient.
Context
The MAE rate (2.4%) and RV/LV reduction (0.39) sit squarely within prior pivotal device ranges (0%–3.8% MAE; 0.38–0.44 RV/LV), and complement STORM-PE — the first randomised thrombectomy-versus-anticoagulation trial — which showed greater 48-hour RV/LV reduction with thrombectomy.
Limitations
Single-arm with no anticoagulation-alone comparator, surrogate-anchored endpoints, short follow-up, and benefit on clinical outcomes (mortality, long-term function) unproven.
Bottom line: The Vertex system is a technically effective, low-complication aspiration platform for intermediate-risk PE — but, as for the whole class, a survival or functional benefit over anticoagulation alone remains to be demonstrated, so patient selection still governs its use.
ENCIRCLE MAC — Transseptal transcatheter mitral valve replacement for mitral annular calcification
Guerrero ME et al. · J Am Coll Cardiol 2026 · primary source ↗
Structured abstract
Background
Symptomatic mitral valve dysfunction with mitral annular calcification (MAC) is among the hardest problems in structural heart disease, with advanced age, comorbidity and hostile anatomy often precluding surgery or transcatheter repair.
Methods
The ENCIRCLE MAC Registry is a prospective, multicentre, single-arm study of fully percutaneous transseptal TMVR with the SAPIEN M3 system in patients with MAC-related mitral dysfunction (≥moderate-to-severe MR, severe mitral stenosis, or combined moderate MR with at least moderate stenosis) deemed unsuitable for surgery or repair; the primary endpoint was a non-hierarchical composite of all-cause mortality and heart-failure hospitalisation (HFH) at 1 year.
Key results
Of 364 screened patients, TMVR was attempted in 100 and a valve implanted in 93 (mean age 76.1 ± 9.2 years, 73.0% NYHA III/IV, mean STS-PROM 8.4% ± 4.9%); the 1-year Kaplan-Meier estimate of death or HFH was 33.6% (95% CI 25.0%–44.1%), comprising 21.1% mortality and 19.4% HFH, with disabling stroke in 5.1%, clinically significant LVOT obstruction in 4.8%, and mitral reintervention in 11.4%. Among implanted patients, MR ≤1+ was achieved in 98.5% at 1 year, with sustained improvements in NYHA class, KCCQ score and 6-minute walk distance.
Conclusion
In carefully selected MAC patients unsuitable for surgery or repair, transseptal SAPIEN M3 TMVR delivered durable MR reduction and meaningful functional gains through 1 year.
Critical appraisal
Study design
Prospective multicentre single-arm registry with no comparator — appropriate given the absence of alternatives for this population, but it cannot isolate device benefit from natural history.
Clinical relevance
Offers a defined, reproducible transseptal pathway (no thoracotomy, no apical access) for patients previously left with medical therapy alone.
Context
MAC has frustrated both surgical and valve-in-MAC approaches with high LVOT-obstruction and mortality rates; a dedicated transseptal system with 98.5% MR ≤1+ is a step forward, though the 33.6% death/HFH burden reflects how sick these patients are.
Limitations
Only 93 of 100 attempted procedures achieved implantation, hemolysis requiring transfusion or reintervention occurred in 9.2%, and single-arm design plus careful selection limit generalisability.
Bottom line: Transseptal TMVR with SAPIEN M3 is a reasonable, durable option for highly selected MAC patients with no surgical or repair pathway — but event rates stay high, so Heart-Team selection and candid expectation-setting are essential.
— — Coronary artery disease management in severe aortic stenosis undergoing TAVI: a meta-analysis of randomised trials
Bujak K et al. · EuroIntervention 2026 (online first) · source ↗

This systematic review and pairwise/network meta-analysis compared routine coronary revascularisation with conservative management in TAVI candidates, including 5 randomised trials (N = 1,644) with 4 in the primary quantitative synthesis (N = 1,476). Compared with conservative treatment, routine PCI lowered MACE (risk ratio 0.80, 95% CI 0.65–0.98) and unplanned revascularisation (RR 0.30, 95% CI 0.11–0.83) but increased major bleeding (RR 1.57, 95% CI 1.07–2.32), and in the network meta-analysis physiology-guided PCI reduced MACE, stroke and unplanned revascularisation and ranked highest for every ischaemic outcome. Routine revascularisation reduces ischaemic outcomes in TAVI candidates at the cost of more major bleeding, and physiology-guided PCI may carry the most favourable efficacy profile among the strategies compared.

— — Acute coronary occlusions in NSTEMI undergoing PCI: insights from the CathPCI Registry
Kherallah RY et al. · JACC Cardiovasc Interv 2026 · source ↗

This CathPCI Registry analysis of 1,198,544 patients undergoing PCI (April 2018–June 2023) stratified presentations into STEMI, NSTEMI with an acute coronary occlusion (ACO, defined as 100% acute stenosis) and NSTEMI without ACO. NSTEMI-ACO accounted for 11.0% of the cohort (about 15% of NSTEMIs) and carried a higher risk of the in-hospital composite of stroke, MI or cardiovascular death than NSTEMI-no-ACO (adjusted OR 2.26, 95% CI 2.18–2.34) but a lower risk than STEMI (adjusted OR 0.54, 95% CI 0.52–0.56), with more cardiogenic shock and cardiac arrest. The study is a large real-world confirmation that occlusion MI is common among NSTEMIs and prognostically distinct, strengthening the case for earlier identification and reperfusion.

PRO-TAVI — Impact of the extent of coronary artery disease on outcomes in TAVR (SYNTAX-score substudy)
Broeze GM et al. · JACC Cardiovasc Interv 2026 · source ↗

This substudy of the randomised PRO-TAVI trial analysed 466 patients with significant coronary disease undergoing TAVR, randomised to deferral of PCI or PCI before TAVR and stratified by SYNTAX score (SS). The primary composite of death, MI, stroke and major bleeding did not differ across SS categories, but in patients with SS >17 the ischaemic composite occurred more often with deferral (30.9% vs 14.8%; adjusted HR 2.43, 95% CI 1.04–5.64; P = 0.04), driven by mortality and stroke, while bleeding was consistently lower with deferral. The findings suggest PCI deferral is safe and bleeding-sparing overall, yet patients with more complex coronary anatomy may benefit from revascularisation before TAVR — pending randomised confirmation.

— — Intravascular imaging-guided drug-eluting stenting for complex coronary lesions: a meta-analysis of RCTs
Elbahloul MA et al. · JACC Adv 2026 · source ↗

This meta-analysis pooled 18 randomised trials enrolling 18,975 patients comparing intravascular imaging (IVI)- with angiography-guided PCI for complex coronary lesions, incorporating recent large trials with divergent results. IVI guidance reduced MACE (RR 0.67, 95% CI 0.55–0.83), cardiac death (RR 0.72, 95% CI 0.53–0.97), myocardial infarction (RR 0.87, 95% CI 0.76–0.998), target-lesion revascularisation (RR 0.72) and stent thrombosis (RR 0.52, 95% CI 0.35–0.78), with lower event rates among patients achieving imaging-defined stent optimisation than among those who did not (RR 0.41, 95% CI 0.26–0.62) and a geographic interaction with benefit predominantly in Asian trials. The analysis reaffirms imaging guidance for complex PCI and suggests that achieving optimisation drives part of the benefit.

Notable

— — Conduction-system versus right-ventricular pacing after TAVR: a multicentre propensity-matched study
Diez-Delhoyo F et al. · JACC Cardiovasc Interv 2026 · source ↗

This retrospective nationwide registry propensity-matched 466 pairs of patients requiring permanent pacing within 30 days of TAVR to conduction-system (cardiac physiological) pacing or right-ventricular pacing, with a median 24-month follow-up. Conduction-system pacing was associated with a lower composite of cardiovascular death or heart-failure hospitalisation (9.7% vs 17.6%; HR 0.57, 95% CI 0.39–0.83; P = 0.003), driven by fewer heart-failure hospitalisations (7.9% vs 13.9%), with no difference in mortality. The signal supports conduction-system pacing in the post-TAVR pacemaker population, but randomised trials are needed before it becomes the default.

MATRIX — Frequency and prognosis of alternative occlusion-MI definitions across the ACS spectrum
Milzi A et al. · JACC Cardiovasc Interv 2026 · source ↗

This analysis of 6,558 ACS patients from the MATRIX trial tested five proposed occlusion-MI (OMI) definitions against 1-year all-cause mortality. OMI frequency ranged from 37.8% (culprit TIMI 0) to 76.1%, and four of the five definitions independently predicted mortality (adjusted HRs 2.08, 1.97, 1.70 and 1.47; not the fifth, 1.26); the three based exclusively on impaired culprit flow improved discrimination and reclassification beyond the STEMI/NSTEMI classification; NSTE-ACS patients meeting the TIMI 0 or TIMI 0–1 definitions had mortality similar to STEMI. OMI defined by impaired culprit flow carries prognostic weight beyond the presence or absence of ST-elevation.

— — Prolonged balloon inflation during balloon-expandable TAVI: valve expansion and early outcomes
Cardaioli F et al. · EuroIntervention 2026 · source ↗

This single-centre study analysed 84 patients with tricuspid aortic stenosis treated with the SAPIEN 3 Ultra RESILIA valve using a prolonged (8-second) balloon-inflation implantation strategy, with VARC-3-adjudicated 30-day outcomes. Prolonged inflation produced complete expansion at the inflow and outflow with only mild midframe underexpansion (98.5 ± 1.3%), device success in all cases, no 30-day stroke, a 6% pacemaker rate, a mean gradient of 9.2 mmHg and no moderate or severe paravalvular leak. The results suggest prolonged inflation may be a simpler alternative to routine "double-tap" post-dilatation, pending larger comparative studies.

PROPHET-FFR — Clinical impact of physiology-guided PCI with post-PCI optimisation
Galante D et al. · Circ Cardiovasc Interv 2026 · source ↗

This ambispective observational study categorised 1,986 patients with intermediate stenoses into deferred PCI (n = 1,307), conventional PCI without post-PCI physiology (n = 331) and physiology-guided PCI with post-PCI assessment and optimisation (n = 348). At 12 months, MACE occurred in 4.1%, 7.6% and 3.4% respectively (P = 0.011), and post-PCI physiology had lower MACE than conventional PCI (3.4% vs 7.6%; P = 0.019), driven by less target-vessel revascularisation and consistent after propensity matching (2.7% vs 8.0%). The observational signal supports physiological confirmation and optimisation after PCI but is hypothesis-generating rather than definitive.

— — High-potency P2Y12 inhibitors in chronic coronary syndrome undergoing PCI: an NCDR CathPCI analysis
Fath AR et al. · Circ Cardiovasc Interv 2026 · source ↗

This CathPCI Registry analysis examined discharge antiplatelet choice in 783,977 chronic-coronary-syndrome patients across 1,815 hospitals (April 2018–June 2024), excluding those on oral anticoagulants. Despite guidelines favouring clopidogrel, 35% were discharged on a high-potency P2Y12 inhibitor, a share that declined from 35% to 31% (driven by less ticagrelor, 29% to 23%), with procedural complexity and prior MI favouring high-potency use and age ≥75 or prior stroke disfavouring it amid wide inter-hospital variation (median odds ratio 2.59). Guideline-discordant high-potency prescribing in stable disease is common and highly variable — a clear quality-improvement target.

— — Coronary microvascular disease in MINOCA versus ANOCA: classification and outcomes
Carrington JG et al. · Circ Cardiovasc Interv 2026 · source ↗

This prospective invasive coronary-function-testing cohort classified 622 patients with nonobstructive arteries as MINOCA (73, 12%) or ANOCA (549, 88%) and followed them a median 4.5 years. Coronary microvascular disease was common in both (71% vs 64%), but endothelium-independent dysfunction was more frequent in MINOCA (41% vs 21%), and MINOCA carried substantially higher MACE (40% vs 11%; adjusted HR 2.4), with microvascular disease predicting events in both groups. The data argue that comprehensive functional testing has prognostic value across nonobstructive presentations, with MINOCA the higher-risk phenotype.

— — Early outcomes of transcatheter vacuum-assisted mass extraction in the left heart and aortic arch
Fang J et al. · EuroIntervention 2026 (online first) · source ↗

This multicentre series from six US and Canadian centres reported transcatheter vacuum-assisted mass extraction in 62 consecutive patients with left-heart or aortic-arch masses, using VARC-3-defined endpoints. Feasibility (technically successful extraction without unplanned surgery or intraprocedural death) was 85.5% and effectiveness (≥70% debulking) 77.4%, but the 30-day safety composite occurred in 14.5% with a 3.2% procedure-attributable mortality, 6.5% stroke and 8.1% major vascular complications. The technique is feasible with reasonable effectiveness, yet the complication rate underscores the need for better case selection and embolic protection.

ScienCrown — Short-frame, recapturable self-expanding TAVR in pure aortic regurgitation
Yan H et al. · Cardiovasc Revasc Med 2026 · source ↗

This exploratory single-centre series treated 13 patients with symptomatic moderate-to-severe or severe pure native aortic regurgitation using the transfemoral short-frame, fully recapturable self-expanding ScienCrown valve, with VARC-3 technical success as the primary endpoint. Technical success and 30-day device success were each 100% (13/13) and the early-safety endpoint was met in 92.3%, with one new pacemaker (7.69%), no embolisation, coronary obstruction or new cerebral infarction, a mean gradient of 7.67 mmHg and no moderate or severe residual regurgitation. The early signal is encouraging for an anatomy that defeats most TAVR platforms, but multicentre comparative data with longer follow-up are needed.

— — M-TEER at centres with versus without on-site cardiac surgery, sharing an interventional team
Marchisio G et al. · Catheter Cardiovasc Interv 2026 · source ↗

This registry compared 64 patients with severe (4+) mitral regurgitation undergoing M-TEER by the same interventional operator team at a centre without on-site surgery (n = 21) and one with on-site surgery (n = 43), linked by a shared Heart Team and formal transfer pathway. MVARC technical success was 100% versus 97.7%, and device and procedural success were each 81.0% versus 72.1% (risk difference +8.9%, 95% CI −14.9% to +27.5%; P = 0.547), with no surgical conversions or in-hospital deaths at either site. The small, same-team comparison supports exploring network-based M-TEER with shared expertise, but confidence intervals were wide and non-inferiority was not established.

DapaTAVI — Dapagliflozin after TAVI according to frailty
Gonzalez-Manzanares R et al. · J Am Heart Assoc 2026 · source ↗

This post hoc analysis of the DapaTAVI randomised trial assessed whether the benefit of post-TAVI dapagliflozin varied by frailty among 1,222 patients (47.2% frail, 40.3% prefrail, 12.4% robust), with a primary endpoint of death or worsening heart failure at 1 year. Dapagliflozin lowered the primary endpoint in frail patients (HR 0.53, 95% CI 0.36–0.77) but not in prefrail (HR 1.01) or robust patients (HR 1.26), with a significant interaction (P = 0.021) and no frailty-dependent safety signal. The findings suggest the post-TAVI SGLT2-inhibitor benefit concentrates in frail patients, who also had the worst baseline prognosis.

Tier 3

Worth knowing

— — Definition and prognosis of mitral regurgitation in transthyretin amyloid cardiomyopathy
Hauptmann L et al. · Circulation 2026 · source ↗

This multicentre cohort (8 centres, 4 countries) studied 1,101 patients with newly diagnosed transthyretin amyloid cardiomyopathy (722 derivation, 379 validation) with core-laboratory echocardiographic quantification of mitral regurgitation and endpoints of mortality and heart-failure hospitalisation. Over a median 24.9 months, 28.5% died and 22.2% were hospitalised for heart failure; spline-derived disease-specific thresholds reclassified 21% as severe MR versus ≈12%–13% by guideline cutoffs and, as a composite scheme, independently predicted mortality (HR 1.20) and HFH (HR 1.21). The restrictive low-flow physiology of amyloid means conventional criteria underestimate MR, and lower, disease-specific thresholds improve prognostication.

— — Intraprocedural cardiac arrest during PCI in the United States (NCDR CathPCI)
Mosarla RC et al. · JACC Cardiovasc Interv 2026 · source ↗

This CathPCI Registry analysis identified 36,755 intraprocedural cardiac arrests among 3,956,840 PCIs performed from 2018 to 2023. Intraprocedural arrest complicated 0.93% of PCIs (0.24% elective, 1.28% acute coronary syndrome) and its incidence rose over time (0.62% to 1.07%), with in-hospital death in 40.5% of affected patients — though risk-adjusted mortality fell slightly (45.1% to 43.6%). STEMI, non-ST acute coronary syndromes, ventricular arrhythmias, severe aortic stenosis, mechanical support and coronary perforation were among the independent predictors of this rare but highly lethal complication.

COMBINE-INTERVENE — Design and rationale of combined ischaemia- and vulnerable-plaque-guided PCI
Kedhi E et al. · EuroIntervention 2026 · source ↗

This paper describes the design of a global, prospective, randomised, single-blinded, sham-controlled superiority trial enrolling 1,223 all-comer patients with multivessel disease (≥2 lesions, diameter stenosis ≥50%). The experimental arm revascularises lesions that are severely ischaemic (FFR ≤0.75) or carry OCT-assessed vulnerable-plaque morphology even without ischaemia (FFR >0.80), versus a standard FFR ≤0.80 strategy, with a 24-month composite of cardiac death, any MI or clinically driven revascularisation; enrolment is complete across 50 centres (NCT05333068). It is the first trial to test whether treating plaques for their biology — not just their flow significance — reduces events.

FARGO — Long-term outcomes after physiology-guided versus angiography-guided CABG
Trøan J et al. · JACC Adv 2026 · source ↗

This extended follow-up of the FARGO randomised trial reports outcomes in 100 patients assigned to FFR-guided or angiography-guided coronary artery bypass grafting after a median of 8.8 years. The FFR-guided group had more MACCE (52% vs 32%; P = 0.03), driven by more revascularisation (32% vs 12%; P = 0.01) that was concentrated within the first year, while myocardial infarction, death and stroke did not differ. FFR-guided grafting, which deferred some lesions, did not improve long-term outcomes and was associated with more early repeat revascularisation — a cautionary counterpoint to physiology enthusiasm in the surgical setting.

PROLONG-TAVR — Short-term complications of TAVR by prosthesis: a meta-analysis
Castillo-Perez M et al. · Catheter Cardiovasc Interv 2026 · source ↗

This systematic review and meta-analysis pooled 242 studies comprising 181,187 patients to estimate prosthesis-specific short-term TAVR complication rates. SAPIEN 3 had the lowest moderate-to-severe paravalvular leak (1.04%) and stroke (1.87%), ACURATE neo2 the lowest permanent pacemaker rate (7.7%), and Evolut PRO and SAPIEN 3 among the lowest major-vascular and bleeding rates, while annular rupture, coronary obstruction and valve embolisation remained rare (<1%). Newer-generation valves have reduced short-term complications overall, with device-specific trade-offs — paravalvular leak remains more frequent with self-expanding valves, whereas pacemaker implantation, the most frequent complication, is generally less frequent with balloon-expandable valves and ACURATE neo2.

— — Pre-procedural prediction of LVEF normalisation after TAVI
Hida Y et al. · Catheter Cardiovasc Interv 2026 · source ↗

Using a Japanese multicentre registry, this study derived a pre-procedural score for complete LVEF normalisation (>50%) one year after TAVI in 177 patients with baseline LVEF <40%. Fifty-three percent normalised, and lower body-mass index (OR 0.86), higher aortic peak velocity (OR 2.45) and smaller LV end-diastolic diameter (OR 0.93) independently predicted recovery, stratifying patients into low-, intermediate- and high-probability groups (15%, 45% and 71%). The simple tool is intended for counselling and follow-up planning — explicitly not for TAVI eligibility — and awaits broader validation.

— — ANOCA patients with prior obstructive CAD and PCI have higher odds of epicardial spasm
Tzoumas A et al. · JACC Adv 2026 · source ↗

This prospective registry of 624 ANOCA patients undergoing invasive coronary functional testing compared those with (11%) and without a history of obstructive CAD treated by PCI. Patients with prior PCI had a much higher prevalence of angiographic epicardial spasm (70% vs 34%) and 4.55-fold higher adjusted odds (95% CI 2.65–8.07), along with an 11-point lower Seattle Angina summary score, while microvascular dysfunction was similar. These findings underscore the need for comprehensive invasive coronary functional testing in ANOCA patients with prior PCI.

— — Prognostic impact of coronary artery ectasia after acute coronary syndrome: a meta-analysis
Mohamed AE et al. · Cardiovasc Revasc Med 2026 · source ↗

This systematic review and meta-analysis pooled 12 observational studies (13,540 patients, 1,177 with coronary artery ectasia) presenting with acute coronary syndrome. Ectasia was associated with a higher risk of recurrent myocardial infarction (RR 1.96, 95% CI 1.16–3.32) but was not significantly associated with all-cause or cardiac mortality, repeat revascularisation or overall MACE. Ectasia appears to mark a higher re-infarction-risk phenotype after ACS, though ectasia-specific risk stratification and treatment remain undefined.

— — Dual antiplatelet therapy duration in diabetic patients after drug-eluting stenting
Fan R et al. · Catheter Cardiovasc Interv 2026 · source ↗

This single-centre prospective cohort grouped 536 diabetic patients after drug-eluting stent implantation by actual DAPT duration — short (<6 months, n = 312) or extended (≥6 months, n = 224) — with 12-month MACE and BARC 2/3/5 bleeding endpoints. Extended DAPT reduced MACE (7.8% vs 10.5%; HR 0.72, 95% CI 0.53–0.98), driven by less target-vessel revascularisation, without a significant rise in overall bleeding, but it more than doubled recurrent bleeding in patients with a prior bleeding history (HR 2.10). The data favour individualised DAPT duration in diabetics, specifically avoiding prolongation in those who have already bled.

— — Impaired flow and physiology in coronaries anomalous from the opposite sinus
Lo Rito M et al. · Catheter Cardiovasc Interv 2026 · source ↗

This prospective study used continuous thermodilution to measure absolute coronary blood flow and microvascular resistance in 48 patients with anomalous aortic origin of a coronary artery (31 with high-risk anatomy, 17 without) versus 45 controls. At rest, flow and resistance were similar across groups, but during hyperaemia the high-risk group had significantly lower coronary blood flow (179 vs 290 mL/min; P = 0.015) and higher microvascular resistance (433 vs 305; P = 0.012). High-risk anatomical features — intramural segment, acute take-off angle and slit-like ostium — translate into measurable physiological impairment, offering an objective adjunct to anatomy alone.

— — Real-world invasive evaluation of myocardial bridging: an Italian multicentre survey
Elia S et al. · Catheter Cardiovasc Interv 2026 · source ↗

This survey captured the practices of 93 interventional cardiologists across 28 Italian centres regarding functional assessment of myocardial bridging. Physiology was performed in fewer than 10% of bridging cases by 57% of respondents, and acetylcholine testing for bridge-related spasm by only 49.5%, yet 87.1% endorsed a "full-physiology" approach as useful and 93.5% would join a randomised trial, citing evidence uncertainty and procedural time as barriers. The wide, evidence-starved variation in practice underscores the need for standardised invasive workflows and dedicated mechanistic studies in myocardial bridging.

Landmark Revisited

DANAMI-3-PRIMULTI — Complete revascularisation versus culprit-lesion-only PCI in STEMI with multivessel disease
Engstrøm T et al. · Lancet 2015 · primary source ↗
Structured abstract
Background
Patients with STEMI and multivessel disease have a worse prognosis than those with single-vessel disease, but it was unknown whether FFR-guided complete revascularisation of non-infarct arteries improved outcomes over treating the infarct-related artery alone.
Methods
Open-label randomised trial at two Danish university hospitals: after successful primary PCI of the infarct-related artery, 627 STEMI patients with ≥1 significant additional stenosis were randomised 1:1 to no further invasive treatment or FFR-guided complete revascularisation before discharge; the primary endpoint was a composite of all-cause mortality, non-fatal reinfarction and ischaemia-driven revascularisation of non-infarct lesions, analysed by intention to treat.
Key results
Over a median 27 months, the primary endpoint occurred in 68 of 313 (22%) culprit-only patients versus 40 of 314 (13%) complete-revascularisation patients (hazard ratio 0.56, 95% CI 0.38–0.83; P = 0.004); the effect was driven by fewer repeat revascularisations, with no difference in all-cause mortality or non-fatal reinfarction.
Conclusion
FFR-guided complete revascularisation significantly reduced future events, so patients could safely have all lesions treated during the index admission rather than the infarct artery alone.
The question & the verdict

By 2015 primary PCI reliably reopened the culprit artery, but whether to touch bystander stenoses in STEMI — and how to pick them — was contested, with fear of destabilising non-culprit lesions in a prothrombotic milieu. DANAMI-3-PRIMULTI, alongside PRAMI and CvLPRIT, delivered the verdict that staged (in-hospital, pre-discharge) FFR-guided completion was better than leaving bystander disease alone, helping move guidelines toward routine complete revascularisation in selected STEMI patients. Crucially, the win was in repeat revascularisation, not death or reinfarction — a nuance often lost when the trial is cited.

Statistical appraisal

The primary endpoint was a composite dominated by ischaemia-driven revascularisation — a soft, operator-influenced component especially vulnerable to bias in an open-label trial where the complete-revascularisation arm was already catheterised and physiologically mapped. The study was event-driven and modest in size, so the neutral hard-endpoint results (mortality, reinfarction) sit inside wide confidence intervals rather than proving true equivalence. Two design choices shaped its external validity: completion was FFR-guided (not angiographic), and it was staged before discharge rather than performed acutely — exactly the sequencing distinction that this week's meta-analysis suggests may carry a mortality penalty when completion is instead done immediately.

Subsequent substudies & follow-ups

A disease-severity substudy (Lønborg J et al., Circ Cardiovasc Interv 2017; PMID 28404623) found the benefit concentrated in 3-vessel disease (HR 0.33, 95% CI 0.17–0.64) with no significant effect in 2-vessel disease (HR 0.77; P for interaction = 0.046), and in non-infarct stenoses ≥90% (HR 0.32, 95% CI 0.18–0.62). The 10-year follow-up (Marquard JM et al., J Am Coll Cardiol 2025; PMID 40392668) showed the primary outcome remained reduced (HR 0.76, 95% CI 0.60–0.94; P = 0.014), still driven by less revascularisation (OR 0.62, 95% CI 0.44–0.89), with no difference in death (25% vs 24%) or recurrent MI (OR 0.90, 95% CI 0.60–1.35).

Why it still matters: DANAMI-3-PRIMULTI anchored the shift to complete revascularisation in STEMI — but its durable benefit was fewer repeat procedures, not fewer deaths, and it tested a staged, physiology-guided strategy; that distinction is precisely the lens for reading this week's finding that doing it all immediately may be the costlier choice.
