Top Highlights

  • Transcatheter tricuspid repair cut death and HF hospitalisation — TRIC-I-HF (NEJM) showed transcatheter tricuspid repair reduces death and heart-failure hospitalisation, with a 2.42 win ratio at 1 year and 3-year event-free survival of 52.4% vs 21.0%. ↓
  • Sirolimus-eluting balloons rivalled DES in high-bleeding-risk PCI — in the SELUTION DeNovo HBR substudy, an SEB-first strategy trended to lower target-vessel failure (4.9% vs 7.9%) while letting far more patients stop antiplatelets early. ↓
  • A 3-month DOAC cut TAVR leaflet thrombosis early, but the benefit vanished by 1 year — NOTION-4 saw HALT fall to 12.1% at 3 months yet converge (32.2% vs 28.3%) once anticoagulation stopped, at a bleeding cost. ↓
  • Evolocumab before PCI slashed LDL but not 1-year events — AMUNDSEN got >80% of acute-MI patients to target (median LDL-C 16 vs 56 mg/dL) with no clinical benefit at one year. ↓
  • Anticoagulation beat aspirin for TAVI leaflet protection — in ACASA-TAVI, NOAC monotherapy halved HALT (16.2% vs 28.6%) and was noninferior for bleeding. ↓
  • Bivalirudin helped even low-bleeding-risk STEMI — the BRIGHT-4 subanalysis showed a death-and-bleeding benefit in low-risk patients (1.4% vs 2.9%), not just high-risk ones. ↓
  • Pressure-optimised stenting improved primary-PCI reperfusion — RAPID-POP raised hierarchical reperfusion success (WIN ratio 1.42) and cut post-dilatation. ↓
  • ESC 2026 late-breakers (pre-publication) — ISOLEDS (OCT noninferior to IVUS in left-main bifurcation PCI), DISCO (no survival gain from immediate angiography after OHCA without ST-elevation), PRAGUE-26 (catheter-directed thrombolysis improved intermediate-high-risk PE) and EVAOLD (selective strategy not noninferior in elderly NSTEMI). ↓
  • Landmark revisited — MASTER DAPT, the trial that made one month of DAPT the default for high-bleeding-risk PCI, cutting bleeding with no rise in ischaemic events. ↓

Congress Highlights — ESC 2026

Late-breaking trials presented at ESC Congress 2026 (Munich, 28–31 August) that do not yet have a peer-reviewed publication — congress-reported and exempt from the PubMed gate, so every figure below is verified against the linked ESC Press Office source. Simultaneously published Hot Line late-breakers (TAVI PCI, TRIC-I-HF, ACASA-TAVI) appear in the tiers below with their PubMed links.

ISOLEDS — OCT was noninferior to IVUS for guiding distal left-main bifurcation PCI
Zeng Y (Beijing Anzhen Hospital) · ESC 2026 late-breaker · source ↗

[ESC 2026 — late-breaker, pre-publication] This open-label noninferiority trial randomised 664 patients with true distal left-main bifurcation lesions at 24 Chinese centres 1:1 to optical coherence tomography (OCT)- or intravascular ultrasound (IVUS)-guided PCI (mean age 63.5 years, 22.1% women). At a median 362 days, the primary endpoint of target lesion failure (cardiac death, target-lesion MI or clinically driven target-lesion revascularisation) occurred in 14.4% with OCT vs 19.6% with IVUS (adjusted hazard ratio 0.90; 95% CI 0.59–1.36; p for noninferiority=0.0003), meeting noninferiority. These figures are congress-reported and not yet peer-reviewed; the investigators stress the trial does not establish equivalence or superiority, and imaging choice should still reflect anatomy, image-acquisition feasibility and local expertise.

DISCO — Immediate coronary angiography did not improve survival after OHCA without ST-elevation
Rubertsson S (Uppsala University) · ESC 2026 late-breaker · source ↗

[ESC 2026 — late-breaker, pre-publication] DISCO randomised 1,006 unconscious patients with witnessed out-of-hospital cardiac arrest, return of spontaneous circulation and no ST-elevation on ECG at 23 centres in Sweden, Denmark and the Netherlands to immediate coronary angiography (within 120 minutes) or a deferred strategy (angiography delayed at least 72 hours). Survival at 30 days — the primary endpoint — did not differ (54.6% immediate vs 53.6% deferred; hazard ratio 0.95; 95% CI 0.74–1.21; p=0.67), with no difference at 180 days or in neurological secondary endpoints, and an individual-patient-data meta-analysis of five randomised trials (2,173 patients) reached the same conclusion. These results are congress-reported and not yet peer-reviewed.

PRAGUE-26 — Catheter-directed thrombolysis improved short-term outcomes in intermediate-high-risk pulmonary embolism
Kroupa J / Kočka V · ESC 2026 late-breaker · source ↗

[ESC 2026 — late-breaker, pre-publication] This academic trial randomised 558 patients (median age 64 years, 41% women) with acute intermediate-high-risk pulmonary embolism at 11 Czech sites 1:1 to conventional catheter-directed thrombolysis (CDT, without ultrasound facilitation) or standard anticoagulation alone. The primary composite of all-cause mortality, PE recurrence or cardiorespiratory decompensation/collapse within 7 days occurred in 0.7% with CDT vs 6.8% with anticoagulation (relative risk 0.10; 95% CI 0.02–0.44; p<0.001), driven mainly by less decompensation/collapse, with no apparent difference in 7-day bleeding (4.6% vs 5.0%; p=0.846) although two intracranial bleeds occurred in the CDT arm and none with standard care. These figures are congress-reported and await peer-reviewed publication.

EVAOLD — A stress-imaging-guided selective strategy did not prove noninferior to routine invasive management in elderly NSTEMI
Barone-Rochette G (University Hospital of Grenoble) · ESC 2026 late-breaker · source ↗

[ESC 2026 — late-breaker, pre-publication] This open-label noninferiority trial at 25 French centres randomised patients aged 80 years or older hospitalised with NSTEMI to a stress-imaging-guided selective invasive strategy (stress test, then angiography only for moderate–severe ischaemia) or routine invasive management, and was stopped for futility at the first prespecified interim analysis after 587 of a planned 1,756 patients. The primary endpoint of death, nonfatal MI or nonfatal stroke at 1 year occurred in 24.1% of the selective-strategy group vs 20.7% of the routine-invasive group (hazard ratio 1.22; 95% CI 0.86–1.72; p=0.27), so selective management did not demonstrate noninferiority. These results are congress-reported and not yet peer-reviewed, and should be read in light of early termination and lower-than-expected event rates.

Tier 1

Practice-changing

TRIC-I-HF — Transcatheter tricuspid repair vs medical therapy in high-risk severe tricuspid regurgitation
Hausleiter J et al. · N Engl J Med 2026 · source ↗

This ESC 2026 Hot Line trial at 29 German valve centres randomised 360 patients (mean age 80.3; 56.4% women) with symptomatic severe tricuspid regurgitation at high heart-failure risk to transcatheter tricuspid repair plus medical therapy (n=237; predominantly edge-to-edge repair) or medical therapy alone (n=123). The first primary endpoint — a win ratio of all-cause mortality, heart-failure hospitalisation and failure to improve quality of life at 1 year — favoured repair (win ratio 2.42; 95% CI 1.76–3.33; p<0.001), and freedom from the co-primary of all-cause mortality or heart-failure hospitalisation at 3 years was 52.4% vs 21.0% (hazard ratio 0.40; 95% CI 0.29–0.55; p<0.001). TRIC-I-HF shows transcatheter repair reduces death and heart-failure hospitalisation in advanced disease.

SELUTION DeNovo HBR — PCI using sirolimus-eluting balloons in patients at high bleeding risk
Rissanen T et al. · EuroIntervention 2026 (online first) · Trial: NCT04859985 ↗
Structured abstract
Background
Drug-eluting stents (DES) are the mainstay of PCI in high-bleeding-risk (HBR) patients, but drug-coated balloons may be an alternative that reduces reliance on prolonged antiplatelet therapy.
Methods
The SELUTION DeNovo trial randomised 3,323 PCI candidates 1:1 to sirolimus-eluting balloons (SEB) with provisional stenting or systematic DES; this prespecified substudy analysed 522 patients meeting Academic Research Consortium (ARC)-HBR criteria (269 SEB, 253 DES). At 1 year the SEB and DES strategies were compared for target-vessel failure (TVF; cardiac death, target-vessel MI, clinically driven target-vessel revascularisation), BARC 3–5 bleeding, and net adverse clinical events (NACE; TVF plus BARC 3–5).
Key results
Bailout stenting was needed in 17.1% of SEB patients and 67% of all patients were discharged on oral anticoagulants. At 1 year, TVF occurred in 4.9% (SEB) vs 7.9% (DES) (absolute risk difference [RD] −3.05%, 95% CI −7.27 to 1.17); BARC 3–5 bleeding in 2.3% vs 3.2% (RD −0.91%, 95% CI −3.71 to 1.89); and NACE in 6.8% vs 10.7% (RD −3.94%, 95% CI −8.80 to 0.92). All antiplatelet medication was stopped in 7.7% vs 2.5% at 30 days (p=0.005) and 27.8% vs 14.9% at 6 months (p<0.001).
Conclusion
Although this subset is not powered for definitive conclusions, an SEB strategy with minimal stenting appears to be a safe and effective alternative to systematic DES implantation for HBR patients.
Critical appraisal
Study design
Prespecified HBR subgroup (522 of 3,323) of a multicentre, open-label randomised trial, analysed by intention-to-treat using cumulative-incidence functions with non-cardiac death as a competing risk.
Clinical relevance
A leave-nothing-behind SEB strategy let roughly twice as many HBR patients discontinue all antiplatelets by 6 months — the group in whom bleeding, not restenosis, most often drives outcomes.
Context
The authors note that the DEBUT trial showed paclitaxel-coated balloons superior to bare-metal stents in HBR patients, and a BASKET-SMALL 2 HBR substudy suggested paclitaxel balloons comparable to DES in small vessels; SELUTION extends the question to a sirolimus balloon across a broader vessel range.
Limitations
Underpowered subset with wide confidence intervals crossing zero for every endpoint; exploratory, non-multiplicity-adjusted CIs that (per the authors) should not be used to infer definitive treatment effects; open-label design.
Bottom line: In high-bleeding-risk PCI, a sirolimus-eluting-balloon-first strategy achieved numerically lower TVF and NACE than systematic DES and enabled much earlier antiplatelet cessation — encouraging but hypothesis-generating, pending an adequately powered trial.
NOTION-4 — Short-term anticoagulation and subclinical leaflet thickening after TAVR
Jørgensen TH et al. · J Am Coll Cardiol 2026 · primary source ↗ · Trial: NCT06449469 ↗
Structured abstract
Background
After TAVR, subclinical leaflet thrombosis — seen on CT as hypoattenuated leaflet thickening (HALT) — is common and may be associated with thromboembolic events.
Methods
NOTION-4 was a randomised controlled trial enrolling patients without an indication for oral anticoagulation shortly after successful TAVR, assigned to lifelong single antiplatelet therapy (SAPT) or 3 months of a direct oral anticoagulant followed by lifelong SAPT (DOAC-3m). The primary endpoint was HALT prevalence at 12 months, with the trial powered for superiority of the experimental strategy.
Key results
Of 352 patients randomised 1:1, 176 remained in the SAPT group and 171 in the DOAC-3m group. At 3 months, HALT was seen in 31.8% (SAPT) vs 12.1% (DOAC-3m); at 1 year, HALT occurred in 32.2% vs 28.3% among patients with available CT (risk difference −3.9%; 95% CI −14.4% to 6.6%; P=0.54). The combined risk of all-cause mortality, stroke, or major/life-threatening bleeding at 12 months was 2.3% (SAPT) vs 8.2% (DOAC-3m) (risk difference 5.9%; 95% CI 1.2% to 10.6%).
Conclusion
Three months of DOAC significantly reduced HALT at 3 months versus SAPT, but the effect was attenuated by 9 months after discontinuation of anticoagulation.
Critical appraisal
Study design
Randomised, superiority-powered trial with a CT-imaging primary endpoint (HALT prevalence) at 12 months in anticoagulation-naïve TAVR recipients.
Clinical relevance
It directly tests the intuitive strategy of a short anticoagulation "burst" to prevent leaflet thrombosis — and shows the imaging benefit does not persist once the DOAC is stopped.
Context
HALT is a CT finding of uncertain clinical import; NOTION-4 adds to the antithrombotic-after-TAVR literature alongside this issue's ACASA-TAVI trial, which tested continuous anticoagulation rather than a 3-month course.
Limitations
Surrogate imaging endpoint rather than clinical events; a numerically higher composite of death/stroke/bleeding in the DOAC-3m arm; CT scans were not available for all patients at 1 year.
Bottom line: A time-limited 3-month DOAC transiently suppresses TAVR leaflet thickening but confers no durable imaging advantage after it stops — and did not reduce clinical events — arguing against routine short-course anticoagulation to prevent HALT.
AMUNDSEN — Evolocumab before PCI for acute myocardial infarction
Montalescot G et al. · JAMA 2026 · primary source ↗ · Trial: NCT04951856 ↗
Structured abstract
Background
PCSK9 inhibition is recommended only as second- or third-line lipid-lowering therapy after MI, leaving prolonged periods of inadequate LDL-C control; whether an in-cath-lab decision to start it before mechanical reperfusion improves LDL-C control and outcomes was unknown.
Methods
This international, phase 4, prospective randomised open-label, blinded-endpoint trial at 48 sites in 6 countries enrolled adults with high-risk STEMI (aged >55 years) or high-risk NSTEMI undergoing PCI, randomised 1:1 to evolocumab 140 mg subcutaneously every 2 weeks for 1 year (first injection before PCI) plus standard care (n=1,087) or standard care alone (n=1,074). The primary outcome was LDL-C <55 mg/dL and ≥50% reduction from baseline at 12 months; the main clinical endpoint was all-cause death or unplanned cardiovascular hospitalisation at 12 months.
Key results
Among 2,161 patients (mean age 67 years; 79% male; 58% STEMI), the primary outcome was met in 792 of 970 (82%) with evolocumab vs 370 of 934 (40%) with standard care (adjusted OR 5.54; 95% CI 4.50–6.82; P<.001); at 6 weeks median LDL-C was 16 vs 56 mg/dL. The clinical endpoint occurred in 159 of 1,087 (14.6%) with evolocumab vs 165 of 1,074 (15.4%) with standard care (adjusted OR 0.94; 95% CI 0.73–1.19; P=.59).
Conclusion
First-line evolocumab produced rapid, sustained LDL-C reduction with >80% reaching target at 1 year, but detected no clinical benefit in the first year — arguing against clinically meaningful acute pleiotropic effects on top of standard care.
Critical appraisal
Study design
Large multinational PROBE-design randomised trial with a biochemical co-primary endpoint (LDL-C target attainment) and an exploratory clinical endpoint at 12 months.
Clinical relevance
It settles the achievable-LDL question decisively — starting a PCSK9 inhibitor in the cath lab gets the vast majority of acute-MI patients to guideline target within weeks.
Context
The neutral clinical result mirrors the timeframe of prior PCSK9-outcome trials, where event-curve separation emerges over years, not months; standard-care patients could also receive a PCSK9 inhibitor per guideline indication.
Limitations
Open-label; only one year of follow-up — too short to expect a hard-outcome signal; the clinical endpoint was underpowered relative to the biochemical primary.
Bottom line: Initiating evolocumab before PCI for acute MI reliably drives LDL-C to target far faster than usual care, but a one-year window shows no clinical dividend — the case for early PCSK9 inhibition rests on speed to target and long-term, not acute, benefit.
ACASA-TAVI — Anticoagulation vs antiplatelet monotherapy after TAVI
Dodgson CS et al. · JAMA 2026 · source ↗

This Norwegian 3-centre open-label, blinded-endpoint trial randomised 360 patients aged 65–80 undergoing TAVI 1:1 to 12 months of factor-Xa-inhibitor NOAC monotherapy or aspirin monotherapy, with co-primary endpoints of leaflet thrombosis (HALT on core-lab 4D-CT at 12 months) and a VARC-3 bleeding/thromboembolic/death safety composite. HALT occurred in 16.2% with NOAC vs 28.6% with aspirin (risk ratio 0.55; 95% CI 0.37–0.82; P=.004), and the safety composite occurred in 7.5% vs 10.6% (risk difference −3.3%; P for noninferiority <.001). NOAC monotherapy reduced HALT and was noninferior for bleeding, thromboembolism or death, suggesting anticoagulation can benefit selected younger TAVI patients.

BRIGHT-4 — Bivalirudin vs heparin by bleeding risk in primary PCI for STEMI
Qiu M et al. · J Am Coll Cardiol 2026 · source ↗

This prespecified analysis of BRIGHT-4 stratified 6,016 radial-access primary-PCI STEMI patients by baseline bleeding risk (low bleeding risk [LBR] = CRUSADE score <30; 4,581 patients, 76.1%) to test whether bivalirudin's benefit is confined to higher-risk patients. In LBR patients the 30-day composite of death or BARC 3–5 bleeding occurred in 1.4% with bivalirudin vs 2.9% with heparin (HR 0.49; 95% CI 0.32–0.75), versus 8.1% vs 9.2% (HR 0.88; 95% CI 0.62–1.26) in non-LBR patients. Bivalirudin plus a 2–4-hour high-dose infusion reduced death and major bleeding even in low-bleeding-risk STEMI, arguing against restricting it to high-risk patients.

RAPID-POP — Pressure-optimised vs conventional stenting during primary PCI
Hakeem A et al. · JACC Cardiovasc Interv 2026 · source ↗

This multicentre blinded-endpoint randomised trial assigned 607 primary-PCI STEMI patients to pressure-optimised stenting (POP; inflation to ≥14 atm held until pressure stability) or conventional rapid inflation–deflation. POP improved the hierarchical reperfusion endpoint (WIN ratio 1.42; 95% CI 1.11–1.79; P=0.004; net WIN difference +12.64%) and reduced post-dilatation (65.3% vs 91.0%; P<0.001), with a nonsignificant 30-day MACE trend (2.0% vs 4.1%; HR 0.49; P=0.15). A simple change in stent-deployment technique improved microvascular reperfusion, though larger trials are needed to confirm effects on clinical outcomes.

REBOOT-PARADOX — TAVR or medical therapy for paradoxical low-flow, low-gradient AS
Mehilli J et al. · J Am Coll Cardiol 2026 · source ↗

This multicentre open-label trial randomised 120 of a planned 783 symptomatic patients with paradoxical low-flow, low-gradient AS (aortic valve area ≤1.0 cm², mean gradient <40 mm Hg, stroke-volume index <35 mL/m², EF ≥50%) 2:1 to TAVR plus optimal medical therapy or OMT alone before enrolment stopped early for slow recruitment. The primary endpoint of all-cause death occurred in 29.0% vs 37.1% (HR 0.83; 95% CI 0.41–1.66; P=0.60) and 5-year mortality was 44.9% vs 56.6% (HR 0.76; 95% CI 0.43–1.35; P=0.35), with 19 OMT patients crossing over to TAVR mainly for symptom progression. TAVR improved symptoms (NYHA I/II at 1 year, 83.0% vs 59.0%) and reduced rehospitalisations, but the underpowered trial leaves the mortality effect unresolved and supports individualised surveillance with timely TAVR on deterioration.

Tier 2

Notable

TVT Registry — Redo-TAVR with self-expanding Evolut valves
Rogers T et al. · JACC Cardiovasc Interv 2026 · source ↗

In the STS/ACC TVT Registry, 439 patients (mean 78.9 years, 63.1% women) undergoing redo-TAVR with self-expanding Evolut valves were compared with contemporary native-valve Evolut TAVR. Procedural complications were low; 1-year gradients were modestly higher than after native TAVR (11.3 vs 8.2 mm Hg; P<0.001) but adjusted mortality (HR 0.91), stroke (HR 0.91) and readmission (HR 0.92) were comparable, with sustained quality-of-life gains. Redo-TAVR with Evolut appears feasible and effective for failed transcatheter valves in appropriately selected patients.

ACOR — Supra- vs intra-annular self-expanding valves in valve-in-valve TAVR
Singh K et al. · JACC Cardiovasc Interv 2026 · source ↗

This Australian Cardiovascular Outcomes Registry analysis compared supra-annular (SASEV, n=566) versus intra-annular (IASEV, n=117) self-expanding valves across 683 valve-in-valve TAVR procedures. Post-procedure mean gradients were similar (14.9 vs 13.3 mm Hg; P=0.070); at 12 months there were numerical but nonsignificant trends toward more major adverse cardiac/cerebrovascular events (11.9% vs 7.1%; P=0.129) and stroke (5.4% vs 1.7%; P=0.058) with IASEV. Both valve types delivered favourable and broadly similar haemodynamic and clinical outcomes after ViV-TAVR.

MiCLASP — 1-year PASCAL M-TEER in atrial vs ventricular functional MR
Mahabadi AA et al. · Circ Cardiovasc Interv 2026 · source ↗

This European post-market study reported 1-year mitral transcatheter edge-to-edge repair outcomes with the PASCAL system in 295 patients with functional MR — atrial (AFMR, 17.6%) and ventricular (VFMR, 82.4%). Procedural success was high (96.2% AFMR, 97.1% VFMR); 1-year freedom from death or heart-failure hospitalisation was 79.3% vs 70.9% (P=0.267), with MR ≤1+ in 93.1% and 81.3% and significant NYHA and KCCQ improvement in both groups. PASCAL M-TEER was safe and effective across both functional-MR phenotypes despite differing baseline profiles.

— — Myocardial bridges and long-term outcomes after LAD CTO recanalisation
Geng Y et al. · JACC Cardiovasc Interv 2026 · source ↗

Among 685 patients undergoing IVUS-guided PCI of left-anterior-descending chronic total occlusions, myocardial bridges were identified in 393 (57.4%), and stents extended into the bridged segment in 44.3% of these. Over 5 years, patients with bridges had more MACE (16.1% vs 8.9%; P=0.006), driven by ischaemia-driven revascularisation, and stent extension into the bridge carried higher MACE than sparing it (21.4% vs 11.9%; P=0.0017). Bridge-related risk after LAD CTO recanalisation is driven mainly by stenting across the bridge, favouring a bridge-sparing strategy.

— — Antithrombotic therapy after PCI in atrial fibrillation (IPD network meta-analysis)
Chiarito M et al. · Eur Heart J 2026 · source ↗

This individual-patient-data network meta-analysis pooled 6 randomised trials (10,634 AF patients undergoing PCI) comparing DOAC + P2Y12 inhibitor, VKA + dual antiplatelet therapy, VKA + single antiplatelet, and DOAC + DAPT. The composite of cardiovascular death, MI or stroke at 1 year did not differ across strategies (e.g. DOAC + P2Y12 inhibitor vs VKA + DAPT, HR 1.16; 95% CI 0.97–1.41), while DOAC + P2Y12 inhibitor reduced TIMI major bleeding (HR 0.48 vs VKA + DAPT) and intracranial haemorrhage (HR 0.21). A 14-day landmark analysis flagged more early MI and stent thrombosis with the least-intensive regimens, supporting a brief window of more intensive antiplatelet cover immediately after PCI.

DANCODE — Vitamin K2 and D3 in severe coronary calcification
Hasific S et al. · Circulation 2026 · source ↗

This multicentre double-blind trial randomised 400 patients with severe coronary calcification (CAC ≥400 Agatston units; 398 included in analysis, median 903 AU) to vitamin K2 (720 µg/d) plus vitamin D3 (25 µg/d) or placebo for 24 months. Mean CAC increase was 196 AU with supplementation vs 248 AU with placebo (between-group difference −52 AU; 95% CI −79 to −24), consistent across sex and baseline-CAC subgroups. Vitamin K2/D3 modestly slowed calcium progression on CT, though the clinical significance of this imaging endpoint remains unproven.

— — Optimised balloon-expandable TAVI and clinical outcomes
Vitez L et al. · JACC Adv 2026 · source ↗

This single-centre cohort assessed fluoroscopic implantation quality in 1,032 patients undergoing transfemoral balloon-expandable TAVR (Sapien S3/S3 Ultra). Suboptimal implantation — noncoaxiality, excessive depth, or ≥20% underexpansion — occurred in 42.9% and was associated with more 30-day early-safety events (43.3% vs 19.9%; adjusted OR 3.05) and more 5-year stage-2 bioprosthetic valve failure (subhazard ratio 4.40; 95% CI 1.25–13.59). Optimising coaxiality, depth and expansion at implantation may protect long-term valve durability.

— — Bioprosthetic valve failure after TAVR (international registry)
Trimaille A et al. · Circ Cardiovasc Interv 2026 · source ↗

In an international 13-centre registry of 7,501 TAVR patients, bioprosthetic valve failure (VARC-3) occurred in 380 (5.1%) over a median 4 years of follow-up. Cumulative BVF incidence rose from 1.4% at 1 year to 4.6% at 5 years and 8.3% at 10 years, with younger age, smoking, balloon-expandable valves and prosthesis size <26 mm among independent predictors. Late valve failure is uncommon but climbs steadily with time, underscoring durability surveillance as TAVR expands to younger patients.

VESALIUS-CV — Total (first and recurrent) events with evolocumab
Nicolau JC et al. · J Am Coll Cardiol 2026 · source ↗

This analysis of VESALIUS-CV (12,257 high-risk patients without prior MI or stroke) evaluated evolocumab's effect on total first-and-recurrent major adverse cardiovascular events. Evolocumab reduced first 4-point MACE by 19% (HR 0.81; 95% CI 0.73–0.89) and total 4-point MACE by 20% (incidence-rate ratio 0.80; 95% CI 0.71–0.90), projected to prevent 55 total events per 1,000 patients over 5 years. Counting recurrent events magnifies the absolute benefit of PCSK9 inhibition in these primary-prevention-like high-risk patients.

EuroTR — Prognostic value of NYHA class in tricuspid TEER
Wolff C et al. · JACC Adv 2026 · source ↗

In 3,424 patients from the EuroTR registry undergoing tricuspid transcatheter edge-to-edge repair, baseline NYHA class was tested as a prognostic marker over a median 460 days. Two-year mortality was 28.4%, and each 1-class higher baseline NYHA independently predicted death (HR 1.8 per class; 95% CI 1.5–2.1; P<0.001). Simple NYHA assessment carries strong prognostic weight in T-TEER candidates and may aid patient selection and timing.

Tier 3

Worth knowing

SIRONA — 2-year sirolimus- vs paclitaxel-coated balloon in femoropopliteal disease
Teichgräber U et al. · JACC Cardiovasc Interv 2026 · source ↗

SIRONA randomised 482 patients with femoropopliteal disease (96.5% intermittent claudication) to sirolimus-coated (SCB, n=238) or paclitaxel-coated (PCB, n=244) balloon angioplasty; this prespecified analysis reports 2-year outcomes. Vascular quality-of-life gains were sustained and similar (between-group difference 0.05; P=0.63), with comparable primary patency (64.6% vs 67.2%; P=0.34) and freedom from clinically driven target-lesion revascularisation (91.2% vs 88.1%; P=0.42). Sirolimus-coated balloons matched paclitaxel devices through 2 years, offering an alternative antiproliferative option pending longer-term safety data.

— — TAVR for severe bicuspid aortic stenosis (meta-analysis)
Kumar S et al. · JACC Adv 2026 · source ↗

This meta-analysis of 52 studies compared TAVR in bicuspid versus tricuspid aortic valves and TAVR versus surgery in bicuspid disease. Bicuspid anatomy carried more paravalvular leak (RR 1.44), aortic-root injury (RR 1.83), 30-day stroke (RR 1.27) and 30-day mortality (RR 1.25) but lower 1-year mortality (RR 0.84), with STS score — not bicuspid anatomy — predicting 1-year death. Bicuspid TAVR outcomes have improved over time, and mid-term survival appears driven by comorbidity rather than valve morphology.

— — Timing of non-culprit revascularisation in STEMI (meta-analysis)
Ali R et al. · Cardiovasc Revasc Med 2026 · source ↗

This meta-analysis of 5 randomised trials compared immediate, early-staged (same hospitalisation) and delayed-staged non-culprit revascularisation in STEMI with multivessel disease. Immediate complete PCI was not superior to early-staged revascularisation (RR 1.25; 95% CI 0.92–1.70), and hard-endpoint (death plus MI) analyses showed no significant timing differences. The optimal timing of complete revascularisation remains unsettled, with no clear advantage for immediate over in-hospital staged PCI.

— — Post-dilatation and complications in STEMI primary PCI
Bhatti KI et al. · Catheter Cardiovasc Interv 2026 · source ↗

This retrospective cohort of 12,608 primary-PCI STEMI patients compared those receiving noncompliant-balloon post-dilatation (62.9%) with those who did not. In matched analyses, post-dilatation was associated with lower in-hospital mortality (RR 0.952; 95% CI 0.939–0.966), cardiac arrest (RR 0.957; 95% CI 0.945–0.970) and cardiogenic shock (RR 0.987; 95% CI 0.979–0.996). Routine post-dilatation was linked to fewer complications, though residual confounding limits this observational signal.

SCOPE I — Sex-specific patient-centred outcomes after TAVR
Tomii D et al. · JACC Adv 2026 · source ↗

This post hoc SCOPE I analysis compared patient-centred outcomes by sex in 692 transfemoral TAVR patients. Women were less likely to reach KCCQ overall summary ≥75 at 1 year (64.4% vs 74.2%) but had lower 3-year mortality (19.5% vs 26.3%), yielding no significant sex difference in the composite of being alive with good health status at 3 years (42.7% vs 46.1%). Men and women achieve similar overall patient-centred benefit from TAVR despite divergent survival and symptom trajectories.

— — Sex differences in outcomes after M-TEER for severe MR
Sauter RJ et al. · JACC Adv 2026 · source ↗

This single-centre study examined sex differences in 293 patients undergoing mitral transcatheter edge-to-edge repair for severe MR (40.6% women). Six-month all-cause mortality did not differ between women and men (17% vs 22%; P=0.301; adjusted OR 0.93), despite men having more coronary disease and functional MR. Sex did not independently affect early survival after M-TEER.

OCCUPI — OCT-guided PCI in complex multivessel disease
Jin IT et al. · Heart 2026 · source ↗

This post hoc analysis of the OCCUPI trial evaluated OCT- versus angiography-guided PCI in 985 patients with multivessel disease and at least one complex lesion. OCT guidance roughly halved 1-year MACE (4.7% vs 8.9%; HR 0.51; 95% CI 0.31–0.85; P=0.008), and meeting the prespecified OCCUPI optimisation criteria was associated with lower MACE (3.1% vs 8.6%; P=0.011). OCT-guided stent optimisation extends its clinical benefit to anatomically complex multivessel PCI.

— — Intravascular-imaging-guided DES and cardiac death in diabetes
Zhao JY et al. · J Am Heart Assoc 2026 · source ↗

This prospective multicentre registry analysed 11,530 patients with diabetes undergoing drug-eluting-stent implantation, comparing intravascular-imaging (IVI)-guided (n=2,425) with angiography-guided PCI after 1:1 propensity matching (2,409 pairs). At 1 year, IVI guidance was associated with lower cardiac death (2.5% vs 4.3%; HR 0.59; 95% CI 0.43–0.80; P<0.001), confirmed on competing-risk analysis. Intravascular imaging was linked to larger, better-optimised stents and roughly a 40% relative reduction in cardiac death in this high-risk diabetic cohort.

— — IVUS- vs angiography-guided PCI in unprotected left main disease (meta-analysis)
Emara A et al. · Int J Cardiol 2026 · source ↗

This updated meta-analysis pooled 4 randomised trials (1,446 patients) comparing IVUS- versus angiography-guided PCI in unprotected left main coronary disease. MACE did not differ significantly overall (RR 0.57; 95% CI 0.30–1.09; P=0.09), though a leave-one-out sensitivity analysis excluding the OPTIMAL trial rendered the IVUS reduction statistically significant, with no differences in cardiac mortality (RR 0.68) or MI (RR 0.93). IVUS guidance shows a consistent direction of benefit in left-main PCI, but the randomised evidence stops short of definitive superiority.

— — MitraClip G4 vs PASCAL for functional MR (propensity-matched)
Ackermann P et al. · J Am Heart Assoc 2026 · source ↗

This single-centre study compared the two most-used M-TEER systems, MitraClip G4 (n=150) and PASCAL (n=169), in 319 patients with severe symptomatic functional MR, with 116 propensity-matched pairs. Procedural success (MR ≤2) was high and similar (98.1% vs 97.3%; P=0.61), with no significant differences in 1-year survival or freedom from heart-failure hospitalisation. Both contemporary edge-to-edge devices delivered comparable safety and efficacy in functional MR.

Landmark Revisited

MASTER DAPT — Dual antiplatelet therapy after PCI in patients at high bleeding risk
Valgimigli M et al. · N Engl J Med 2021 · primary source ↗ · Trial: NCT03023020 ↗
Structured abstract
Background
The appropriate duration of dual antiplatelet therapy (DAPT) after drug-eluting stent implantation in patients at high bleeding risk remained unclear.
Methods
One month after implantation of a biodegradable-polymer sirolimus-eluting stent, high-bleeding-risk patients were randomised to discontinue DAPT immediately (abbreviated therapy) or to continue it for at least 2 additional months (standard therapy). The three ranked primary outcomes — net adverse clinical events (NACE: death, MI, stroke, or major bleeding), major adverse cardiac or cerebral events (MACCE: death, MI, or stroke), and major or clinically relevant nonmajor bleeding — were assessed at 335 days; the first two for noninferiority, the third for superiority.
Key results
Among 4,434 per-protocol patients, NACE occurred in 165 (7.5%) with abbreviated vs 172 (7.7%) with standard therapy (difference −0.23 percentage points; 95% CI −1.80 to 1.33; P<0.001 for noninferiority). MACCE occurred in 133 (6.1%) vs 132 (5.9%) (difference 0.11 points; 95% CI −1.29 to 1.51; P=0.001 for noninferiority). In the intention-to-treat population (4,579 patients), major or clinically relevant nonmajor bleeding occurred in 148 (6.5%) vs 211 (9.4%) (difference −2.82 points; 95% CI −4.40 to −1.24; P<0.001 for superiority).
Conclusion
In high-bleeding-risk patients free of events at 1 month, abbreviated (1-month) DAPT was noninferior to prolonged DAPT for ischaemic events and reduced clinically relevant bleeding.
The question & the verdict

MASTER DAPT asked whether patients at high bleeding risk could safely stop DAPT at one month rather than persist for the guideline-favoured longer courses. The verdict — noninferior ischaemic outcomes with clearly less bleeding — made one month of DAPT the default in high-bleeding-risk PCI and reframed the field around "how short is safe" rather than "how long is enough." It is the direct forerunner of this week's minimalist antiplatelet strategies, from the SELUTION HBR drug-coated-balloon substudy to the DOAC-monotherapy TAVI and AF-plus-PCI antithrombotic data.

Statistical appraisal

The trial used a hierarchical, ranked testing of three primary outcomes — noninferiority for NACE and MACCE in the per-protocol population and superiority for bleeding in the intention-to-treat population — with cumulative incidences at 335 days. Randomisation only after an event-free first month enriched for lower-risk survivors, so the result applies to patients who tolerated a month of DAPT; the open-label design and operator discretion over stent choice (a single biodegradable-polymer sirolimus platform) bound generalisability to that device family. The bleeding benefit was consistent and the ischaemic curves tracked together, but subgroup certainty for the highest-risk anatomies came only later.

Subsequent substudies & follow-ups

· Oral-anticoagulation subgroup (Smits PC et al., Circulation 2021): rates of NACE and MACCE did not differ with abbreviated DAPT whether or not patients had an OAC indication, and bleeding was lower with abbreviated therapy in those without an OAC indication. source ↗

· Complex-PCI subgroup (Valgimigli M et al., Eur Heart J 2022): in event-free patients at 1 month, abbreviated DAPT gave similar NACE and MACCE and lower bleeding than standard DAPT regardless of PCI or patient complexity. source ↗

· Recent-MI subgroup (Smits PC et al., J Am Coll Cardiol 2022): a 1-month DAPT strategy in high-bleeding-risk patients with acute or recent MI produced similar NACE and MACCE and reduced bleeding versus non-abbreviated DAPT. source ↗

Why it still matters: MASTER DAPT anchored the modern principle that in high-bleeding-risk PCI, less antiplatelet therapy is usually safe — the evidentiary backbone for this issue's drug-coated-balloon, TAVI-antithrombotic and AF-plus-PCI strategies that all try to shorten or lighten the antithrombotic burden without paying an ischaemic price.
