— — Drug-Coated Balloons Versus Drug-Eluting Stents for De Novo Coronary Lesions: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis
Mondragon D et al. · Heart 2026 · primary source ↗
Structured abstract
Background
Drug-coated balloon (DCB) angioplasty has emerged as a potential stent-free strategy for de novo coronary artery disease, but robust randomised evidence against modern drug-eluting stents (DES) has remained limited.
Methods
Systematic review and meta-analysis of randomised controlled trials comparing DCB angioplasty with DES for de novo coronary lesions with ≥9 months of clinical follow-up, searched in PubMed, Embase, and Cochrane CENTRAL to October 2025; trials limited to in-stent restenosis, bifurcation PCI, or observational designs were excluded. Risk ratios (RR) were pooled with random-effects models (DerSimonian-Laird with Hartung-Knapp adjustment); risk of bias was appraised with Cochrane RoB 2.
Key results
7 trials encompassing 7,138 patients (DCB 3,570; DES 3,568) were included. At 12 months, the device-oriented composite endpoint (cardiac death, target-vessel MI, target-lesion revascularisation) showed no difference between strategies (RR 1.02, 95% CI 0.63–1.64; p=0.93; I²=52%). Secondary outcomes were comparable: cardiac death (RR 1.26, 0.75–2.14), target-vessel MI (RR 0.71, 0.36–1.39), target-lesion revascularisation (RR 1.09, 0.52–2.30), and target-vessel revascularisation (RR 0.98, 0.58–1.65). Trials using sirolimus-coated balloons showed similar results.
Conclusion
DCB angioplasty shows comparable short-term safety and efficacy to modern DES for de novo coronary disease; longer follow-up is needed to confirm whether avoidance of a permanent implant translates into clinical benefit.
Critical appraisal
Study design
A rigorous meta-analysis of 7 RCTs totalling 7,138 patients, using Hartung-Knapp-adjusted random-effects pooling and formal risk-of-bias appraisal — currently the broadest randomised evidence base comparing DCB against modern DES specifically in de novo disease.
Clinical relevance
Comparable 12-month device-oriented outcomes support DCB as a reasonable default strategy for de novo lesions, not merely a bail-out option for small-vessel or high-bleeding-risk disease.
Context
This issue's Landmark Revisited, BASKET-SMALL 2, is the single largest contributing RCT to this pooled evidence and remains the primary randomised anchor for "leave nothing behind" strategy in small-vessel disease specifically.
Limitations
Moderate heterogeneity on the primary endpoint (I²=52%); follow-up in the pooled trials is limited to 12 months, so any durability advantage from avoiding a permanent implant remains unconfirmed; trials pooled across different DCB technologies and DES generations.
Bottom line: DCB angioplasty achieves 12-month device-oriented outcomes indistinguishable from modern DES in de novo coronary disease, supporting broader "leave nothing behind" adoption pending longer-term confirmation of any durability edge.
NOTION-3 — Impact of Coronary Diameter Stenosis and Proximal Lesion Location on Outcomes Following PCI in Patients Undergoing TAVI
Riihiniemi M et al. · Circ Cardiovasc Interv 2026 · primary source ↗
Structured abstract
Background
Optimal treatment of coronary artery disease in patients undergoing transcatheter aortic valve implantation (TAVI) remains debated.
Methods
Prespecified substudy of NOTION-3, which randomized patients with severe symptomatic aortic stenosis and physiologically significant stable coronary artery disease to PCI or conservative treatment of coronary disease alongside TAVI. Patients were stratified by diameter stenosis (<90% vs ≥90%) and stenosis location (proximal vs nonproximal). Primary outcome: major adverse cardiovascular events (MACE), a composite of all-cause death, MI, and urgent revascularization.
Key results
Of 455 randomized patients, 184 (40%) had diameter stenosis <90% and 271 (60%) ≥90%; 294 (65%) had at least one proximal stenosis, 161 (35%) nonproximal only. At median follow-up of 2 years, PCI reduced absolute MACE risk by 13 points in patients with ≥90% stenosis (PCI 24% vs conservative 37%; HR 0.53, 95% CI 0.34–0.82) but showed no benefit for <90% stenosis (31% vs 33%; HR 1.04, 95% CI 0.62–1.73; P for interaction=0.051). PCI also reduced MACE for proximal stenosis (26% vs 38%; HR 0.61, 95% CI 0.40–0.93), though the difference versus nonproximal stenosis was not significant (28% vs 32%; HR 0.91, 95% CI 0.52–1.61; P for interaction=0.27).
Conclusion
The benefit of PCI in TAVI patients with physiologically significant stable coronary disease was seemingly pronounced in high-grade stenosis, while stenosis location did not significantly modify outcome.
Critical appraisal
Study design
A prespecified substudy of a randomized trial (NOTION-3 — Nordic Aortic Valve Intervention), stratifying the randomized comparison by anatomic severity and location after the fact.
Clinical relevance
Helps refine which TAVI patients with concomitant coronary disease actually benefit from PCI — high-grade (≥90%) stenosis appears to be the driver of NOTION-3's overall benefit, informing selection for combined TAVI-plus-PCI versus TAVI-alone strategies.
Context
Extends the "when to revascularize" question already refined for standalone chronic coronary syndrome by physiology-guided trials into the growing TAVI-with-CAD population.
Limitations
The interaction test for stenosis severity was borderline (P=0.051); the substudy was not powered for interaction testing and relies on post-randomization anatomic stratification with modest event counts in each subgroup.
Bottom line: In TAVI patients with concomitant coronary disease, the benefit of PCI appears concentrated in those with high-grade (≥90%) stenosis rather than proximal location — a signal that should sharpen, not broaden, criteria for combined revascularization at TAVI.
— — Implications of the 2026 Dyslipidemia Guideline for Primary Prevention Statin Therapy
Anderson TS et al. · JAMA 2026 · primary source ↗
Structured abstract
Background
The 2026 American Heart Association/American College of Cardiology/multisociety guideline on dyslipidemia management issued new recommendations on estimating ASCVD risk and on populations eligible for statins for primary prevention.
Methods
Nationally representative, cross-sectional sample of nonpregnant US adults aged 30–79 years without known ASCVD from NHANES 2017–2023, comparing eligibility for primary-prevention statin therapy under the 2026 versus 2018 lipid guidelines.
Key results
The weighted sample included 4,366 NHANES participants representing 154.5 million US adults (mean age 51 years, 52.0% female). 5.5% (95% CI 4.7%–6.6%) had untreated LDL-C below 70 mg/dL; 17.8% (16.3%–19.5%) reported currently taking statins; 8.6% (7.6%–9.8%) met statin-eligibility criteria independent of risk estimation (LDL-C ≥190, diabetes, or CKD); the remaining 68.0% (65.9%–70.0%) fell under risk-estimation-guided criteria. Overall, an estimated 87.5 million (56.6%, 95% CI 54.2%–58.9%) US adults aged 30–79 were statin-eligible under the 2026 guideline, including 21.5 million (13.9%, 95% CI 12.5%–15.5%) newly eligible. More than 93% of adults aged 70–79 and 85% aged 60–69 were eligible, versus 11% aged 30–39. Newly eligible individuals were younger and lower-risk (mean 10-year ASCVD risk 3.1%, 95% CI 2.7%–3.5%) than previously eligible individuals (6.1%, 95% CI 5.8%–6.4%).
Conclusion
The 2026 dyslipidemia guideline substantially expands the US population recommended for primary-prevention statin therapy, predominantly among lower-risk individuals.
Critical appraisal
Study design
A nationally representative NHANES cross-sectional analysis (4,366 participants representing 154.5 million adults) directly modeling guideline-eligibility criteria against population survey data.
Clinical relevance
A new guideline release is practice-changing by definition; this population-impact analysis shows the 2026 dyslipidemia guideline recalibrates primary-prevention eligibility upstream of every downstream interventional referral, with direct implications for secondary-prevention discussions after PCI or TAVR.
Context
Complements this issue's procedural evidence by quantifying the upstream medical-therapy population shift that precedes and follows interventional care.
Limitations
Cross-sectional NHANES design estimates guideline eligibility, not actual prescribing behaviour or downstream outcomes; survey sampling-weight extrapolation carries its own uncertainty; only 17.8% of the sample already reported taking statins, an implementation gap this analysis documents but does not resolve.
Bottom line: The 2026 dyslipidemia guideline newly qualifies an estimated 21.5 million lower-risk US adults for primary-prevention statin therapy — a shift clinicians managing post-PCI and post-TAVR secondary prevention should anticipate seeing walk through the door.
— — Effect of Sublingual Nitroglycerin on Absolute Coronary Blood Flow: A Randomized Double-Blind Placebo-Controlled Mechanistic Study
Mahendiran T et al. · JACC Cardiovasc Interv 2026 · source ↗

A randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled mechanistic study in 40 patients with suspected angina used continuous intracoronary thermodilution during cardiac catheterization to test sublingual nitroglycerin's (NTG) effect on absolute coronary blood flow, coronary resistance, and left ventricular work. At 10 minutes, NTG significantly lowered LV end-diastolic pressure (−5.4 mm Hg, a 38.1% decrease) and myocardial work (21.1% decrease), but paradoxically increased microvascular resistance (+8.0%, +121.5 Wood units) and decreased absolute coronary blood flow by 13.3% (−8.7 mL/min). This is the first convincing human demonstration that sublingual NTG's anti-anginal benefit works chiefly by cutting cardiac preload/afterload and myocardial oxygen demand rather than by boosting coronary flow — flow actually falls as an adaptive response to reduced cardiac work.

BIOVASC — Comparison of Immediate Versus Staged Complete Revascularization in Patients With Index PCI Coronary Complications
Gabrielli A et al. · Catheter Cardiovasc Interv 2026 · source ↗

This post hoc analysis of the 1,525-patient BIOVASC trial examined 1,487 index angiographies to determine whether unresolved culprit-lesion PCI complications (153 of 1,487 procedures, 10.3%, 125 unresolved) change the calculus between immediate (ICR) and staged (SCR) complete revascularization in ACS with multivessel disease. Among patients with unresolved complications, 2-year MACE was similar between ICR and SCR (10.6% vs 17.2%; HR 0.58, 95% CI 0.22–1.52), while complex lesion morphology (OR 1.68) and STEMI presentation (OR 1.45) independently predicted procedural complications. Even when a culprit-lesion complication is left unresolved at the index procedure, immediate complete revascularization remains a safe, viable strategy in hemodynamically stable patients rather than a reason to default to staging.

— — Outcomes Stratified by Adapted Inclusion Criteria After Transcatheter Tricuspid Edge-to-Edge Repair
Russo G et al. · Catheter Cardiovasc Interv 2026 · source ↗

Using the 664-patient TRIVALVE registry, this study tested how many real-world T-TEER patients would have met the TRILUMINATE and Tri.Fr pivotal trials' inclusion/exclusion criteria, and whether trial-eligibility predicted outcomes. Only about half of real-world patients met either trial's criteria (240/520, 46% TRILUMINATE-eligible; 260/519, 50% Tri.Fr-eligible), and trial-eligible patients had significantly lower mortality/rehospitalization than ineligible ones (TRILUMINATE: 12% vs 25%, p=0.028; Tri.Fr: 15% vs 22%, p=0.007). Roughly half of patients currently undergoing T-TEER in practice would not have qualified for the pivotal trials that established its evidence base, and those outside the trial criteria fare measurably worse — a real-world selection gap worth weighing before extending T-TEER to higher-risk anatomy.

— — Left Atrial Appendage Closure vs Anticoagulation for Stroke Prevention in Atrial Fibrillation: Meta-Analysis of Randomized Trials
Turagam MK et al. · JACC Clin Electrophysiol 2026 · source ↗

This meta-analysis pooled 7 randomized trials (n=7,353, mean CHA2DS2-VASc 3.9, mean follow-up 37.6 months, 294 stroke/systemic-embolism events) comparing percutaneous left atrial appendage closure (LAAC) against oral anticoagulation (OAC) for stroke prevention in atrial fibrillation. LAAC was similar to OAC for stroke/systemic embolism (RR 1.10, 95% CI 0.82–1.48) while reducing clinically significant bleeding (RR 0.60) and non-procedure-related clinically significant bleeding (RR 0.50), with a possibly more favorable bleeding profile in postablation patients. Across the largest randomized evidence base to date, LAAC prevents stroke at rates statistically indistinguishable from anticoagulation while meaningfully cutting bleeding, supporting individualized, patient-centered selection between the two strategies.

— — Long-Term Valve Durability and Clinical Outcomes After Transcatheter Versus Surgical Aortic Valve Replacement in Low-Risk Patients: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis
Ramamoorthy L et al. · J Thromb Thrombolysis 2026 · source ↗

This meta-analysis of 4 randomized trials (3,014 low-surgical-risk patients, 3–10 years of follow-up) compared long-term durability and clinical outcomes of TAVR versus SAVR. Cardiovascular mortality (HR 1.11) and stroke (HR 1.09) did not differ significantly; permanent pacemaker implantation was significantly more frequent after TAVR (RR 2.08), concentrated in self-expanding valves (RR 2.57 vs 1.40 for balloon-expandable, interaction p=0.02); aortic-valve reintervention was higher with TAVR by risk-ratio (RR 1.58) but not in the prespecified time-to-event analysis (HR 1.29). TAVR and SAVR remain equivalent on mortality and stroke through up to a decade of follow-up, but pacemaker and reintervention risk depend heavily on valve platform.

— — Temporal Trends and Predictors of Access Site Crossover During PCI in Contemporary Practice: A Report From the NCDR CathPCI Registry
Reynolds E et al. · Circ Cardiovasc Interv 2026 · source ↗

This NCDR CathPCI Registry analysis of 3,861,790 index PCI procedures (2018–2024) characterized contemporary vascular access-site crossover rates and their operator-level predictors. Crossover occurred in 192,902 procedures (5.0%), with radial-to-femoral crossover accounting for 81.2% of events; lowest-volume total- and radial-PCI operators showed the highest crossover rates, though rates declined over time for low-radial-volume operators. Contemporary crossover rates now mirror those seen in randomized radial-versus-femoral trials, and the operator-level learning-curve effect confirms structured radial experience — not case volume alone — drives crossover down.

— — Endotype-Guided Medical Therapy and Symptom Response in ANOCA
Farina J et al. · EuroIntervention 2026 · source ↗

In a 525-patient multicentre real-world registry, this study classified ANOCA patients into six endotypes via adenosine- and acetylcholine-based invasive coronary functional testing and tracked whether endotype-guided pharmacological therapy changed angina-related quality of life. Prescribing patterns differed meaningfully by endotype (more beta-blockers/ranolazine/RAAS blockers for microvascular dysfunction, more non-dihydropyridine calcium-channel blockers for vasospastic endotypes), and a clinically meaningful SAQ-7 improvement (≥5 points) was seen in elevated-flow, high-resistance, compensated-high-resistance, and epicardial-spasm endotypes (all p<0.001) but not in microvascular spasm or normal physiology. Endotyping ANOCA translates into genuinely different prescribing and differential symptom benefit, though two endotypes showed no measurable quality-of-life gain from endotype-guided therapy in this cohort — an unmet need worth flagging.

— — Treatment Effect of Lipid-Lowering Strategy Measured by Intracoronary NIRS-Derived Lipid Core Burden Index: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis
Saboe A et al. · Cardiovasc Revasc Med 2026 · source ↗

This systematic review and meta-analysis pooled 7 studies (920 lesions) reporting baseline and follow-up near-infrared spectroscopy (NIRS)-derived maximum lipid core burden index (maxLCBI4mm) to assess whether lipid-lowering therapy measurably shrinks coronary plaque lipid content. Lipid-lowering therapy was not associated with a statistically significant reduction in maxLCBI4mm (SMD −0.18, 95% CI −0.42 to 0.07, p=0.15, I²=60.5%), with no significant subgroup difference by follow-up duration or PCSK9-inhibitor use. Despite a numerical trend favoring more intensive lipid-lowering, this study-level meta-analysis could not confirm that NIRS-measurable plaque lipid content shrinks with therapy — a negative/inconclusive finding, not yet a validated imaging-based marker of treatment response.

— — Association Between Cardiovascular Risk Factors and Pan-Coronary Plaque Burden, Phenotype, and Vulnerability: A 3-Vessel Imaging Study
Chmiel J et al. · JACC Adv 2026 · source ↗

In 131 patients (534 plaques) undergoing 3-vessel optical coherence tomography imaging, this study examined how cardiovascular risk-factor burden relates to pan-coronary plaque number, phenotype, and vulnerability. As risk-factor count rose, plaque number and thin-cap fibroatheroma prevalence both increased (P trend=0.001), and in multivariable analysis only modifiable risk-factor burden was independently associated with pan-coronary vulnerability (IRR 1.36), including more thin-cap fibroatheromas (IRR 1.48), lipid-rich plaques (IRR 1.35), and cholesterol crystals (IRR 1.50). The vulnerability signal tracked specifically with modifiable risk factors, reinforcing that aggressive risk-factor modification — not fixed traits like age — is what reduces the pan-coronary vulnerable-plaque burden visible on imaging.

— — Intravascular Lithotripsy for Coronary Chronic Total Occlusion: A Meta-Analysis of Procedural and Clinical Outcomes
Dirjayanto VJ et al. · AsiaIntervention 2026 · source ↗

This meta-analysis of 5 studies (611 patients) pooled procedural and clinical outcomes of intravascular lithotripsy (IVL) used during chronic total occlusion (CTO) PCI. Technical success was achieved in 97% of cases and procedural success in 94% (significantly higher, 95% vs 91%, in lesions with a Japanese CTO score >2.8), with MACE in 3% (in-hospital) to 6% (discharge follow-up) and perforation in 5% of cases. IVL for calcified CTO lesions delivers high technical and procedural success with an acceptable complication profile, though the evidence base remains limited to 611 pooled patients.

— — Transcatheter Tricuspid Valve Annuloplasty vs Edge-to-Edge Repair: A Propensity-Matched Multicenter Comparative Analysis
von Stein J et al. · JACC Adv 2026 · source ↗

This propensity-matched, multicenter comparison of 1,122 patients (882 T-TEER, 240 transcatheter tricuspid valve annuloplasty [TTVA]) treated for severe secondary TR yielded 111 well-balanced matched pairs for procedural and 1-year clinical outcome comparison. T-TEER achieved higher rates of residual TR ≤1+ (50.0% vs 34.2%) and ≤2+ (87.7% vs 67.6%) with fewer procedural complications (4.5% vs 27.0%, p<0.001) than TTVA, while 1-year freedom from death/HF hospitalization was statistically similar (75.0% vs 79.8%, p=0.408). T-TEER delivers deeper, safer TR reduction than annuloplasty in this matched comparison, but the two strategies converge on 1-year clinical outcomes.

— — Early Versus Delayed Oral Anticoagulation Initiation After Transcatheter Aortic Valve Replacement in Patients With Atrial Fibrillation
Khawar M et al. · J Thromb Thrombolysis 2026 · source ↗

Using the TriNetX US network, this retrospective cohort study propensity-matched 3,372 patients per arm to compare early (within 2 days) versus delayed (5–7 days) oral anticoagulation initiation after transfemoral TAVR in patients with atrial fibrillation. Early OAC was associated with lower net adverse clinical events at 30 days (RR 0.754), 90 days (RR 0.780), and 1 year (RR 0.839), with corresponding reductions in all-cause mortality, major hemorrhage, and acute kidney injury at all time points. Starting anticoagulation within 2 days of TAVR rather than waiting a week was consistently associated with better outcomes in this large matched cohort, though the authors note prospective trials are needed before changing practice on retrospective data alone.

— — Cost-Effectiveness of Valve-in-Valve Transcatheter Aortic Valve Replacement Versus Redo-Surgical Aortic Valve Replacement for Degenerated Bioprosthetic Aortic Valve
Pliakos EE et al. · J Soc Cardiovasc Angiogr Interv 2026 · source ↗

This decision-analytic model compared the cost-effectiveness of valve-in-valve TAVR (ViV-TAVR) versus redo-surgical AVR for degenerated bioprosthetic aortic valves. ViV-TAVR was economically dominant, with lower mean cost ($88,829 vs $91,411) and a slightly higher 2-month survival probability (0.97 vs 0.96) than redo-SAVR, cost-effective in 57–58% of simulations across willingness-to-pay thresholds from $0 to $150,000. ViV-TAVR looks like the economically favorable strategy for degenerated bioprosthetic valves in this model, though the authors flag moderate decisional uncertainty and call for trial-level comparative data to confirm it.

— — Impact of Ventricular Stroke Work on Outcomes After Tricuspid Transcatheter Edge-to-Edge Repair
Hanses U et al. · Can J Cardiol 2026 · source ↗

In 144 patients undergoing T-TEER for severe symptomatic TR, this study used invasive right-heart-catheterization hemodynamics to stratify patients by left and right ventricular stroke work indices (LVSWI, RVSWI) and test their association with 1-year mortality. Overall 1-year mortality was 28%, but varied sharply by subgroup — 15% with high LVSWI (regardless of RVSWI), 31% with low LVSWI/low RVSWI, and 55% with low LVSWI but high RVSWI, the worst-surviving group (log-rank p=0.00027). A simple invasive hemodynamic profile obtained at T-TEER — low left- with high right-ventricular stroke work — flags a subgroup with more than triple the mortality risk of the best-performing group, a signal that could refine patient selection beyond echocardiographic TR grade alone.

— — Risk, Management, and Outcomes in PCI-Treated Acute Coronary Syndrome With Cardiogenic Shock
Ohata T et al. · JACC Adv 2026 · source ↗

This Japanese multicenter registry (24,527 PCI procedures 2009–2019, 870 with ACS complicated by cardiogenic shock) tracked a decade of trends in risk profile, mechanical circulatory support (MCS) use, and outcomes in PCI-treated ACS-cardiogenic shock. Overall MCS use declined from 66.5% to 56.7% (driven largely by falling intra-aortic balloon pump use, 46.5% to 31.2%), yet crude and risk-adjusted in-hospital mortality remained persistently around 30% across all four time periods with no significant improvement. Despite a decade of evolving mechanical-support strategy, in-hospital mortality for PCI-treated ACS-cardiogenic shock has not budged in this large registry — a reminder that changing which device is used has not, on its own, moved the outcome needle.

— — Functional Status and Health-Related Quality of Life Among Survivors of Cardiogenic Shock
Hall EJ et al. · JACC Heart Fail 2026 · source ↗

This prospective 2-hospital cohort study enrolled 141 SCAI Stage C-or-greater cardiogenic shock survivors (118 completed 3-month follow-up; 36% received temporary MCS) to evaluate functional disability and health-related quality of life (HRQoL) beyond hospital discharge. Functional disability (modified Rankin Scale ≥2) was present in 66% at discharge and 46% at 3 months, with pain (67%), mobility limitations (48%), and anxiety/depression (35%) the most common problems, and median EQ-5D index (0.84) fell below the age-matched US population norm (0.94), correlating inversely with functional status (r=−0.68, p<0.001). Nearly half of cardiogenic shock survivors remain functionally disabled at 3 months with below-normal quality of life, underscoring that survival to discharge is an incomplete metric of success.

SXS-CTO — STENTYS Xposition S in the Treatment of Chronic Total Artery Occlusion
Boukantar M et al. · J Invasive Cardiol 2026 · source ↗

This single-center, prospective, randomized pilot trial allocated 23 CTO-PCI patients 1:1 to a self-expanding drug-eluting stent (SE-DES, STENTYS Xposition S) or a balloon-expandable DES (BE-DES), assessing 6-month strut malapposition by optical coherence tomography. SE-DES showed significantly lower malapposition than BE-DES (0.0% vs 0.8%, p=0.01) but greater neointimal thickness (0.3 vs 0.1 mm, p<0.001) and late lumen loss (0.7 vs 0.1 mm, p=0.003), with numerically more in-stent restenosis (4 vs 1 patients, not significant). Self-expanding stents adapt better to the positive remodeling seen after CTO recanalization, but this small trial's own OCT findings caution that eliminating malapposition may trade off against a different restenosis mechanism.

— — The Sequence of Post-Dilation Matters: Proximal Optimization Technique (POT) Reduces Longitudinal Stent Deformation Assessed by Intravascular Ultrasound
Bo-Xu D et al. · Catheter Cardiovasc Interv 2026 · source ↗

In 529 IVUS-guided PCI patients (533 vessels), this study examined which procedural factors predict longitudinal stent deformation (LSD) after post-dilation, focusing on dilation sequence. A distal-to-proximal dilation sequence was independently associated with stent elongation (OR 3.13) as was higher LVEF (OR 1.07), while stent placement at the vessel ostium was protective (OR 0.45). Starting post-dilation from the proximal stent segment — the proximal optimization technique — appears to meaningfully lower the risk of longitudinal stent deformation, a simple sequencing change operators can apply without new equipment.

— — Haemodynamic Predictors of Lesion Progression in Non-Ischaemic Tandem Coronary Stenoses
Zuin M et al. · AsiaIntervention 2026 · source ↗

In 60 propensity-matched patients with non-ischaemic (FFR-CT ≥0.80) tandem LAD stenoses, this study tested whether computational-fluid-dynamics (CFD)-derived wall shear stress metrics predicted 12-month lesion progression beyond standard CT-FFR. Patients with lesion progression (n=30) had significantly lower baseline time-averaged wall shear stress (0.32 vs 0.82 Pa, p<0.001), higher oscillatory shear index (0.22 vs 0.05, p<0.001), and longer relative residence time (1.9 vs 0.7 s, p<0.001) than those with stable lesions. Tandem coronary lesions that look hemodynamically benign by CT-FFR alone can still be primed to progress, and CFD-derived shear-stress metrics may catch risk that pressure-based indices miss.

— — Percutaneous Coronary Interventions Improve Phasic Cardiac-Coronary Interaction in Chronic Coronary Syndromes as Assessed by Wave Intensity Analysis
Taş A et al. · Open Heart 2026 · source ↗

In 27 chronic coronary syndrome patients undergoing elective PCI, this mechanistic study used intracoronary Doppler/pressure wave-intensity analysis to characterize how PCI changes cardiac-coronary coupling at rest and during hyperaemia. PCI significantly improved FFR (0.58 to 0.87), coronary flow reserve (1.68 to 2.79), and lowered hyperaemic microvascular resistance (2.22 to 1.49, all p<0.001), with resting wave speed decreasing (41 to 25 m/s, p<0.001) correlating with microvascular resistance improvement (r=0.77). Beyond simply relieving an epicardial stenosis, PCI measurably restores the phasic mechanical coupling between the heart and its coronary circulation.

— — An Evaluation of the Use of Fibrinolytic Therapy for Treatment of STEMI in Northern New England Over Two Decades
Rothstein E et al. · J Soc Cardiovasc Angiogr Interv 2026 · source ↗

This 20-year retrospective analysis from the Dartmouth-Health regional STEMI network compared clinical characteristics and outcomes of rural pharmacoinvasive (fibrinolysis-first) STEMI patients between a premodern (2001–2012) and modern (2013–2020) era. Despite rising cardiovascular comorbidity burden nationally, comorbidities in this rural cohort stayed stable over time, and adjusted in-hospital MACE (7.2% vs 6.9%, p=0.76) and bleeding (8.0% vs 7.0%, p=0.4) were statistically unchanged between eras. Unlike the steady improvement seen in urban STEMI outcomes over the same two decades, rural patients managed with a pharmacoinvasive strategy have seen no measurable gain in in-hospital outcomes.

— — Cost-Effectiveness of Supersaturated Oxygen Delivery for Infarct Size Reduction in Patients With Anterior ST-Segment Elevation Myocardial Infarction
Vilain K et al. · J Soc Cardiovasc Angiogr Interv 2026 · source ↗

Using a Markov cost-effectiveness model calibrated to Medicare data, this study evaluated whether supersaturated oxygen (SSO2) delivery after primary PCI for anterior STEMI — previously shown to reduce infarct size — is cost-effective for the US health system. SSO2 was projected to increase discounted life expectancy (14.21 vs 14.12 years) and quality-adjusted life expectancy (10.78 vs 10.69 QALYs) at an incremental cost of $3,205/patient, yielding an ICER of $38,415/QALY with a 73% probability of good economic value at a $120,000/QALY threshold. At its modeled cost of roughly $10,528 per use, SSO2 therapy for anterior STEMI clears standard US cost-effectiveness thresholds, adding an economic argument alongside its infarct-size benefit.

— — Beyond Tunnel Length: Tunnel Width-Derived Cover Index Improves Device Sizing and Predicts Outcomes After Transcatheter Patent Foramen Ovale Closure
Piech TD et al. · J Soc Cardiovasc Angiogr Interv 2026 · source ↗

In 102 patients undergoing transcatheter PFO closure (82 with follow-up bubble study), this study used 3D transesophageal echocardiography to test whether tunnel-width-derived metrics predict residual shunting and device-sizing needs better than conventional tunnel length. A tunnel-width-derived cover index predicted residual shunting with high discrimination (AUC 0.88) and a cutoff of ≤9.5 mm gave 90% sensitivity/78% specificity, while tunnel length itself did not correlate with tunnel width or predict either outcome. Despite its widespread use, tunnel length is the wrong metric for PFO device sizing — tunnel-width-based indices reliably predict residual shunting or the need for a larger device.

— — Combined Direct Current Cardioversion and Left Atrial Appendage Occlusion in Patients With Contraindication to Anticoagulation: A Single-Center Feasibility Study
Chen L et al. · J Soc Cardiovasc Angiogr Interv 2026 · source ↗

This single-center feasibility study performed cardioversion (DCCV) immediately followed by percutaneous LAAO (Watchman FLX/FLX Pro) in 12 AF patients with contraindications to post-cardioversion anticoagulation (67% with a bleeding history; mean HAS-BLED 2.1). Acute cardioversion succeeded in 11/12 patients (92%) and LAAO technical success was 100%, with no deaths, strokes, or major bleeding through 3 months and only one low-risk device-related thrombus (9%). Combining cardioversion and appendage closure into one procedure appears feasible with an acceptable early safety profile in this small pilot series, an OAC-sparing option worth testing in a larger prospective trial.

— — Directional Radial Force Alignment Predicts Pacemaker Implantation After Balloon-Expandable Transcatheter Aortic Valve Implantation
Dogan M et al. · Am J Cardiol 2026 · source ↗

In 67 patients undergoing balloon-expandable (Myval) TAVI, this retrospective study tested a novel CT-derived biomechanical parameter — the "Vectorial Angle," measuring deviation between the valve's axis of maximal radial force and the conduction system's anatomical course — as a predictor of permanent pacemaker (PPM) need. PPM was required in 12 patients (17.9%); a narrower Vectorial Angle (OR 0.935 per degree) and shorter membranous septal length (OR 0.447 per mm) independently predicted PPM, with the Vectorial Angle achieving an AUC of 0.806 (optimal cutoff ≤52°). How the valve's expansion force is spatially oriented relative to the conduction system — not just conventional depth or annular measurements — may be a previously unrecognized anatomical driver of pacemaker risk after balloon-expandable TAVI.

— — Safety and Efficacy of Transcatheter Aortic Valve Implantation at Centres With Versus Without On-Site Cardiac Surgery: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis
Elettreby AM et al. · Heart Lung Circ 2026 · source ↗

This systematic review and meta-analysis of 6 observational cohort studies (6,109 patients) compared TAVI outcomes at centres with versus without on-site cardiac surgery backup. No statistically significant differences emerged for in-hospital mortality (RR 0.94), 30-day mortality (RR 0.87), or emergent conversion to open surgery (RR 0.78), though in one included matched-registry study, mortality after emergent surgical conversion was high regardless of setting (50% vs 62.5%). This meta-analysis found no significant safety signal against performing TAVI without on-site surgical backup, but the authors stress that low-to-very-low evidence certainty means the absence of a significant difference should not be read as proven equivalence.

OPTIMUM — Preprocedural New York Heart Association Class IV Independently Predicts Mortality After Effective Edge-to-Edge Repair for Primary Mitral Regurgitation
Koell B et al. · Struct Heart 2026 · source ↗

Using the international OPTIMUM registry (27 sites, 2009–2023, 2,528 primary MR patients treated with M-TEER, 19.7% presenting in NYHA class IV), this study tested whether preprocedural NYHA IV status still predicts mortality once mitral regurgitation has been effectively reduced. Procedural success (residual MR ≤1+: 66.7% vs 64.6%) and 2-year rehospitalization (17.9% vs 20.4%) were comparable between NYHA IV and NYHA II/III patients, yet 2-year all-cause mortality was significantly higher in NYHA IV (31.8% vs 21.1%, log-rank p<0.0001; adjusted HR 1.75). Even when M-TEER achieves an equally good procedural result, presenting in NYHA class IV carries an independently higher mortality risk — a reminder that earlier referral may matter as much as procedural success itself.

— — Percutaneous Embolectomy vs Medical Therapy for Acute Pulmonary Embolism in Patients With Cancer
Ang SP et al. · Cardiovasc Revasc Med 2026 · source ↗

This National Inpatient Sample analysis (2016–2021, 476,915 weighted hospitalizations) compared percutaneous embolectomy against medical therapy alone in cancer patients hospitalized with acute pulmonary embolism, using 1:3 propensity-score matching (n=11,040 matched). Embolectomy use rose from 0.16% to 1.35% of cases despite being performed in patients with much higher baseline acuity (PE-related shock 46.6% vs 5.2%), yet after matching, in-hospital mortality (OR 0.87) and major bleeding (OR 0.95) did not differ significantly from medical therapy, though acute kidney injury was more common (OR 1.36). Percutaneous embolectomy is being used increasingly in the sickest cancer-associated PE patients without an apparent survival or bleeding penalty relative to medical therapy.

— — Pulmonary Embolism in the United States: A Report From the National PERT Consortium
Keeling WB et al. · J Soc Cardiovasc Angiogr Interv 2026 · source ↗

This National PERT Consortium registry report characterized real-world pulmonary embolism care across 54 US sites from October 2015 to June 2024, covering 11,612 consecutively diagnosed patients. Most patients (73.7%) were intermediate risk, 1 in 3 had a prior DVT and 1 in 4 a malignancy history, and 30-day/1-year survival after catheter-based intervention was 95.1%/92.3% versus 67.8% 1-year survival for high-risk PE overall. This is the largest dedicated real-world snapshot of contemporary US PE care to date, and the wide survival gap between risk strata underscores the continued need for earlier recognition and escalation in high-risk PE.

Landmark Revisited

BASKET-SMALL 2 — Drug-Coated Balloons for Small Coronary Artery Disease: An Open-Label Randomised Non-Inferiority Trial
Jeger RV et al. (BASKET-SMALL 2 Investigators) · Lancet 2018;392(10150):849–856 · PMID 30170854 ↗ · NCT01574534
Structured abstract
Background
Drug-coated balloons (DCB) were a novel therapeutic strategy for small native coronary artery disease, but their safety and efficacy against drug-eluting stents (DES) was poorly defined.
Methods
Multicentre, open-label, randomised non-inferiority trial across 14 sites in Germany, Switzerland, and Austria. 758 patients with de-novo lesions <3 mm in diameter and an indication for PCI were randomly allocated 1:1 to angioplasty with DCB versus implantation of a second-generation DES after successful predilatation, via an interactive internet-based response system. The primary objective was non-inferiority of DCB versus DES for major adverse cardiac events (MACE: cardiac death, non-fatal MI, target-vessel revascularisation) at 12 months, with a non-inferiority margin of 4% absolute difference.
Key results
Between April 10, 2012, and February 1, 2017, 382 patients were assigned to DCB and 376 to DES. Non-inferiority was shown (95% CI of the absolute difference in MACE, −3.83% to 3.93%; p=0.0217). At 12 months, MACE occurred in 7.5% of the DCB group versus 7.3% of the DES group (HR 0.97, 95% CI 0.58–1.64, p=0.9180). There were 12 (3.1%) cardiac-related deaths in the DCB group versus 5 (1.3%) in the DES group. Probable or definite stent thrombosis occurred in 3 (0.8%) DCB versus 4 (1.1%) DES patients (HR 0.73), and major bleeding in 4 (1.1%) DCB versus 9 (2.4%) DES patients (HR 0.45).
Conclusion
In small native coronary artery disease, DCB was non-inferior to DES regarding MACE up to 12 months, with similar event rates for both treatment groups.
The question & the verdict

By the early 2010s, drug-eluting stents had become the default treatment even for small coronary vessels, despite small-vessel disease carrying disproportionately high restenosis risk and despite growing clinical interest in "leave nothing behind" strategies that avoid a permanent metallic implant. Drug-coated balloons had shown promise for in-stent restenosis but lacked robust randomised evidence as a primary strategy for de-novo small-vessel disease. BASKET-SMALL 2 asked whether treating de-novo small coronary lesions with DCB angioplasty alone could match the clinical outcomes of modern second-generation DES — and answered yes on its pre-specified non-inferiority margin, with near-identical 12-month MACE (7.5% vs 7.3%), even as a numerically higher cardiac-death count in the DCB arm (12 vs 5) left an open question for longer follow-up to resolve.

Statistical appraisal

BASKET-SMALL 2 was adequately powered for its pre-specified non-inferiority hypothesis (758 patients, 4% absolute margin) and used an interactive internet-based randomisation system across 14 sites, with concordant full-analysis-set and per-protocol results. The open-label design (blinding is largely impractical for a balloon-vs-stent comparison) could theoretically bias softer endpoints, though the MACE components — death, MI, revascularisation — are comparatively hard endpoints resistant to unblinding bias. The numerically higher cardiac-death count in the DCB arm did not reach statistical significance and must be interpreted cautiously given the trial's modest size and event count — a genuine open question the 3-year follow-up below was designed to help resolve. Generalisability is bounded to de-novo lesions <3 mm treated after successful predilatation, compared against second-generation everolimus-eluting stents specifically.

Subsequent substudies & follow-ups

3-year follow-up (Jeger RV et al., Lancet 2020; PMID 33091360 ↗) — MACE remained statistically identical at 3 years (Kaplan-Meier estimate 15% in both groups, HR 0.99, 95% CI 0.68–1.45, p=0.95), with numerically lower, non-significant stent thrombosis (1% vs 2%) and major bleeding (2% vs 4%) favouring DCB — confirming durable, not just short-term, non-inferiority.

Diabetes subgroup (Wöhrle J et al., JACC Cardiovasc Interv 2021; PMID 34412797 ↗) — in 252 diabetic patients, DCB significantly lowered 3-year target-vessel revascularisation versus DES (9.1% vs 15.0%, HR 0.40, p=0.036; p=0.011 for interaction), while MACE was similar between DCB and DES in both diabetics and non-diabetics — suggesting DCB may be preferentially advantageous in diabetic small-vessel disease.

Chronic kidney disease subgroup (Mahfoud F et al., Clin Res Cardiol 2022; PMID 35220449 ↗) — in 174 patients with CKD (eGFR <60), 3-year MACE was unaffected by CKD status or treatment strategy (HR 0.98 DCB vs DES), but DCB carried significantly fewer major bleeding events than DES regardless of renal function (HR 0.26, p=0.037).

Why it still matters: BASKET-SMALL 2 remains the single largest randomised trial anchoring "leave nothing behind" strategy in small-vessel coronary disease, and it is the largest individual contributor to this issue's own DCB-versus-DES meta-analysis. Its diabetes and chronic-kidney-disease substudies show the strategy's benefit may concentrate in comorbidity-defined subgroups — exactly the kind of patient-selection question this issue's imaging and physiology items are still trying to answer prospectively for de novo disease more broadly.
