Practice-changing

STORM-PE — Mechanical thrombectomy vs anticoagulation alone in intermediate-high-risk pulmonary embolism
Rosovsky RP et al. · JACC Cardiovasc Interv 2026 · primary source ↗
Structured abstract
Background
Up to half of pulmonary embolism (PE) survivors have lasting functional limitation, yet catheter reperfusion had never been tested against anticoagulation alone using patient-centred outcomes.
Methods
100 patients with acute intermediate-high-risk PE at 22 international sites were randomised 1:1 to computer-assisted vacuum thrombectomy (CAVT) plus anticoagulation or anticoagulation alone; the primary endpoint was the 48-hour change in right-to-left ventricular (RV/LV) ratio, with functional, quality-of-life and safety endpoints through 90 days.
Key results
At 90 days the CAVT arm walked farther on the 6-minute walk test (478.7 ± 159.5 m vs 368.0 ± 127.8 m, P=0.003) and more were NYHA class I (97.4% [37/38] vs 75.6% [34/45], P=0.005); dyspnoea and quality-of-life scores were comparable, and there was no difference in all-cause or PE-related mortality or symptomatic PE recurrence at 90 days.
Conclusion
CAVT plus anticoagulation improved 90-day functional outcomes versus anticoagulation alone with equivalent safety.
Critical appraisal
Study design
Multinational, randomised, open-label trial — the first PE reperfusion RCT built around patient-centred recovery rather than only the surrogate RV/LV ratio (which was the formal primary endpoint).
Clinical relevance
Addresses the central open question in intermediate-high-risk PE: whether early clot removal translates into how patients actually feel and function months later.
Context
Prior catheter-based PE studies (e.g. single-arm device registries) reported acute RV unloading but left long-term functional benefit unproven; guideline reperfusion remains reserved for haemodynamic deterioration.
Limitations
Small sample, open-label design, only about two-thirds completed the 6-minute walk test, the functional endpoints are secondary/exploratory, and there was no mortality or recurrence difference.
Bottom line: In carefully selected intermediate-high-risk PE, upfront vacuum thrombectomy can speed functional recovery without a safety penalty — but hard-outcome benefit remains unproven, so patient selection still matters.
PREVENT — Lipid burden and the efficacy of preventive PCI of vulnerable plaques
Wee SB et al. · Circulation 2026 · primary source ↗
Structured abstract
Background
Preventive PCI of non-flow-limiting high-risk plaques (FFR >0.80) can reduce events, but which lesions actually benefit is unclear; this post hoc tested the near-infrared spectroscopy (NIRS) lipid core burden index.
Methods
Among 1,606 PREVENT patients, this analysis included 598 patients with 632 lesions (plaque burden >70%, minimal lumen area <4 mm² by IVUS) who also underwent NIRS; lipid-rich plaque (LRP) was prespecified as a maximum 4-mm lipid core burden index >315, with a composite primary endpoint of cardiac death, target-vessel MI, ischaemia-driven target-vessel revascularisation, or hospitalisation for unstable/progressive angina.
Key results
LRPs were present in 223 patients (37.3%); over a median 5.6 years the primary outcome was more frequent with LRP than non-LRP (12.5% vs 4.7%; unadjusted HR 2.08, 95% CI 1.03–4.19). Within LRP lesions, preventive PCI lowered the primary outcome versus optimal medical therapy alone (7.3% vs 17.6%; adjusted HR 0.23, 95% CI 0.13–0.41), with no benefit in non-LRP lesions (5.5% vs 4.2%; adjusted HR 1.00) and a significant treatment-by-LRP interaction (P<0.001).
Conclusion
The benefit of preventive PCI concentrated in NIRS-defined lipid-rich plaques, pointing to plaque composition — not just size — as the discriminator.
Critical appraisal
Study design
Post hoc, non-randomised subgrouping within a positive open-label RCT, restricted to lesions with NIRS imaging.
Clinical relevance
If validated, NIRS lipid mapping could select the minority of vulnerable plaques worth sealing and spare the rest from a stent.
Context
Builds on PREVENT's overall positive result and on earlier natural-history work linking high lipid core burden to future events; the analysis here also shows LRP lesions carried the higher event rate (12.5% vs 4.7%) before treatment effects are considered.
Limitations
Subgroup analysis with residual confounding, modest event counts, and composite endpoints weighted toward revascularisation/angina rather than death or MI alone.
Bottom line: Reserve preventive PCI of FFR-negative high-risk plaques for lesions that are lipid-rich on NIRS; morphological severity alone does not justify a stent — pending prospective confirmation.
SELUTION DeNovo HBR — Sirolimus-eluting balloons for PCI in high bleeding risk
Rissanen TT et al. · EuroIntervention 2026 · primary source ↗
Structured abstract
Background
Drug-eluting stents (DES) are the default for high-bleeding-risk (HBR) PCI, but drug-coated balloons may reduce reliance on prolonged antiplatelet therapy.
Methods
SELUTION DeNovo randomised 3,323 PCI candidates 1:1 to a sirolimus-eluting balloon (SEB) strategy with provisional stenting or systematic DES; this prespecified subgroup comprised 522 patients meeting ARC-HBR criteria (269 SEB, 253 DES), with 1-year target-vessel failure (TVF), BARC 3–5 bleeding and net adverse clinical events (NACE).
Key results
Bailout stenting occurred in 17.1% of the SEB arm and 67% of patients were discharged on oral anticoagulants; at 1 year TVF was 4.9% vs 7.9% (absolute risk difference −3.05%, 95% CI −7.27 to 1.17), BARC 3–5 bleeding 2.3% vs 3.2%, and NACE 6.8% vs 10.7% — and far more SEB patients had stopped all antiplatelets by 6 months (27.8% vs 14.9%, p<0.001).
Conclusion
Although underpowered, the data suggest an SEB-with-minimal-stenting strategy is a safe, effective alternative to systematic DES in HBR patients.
Critical appraisal
Study design
Prespecified subgroup of a large randomised trial — hypothesis-strengthening but not powered for definitive conclusions (all confidence intervals cross zero).
Clinical relevance
The clinically meaningful signal is earlier, more complete antiplatelet withdrawal without a TVF penalty — directly relevant to the HBR patient many operators worry about.
Context
Extends the de-novo DCB evidence base into the HBR population, where reducing reliance on prolonged antiplatelet therapy is the central appeal.
Limitations
Small subset, open-label, provisional stenting in one in six, and no hard-endpoint superiority demonstrated.
Bottom line: In high-bleeding-risk de-novo lesions, a sirolimus-eluting-balloon-first strategy is a reasonable, stent-sparing option that enables earlier antiplatelet withdrawal — adequately powered confirmation is still needed.
— — Intravascular imaging guidance in complex PCI: a meta-analysis across lesion subsets
Basile M et al. · Int J Cardiol 2026 · source ↗

This meta-analysis pooled 15 randomised trials (11,557 patients) comparing intravascular imaging (IVI)- with angiography-guided complex PCI. IVI reduced target-vessel failure (RR 0.70, 95% CI 0.56–0.86; number needed to treat 26), target-vessel and target-lesion revascularisation (RR 0.68 and 0.67) and stent thrombosis (RR 0.42), with a borderline 26% lower cardiovascular mortality (RR 0.74, 95% CI 0.54–1.00) and no all-cause mortality reduction. Effects were similar for IVUS and OCT (interaction p=0.93), and lesion-subset estimates were directionally consistent but reached significance only after excluding the IVUS-CHIP trial.

DanGer Shock — Coronary disease complexity in infarct-related shock (substudy)
Marquard JM et al. · EuroIntervention 2026 · source ↗

This secondary analysis of the DanGer Shock trial examined baseline and residual SYNTAX scores in 355 patients (256 with multivessel disease, 211 with a baseline SYNTAX score) and the effect of microaxial flow-pump (mAFP) support in infarct-related cardiogenic shock. A high baseline SYNTAX score (>32) carried higher 180-day all-cause mortality (adjusted OR 3.12, 95% CI 1.17–8.69), residual SYNTAX independently predicted mortality, and the mAFP effect was not modified by SYNTAX group (p for interaction=0.31). In the small isolated left-main-culprit subgroup (n=35), mAFP use was associated with lower mortality (adjusted OR 0.17, 95% CI 0.03–0.99) — a hypothesis-generating signal, not a treatment-selection rule.

— — Long-term outcomes of iFR- vs FFR-guided physiology in real-world practice
Ferreira D et al. · Catheter Cardiovasc Interv 2026 · source ↗

This single-centre retrospective study compared 1,104 patients (428 FFR, 676 iFR) undergoing invasive coronary physiology between 2012 and 2022. iFR-guided management was associated with higher long-term all-cause mortality (HR 1.52, 95% CI 1.19–1.93) and MACE (HR 1.47, 95% CI 1.19–1.82), driven by mortality, with no difference in MI or unplanned revascularisation; fewer lesions were deemed significant with iFR (42% vs 49%), meaning more deferrals. The findings are observational and hypothesis-generating, but they parallel the 5-year randomised signal revisited in this issue's Landmark feature.

Multivessel TALENT — Standardised vs visual assessment of device success after PCI
Oshima A et al. · EuroIntervention 2026 · source ↗

A prespecified lesion-level substudy of the Multivessel TALENT trial (1,548 three-vessel-disease patients, 4,052 lesions) compared operator visual device success against the EAPCI-endorsed standardised definition adjudicated by a core lab. Site-reported success of 97.4% fell to 88.0% by core-lab quantitative angiography (residual %DS <20%) and 83.2% by combined angiography plus angio-FFR (all p<0.001), and lesions failing the QCA-based definition had more 1-year lesion-oriented events (12.0% vs 9.1%, p=0.041). Objective post-PCI criteria reclassify a substantial share of "successful" procedures and flag higher-risk lesions.

— — Intravascular lithotripsy in left main stem PCI
Cosgrove CS et al. · Catheter Cardiovasc Interv 2026 · source ↗

This prospective, multicentre UK study (NCT04319666) treated 50 patients with calcified distal left main disease and IVUS-confirmed calcium arc ≥270° using intravascular lithotripsy (IVL) before stenting. Device crossing succeeded in all 50; mean minimum stent area was 13.36 ± 3.20 mm² with 84% exceeding 11 mm², and there were no periprocedural deaths or MIs and no 30-day MACE, with 8.0% MACE and no stent thrombosis at 12 months. IVL achieved prognostically validated stent expansion in left main PCI, though IVUS-visible calcium fracture did not itself predict expansion.

MyPro — Early outcomes of the Octapro balloon-expandable TAVI valve
Ielasi A et al. · Int J Cardiol 2026 · source ↗

This real-world registry enrolled 303 patients with severe aortic stenosis undergoing TAVI with the novel balloon-expandable Octapro valve at European and Indian centres. VARC-3 technical success was 97%, 30-day device success 92.4%, mean gradient 9 ± 4 mmHg and moderate-to-severe paravalvular leak 3.3%, with 30-day all-cause mortality 2.6% and a guideline-indicated pacemaker rate of 14.2%. Implantation depth (threshold 4.75 mm) and baseline right bundle branch block independently predicted pacemaker need, underscoring implantation technique.

ASCoP — Timing of high-risk PCI relative to TAVI
Munafò AR et al. · Int J Cardiol 2026 · source ↗

This observational multicentre registry grouped 519 patients needing both TAVI and high-risk PCI by timing: concomitant (30.1%), before TAVI (64.1%) or after TAVI (5.8%). Staging PCI after TAVI was associated with a higher rate of death or unplanned hospitalisation at 1 year (HR 2.31, 95% CI 1.24–4.32), while PCI before TAVI carried lower MACCE (HR 0.55, 95% CI 0.35–0.85), consistent after inverse-probability weighting. The data argue against routinely deferring high-risk coronary work until after the valve.

— — Routine vs deferred PCI in TAVI: a frequentist and Bayesian meta-analysis
Ibrahim A et al. · Int J Cardiol Heart Vasc 2026 · source ↗

This meta-analysis pooled 3 randomised trials (1,156 patients) comparing routine versus deferred PCI in TAVI patients with significant coronary disease. Routine PCI showed no convincing benefit for spontaneous MI (posterior median RR 0.84; 70% probability of benefit) or mortality (RR 0.91), but reduced urgent revascularisation (RR 0.37; 98% probability of benefit) at the cost of more major bleeding (RR 1.56; 96% probability of harm) and life-threatening bleeding (RR 1.74). A selective rather than routine revascularisation strategy appears preferable.

— — Ticagrelor monotherapy vs aspirin plus prasugrel after PCI
Maremmani M et al. · JACC Adv 2026 · source ↗

This frequentist network meta-analysis of 7 randomised trials (27,225 post-PCI patients, 98.6% with ACS) compared de-escalation to ticagrelor monotherapy against 12-month aspirin-plus-prasugrel. Ticagrelor monotherapy had similar MACCE (IRR 1.09, 95% CI 0.86–1.38; non-inferiority not met, p=0.33) but lower major bleeding (IRR 0.54, 95% CI 0.37–0.80), with a diabetes-by-strategy interaction for MACCE (p=0.04). The analysis frames the live ACC-versus-ESC guideline divergence but, lacking head-to-head data, cannot settle it.

— — Mid-term outcomes of Tendyne transcatheter mitral valve replacement
Puluca N et al. · J Thorac Cardiovasc Surg 2026 · source ↗

This prospective single-centre cohort followed the first 100 consecutive Tendyne TMVR patients (mean STS score 7.7%) for up to 4 years. Technical success was 96.0% and 30-day device success 74.0%, with Kaplan-Meier survival of 90.0% at 30 days, 71.2% at 1 year and 54.8% at 3 years, and durable mitral competence (100% MR ≤1+ at 2 years) with symptom relief (84.9% NYHA I/II). There was no learning-curve effect on 30-day survival, and mid-term deaths reflected both cardiovascular and non-cardiovascular causes in this high-risk group.

GLAM-TAVI — Conduction-disturbance management strategies after TAVI
Novelli L et al. · Rev Esp Cardiol (Engl Ed) 2026 · source ↗

This ambispective six-centre study analysed 345 patients who developed conduction disturbances after TAVI, comparing a standardised guideline-directed strategy with an individualised (pacemaker-sparing) approach. The 1-year composite of death or conduction-related rehospitalisation was statistically similar (13.6% individualised vs 7.1% standardised; adjusted HR 1.23, 95% CI 0.56–2.69), but individualised management yielded a lower pacemaker rate (49.4% vs 80.2%) and higher cardiovascular mortality (8.4% vs 1.2%; adjusted HR 9.10, 95% CI 1.59–52.18). Pacemaker-sparing pathways demand structured rhythm surveillance — and the mortality signal, though striking, is observational.

— — Early pulmonary-artery diastolic pressure and survival on VA-ECMO
Tonna JE et al. · JACC Heart Fail 2026 · source ↗

This ELSO Registry analysis examined 7,654 patients on veno-arterial ECMO for cardiogenic shock, of whom 3,077 had pulmonary-artery catheters, testing the prognostic value of 24-hour on-support pulmonary-artery diastolic pressure (PAD). Catheter use itself was not associated with lower mortality (adjusted OR 0.95, 95% CI 0.897–1.006), but PAD >18 mmHg at 24 hours predicted higher 30-day mortality (HR 1.29, 95% CI 1.13–1.46), including among patients receiving left-ventricular unloading. Early haemodynamic decongestion (PAD <18 mmHg) tracks with survival.

— — Transcatheter mitral valve implantation in complex post-repair anatomies
Wahl CM et al. · Int J Cardiol 2026 · source ↗

This single-centre series reported 16 Tendyne TMVI procedures for recurrent or persistent mitral disease after prior repair (SMA, TEER, balloon valvuloplasty) or an aborted TEER attempt. Technical success was 93.8% with none/trace residual regurgitation at discharge (mean gradient 3.9 ± 1.1 mmHg), but 30-day mortality was 12.5% and MVARC device success only 63% — mostly from gradients exceeding 5 mmHg — with estimated 1- and 2-year survival of 67% and 56%. TMVI is feasible in these difficult anatomies but carries substantial residual risk.

Tier 3

Worth knowing

— — Advanced CKD and time to transcatheter heart-valve re-intervention
Verma BR et al. · Cardiovasc Revasc Med 2026 · source ↗

This retrospective study of 271 transcatheter heart-valve re-intervention patients compared those with advanced CKD (eGFR ≤45; n=27) against the rest. Advanced-CKD patients were younger (68.8 vs 74.8 years) and reached re-intervention sooner (7.4 vs 10.1 years, p=0.012), with interval eGFR decline independently linked to shorter time to re-intervention, while in-hospital outcomes were similar. The hypothesis-generating signal argues for closer valve surveillance when renal function is falling.

— — Mitral annular remodelling after edge-to-edge repair
Zancanaro E et al. · Catheter Cardiovasc Interv 2026 · source ↗

This retrospective echocardiographic study of 151 patients undergoing MitraClip M-TEER measured anteroposterior and intercommissural annular diameters before and after repair. Both diameters fell from pre-procedure to discharge (2.2 ± 3.8 and 2.3 ± 3.7 mm; both p<0.001), with further late intercommissural reduction, but acute annular change was not associated with death or heart-failure hospitalisation. M-TEER measurably remodels the annulus, though the prognostic meaning is hypothesis-generating and cross-modality measurement warrants caution.

— — Pre- vs concomitant PCI in frail, high-risk TAVR patients
Love A et al. · Am J Cardiol 2026 · source ↗

This propensity-matched TriNetX cohort (361 per arm) compared pre-TAVR with concomitant PCI in TAVR patients carrying frailty-related high-risk features. The 90-day composite of death, acute kidney injury, bleeding, ACS and stroke was similar (27.4% vs 29.4%; RR 1.07, 95% CI 0.85–1.35), as were 30-day and 1-year composites and all-cause mortality. The results support an individualised rather than fixed approach to PCI timing in frail TAVR candidates.

— — One-time vs staged multivessel PCI in NSTE-ACS
Yang SQ et al. · Cardiol J 2026 · source ↗

This systematic review pooled 10 studies (6,749 NSTE-ACS patients with multivessel disease) comparing single-sitting against staged multivessel PCI. In the randomised trials, one-time PCI reduced repeat revascularisation (RR 0.71, 95% CI 0.51–0.98) with no difference in mortality or reinfarction, while cohort and pooled analyses showed no significant differences. The limited evidence leans toward single-setting complete revascularisation reducing repeat procedures.

— — Preoperative angiographic washout and bypass-graft patency
Delbono LJ et al. · JACC Adv 2026 · source ↗

This single-centre study of 107 CABG patients (201 target vessels) tested whether neural-network-quantified contrast washout on routine preoperative angiography predicts later graft patency. A shallower contrast-intensity downslope was associated with graft failure (adjusted OR 1.77 per standard deviation, 95% CI 1.22–2.57; area under the curve 0.67), with faster washout linked to lower failure hazard (HR 0.51). An automated angiographic marker of target-vessel outflow could help flag grafts at risk.

— — Bedside transcatheter PDA closure in extremely premature infants
Sathanandam S et al. · JACC Cardiovasc Interv 2026 · source ↗

This retrospective review covered 500 extremely premature infants (<28 weeks, <2 kg) undergoing transcatheter PDA closure, 176 of them at the neonatal-ICU bedside. Bedside procedures were shorter (18 vs 32 minutes) with less radiation and 100% procedural success, major complications of 1.7% and 89% survival at a median 6.8 years. Moving the procedure to the bedside eliminates transporting critically ill neonates — a notable structural-intervention workflow shift.

CS-MORT-6 — A mortality score that refines risk within SCAI shock stages
Otabor E et al. · Int J Cardiol 2026 · source ↗

This study derived a six-variable cardiogenic-shock mortality score in MIMIC-IV (n=3,103) and externally validated it in the eICU database (n=1,047). The integer score's discrimination was 0.727 internally and 0.759 externally, and within electronic-health-record SCAI stages mortality differed by 20–47 percentage points across score tertiles. A simple bedside score could sharpen the ordinal SCAI classification, though it needs prospective and real-time evaluation.

— — Novel CMR right-ventricular indices in severe tricuspid regurgitation
Hinojar R et al. · Int J Cardiol 2026 · source ↗

This multicentre CMR study of 289 patients with severe tricuspid regurgitation tested two novel right-ventricular indices — a volumetric RV–pulmonary-artery coupling index and effective RV ejection fraction — against conventional RVEF. Both independently predicted death or heart-failure hospitalisation and improved risk discrimination beyond conventional RVEF (ΔHarrell's C-index 0.056 and 0.043; both p<0.01), and both improved after tricuspid intervention. They may better capture RV performance when planning and following tricuspid procedures.

— — Baseline and discharge blood pressure and outcomes after TAVI
Stolte T et al. · J Am Heart Assoc 2026 · source ↗

This single-centre study related pre- and post-procedure office blood pressure to 1-year MACE in 1,191 TAVI patients. Discharge systolic blood pressure below 115 or above 143 mmHg was associated with higher MACE, while a moderate 5–25 mmHg systolic fall from baseline appeared protective, and baseline blood pressure itself was not predictive. Post-TAVI blood-pressure optimisation may be an under-used, modifiable prognostic lever.

— — Longitudinal progression of coronary calcium density on serial CT Research correspondence
Chong L et al. · EuroIntervention 2026 · source ↗

This EuroIntervention research correspondence is a single-centre retrospective study tracking the same calcified lesion in native, unstented coronary arteries across serial coronary-calcium CT scans over a multi-year window. Using a standard Hounsfield-unit threshold, it quantifies the trajectory of mean calcium density alongside Agatston score, calcium volume and mass — a question relevant to how lesions might behave at future PCI. The open-access text available here is a truncated correspondence, so the numeric results should be read in the source.

CTO-VR — Remodelling of distal coronary vessels after CTO PCI Research correspondence
Griffioen AM et al. · EuroIntervention 2026 · source ↗

The CTO-VR study is a single-centre prospective observational cohort examining how the distal coronary vessel remodels in the weeks to months after successful chronic-total-occlusion PCI, where both reocclusion and late positive remodelling with stent malapposition can occur. It aims to predict distal vessel remodelling from coronary haemodynamic parameters, testing the hypothesis that lower collateral flow before restoration of antegrade flow predicts greater acute or late lumen growth. The open-access text available here is a truncated research correspondence, so quantitative results should be consulted in the source.

Landmark Revisited

DEFINE-FLAIR — Use of the Instantaneous Wave-free Ratio or Fractional Flow Reserve in PCI
Davies JE et al. · N Engl J Med 2017 · primary source ↗
Structured abstract
Background
FFR-guided revascularisation improves outcomes versus angiography alone, but it was unknown whether the adenosine-free instantaneous wave-free ratio (iFR) would deliver the same benefit.
Methods
2,492 patients with coronary disease were randomised 1:1 to iFR- or FFR-guided revascularisation; the primary endpoint was 1-year major adverse cardiac events (all-cause death, non-fatal MI, or unplanned revascularisation), with a non-inferiority margin of 3.4 percentage points.
Key results
At 1 year the primary endpoint occurred in 78 of 1,148 (6.8%) iFR patients and 83 of 1,182 (7.0%) FFR patients (difference −0.2 percentage points, 95% CI −2.3 to 1.8; P<0.001 for non-inferiority; HR 0.95, 95% CI 0.68–1.33). Adverse procedural signs/symptoms were far fewer with iFR (3.1% vs 30.8%, P<0.001) and median procedure time was shorter (40.5 vs 45.0 minutes, P=0.001).
Conclusion
iFR-guided revascularisation was non-inferior to FFR at 1 year, with fewer procedural symptoms and shorter procedures.
The question & the verdict

By 2017 FFR was the reference standard for deciding whether an intermediate stenosis deserved a stent, but adenosine administration added time, cost and patient discomfort. DEFINE-FLAIR — published back-to-back with the registry-based iFR-SWEDEHEART trial (Götberg M et al., N Engl J Med 2017; PMID 28317438: 2,037 patients, iFR 6.7% vs FFR 6.1%, HR 1.12, 95% CI 0.79–1.58) — asked whether a resting, hyperaemia-free index could match FFR. The twin verdict at 1 year was yes: iFR was non-inferior, and the resting workflow was faster and more comfortable. That dual result drove rapid guideline parity between iFR and FFR and widespread adoption of resting indices.

Statistical appraisal

Both trials were designed as non-inferiority trials against a composite endpoint dominated by unplanned revascularisation rather than death or MI, and both were powered for 12-month events. The margins (3.4 and 3.2 percentage points) were generous relative to the low event rates, and iFR led to more lesions being deferred — a difference whose consequences only emerged with longer follow-up. Crucially, non-inferiority at 1 year does not guarantee equivalence of hard outcomes over years: a modest excess hazard confined to deferred lesions can stay hidden inside a wide early confidence interval.

Subsequent substudies & follow-ups

A pre-specified study-level meta-analysis of the 5-year data from both trials (Eftekhari A et al., Eur Heart J 2023; PMID 37634144) pooled 2,254 iFR and 2,257 FFR patients and found more deferral in the iFR arm (50.0% vs 45.2%, P=.001) and, at 5 years, higher all-cause mortality (8.3% vs 6.3%; HR 1.34, 95% CI 1.08–1.67) and MACE (21.5% vs 18.6%; HR 1.18, 95% CI 1.04–1.34), with no difference in MI or unplanned revascularisation. The signal remains unexplained — chance, residual confounding across two open-label trials, or a true consequence of deferring borderline lesions — but it is exactly the pattern echoed by this week's real-world cohort finding higher mortality with iFR guidance.

Why it still matters: DEFINE-FLAIR made resting physiology a mainstream, guideline-endorsed way to guide PCI — but its long-term mortality signal is a standing reminder that 1-year non-inferiority is not the same as long-term equivalence, and that how we treat deferred lesions deserves ongoing scrutiny.
