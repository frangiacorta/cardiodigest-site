SMART-CHOICE 3 — Clopidogrel versus aspirin monotherapy after PCI: a sex-stratified analysis
Kwon W et al. · EuroIntervention 2026 · primary source ↗
Structured abstract
Background
Clopidogrel has emerged as a promising alternative to aspirin for maintenance monotherapy after percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI), but whether the optimal antiplatelet strategy differs by sex remains controversial.
Methods
A prespecified substudy of the SMART-CHOICE 3 randomised trial. Patients at high risk of recurrent ischaemic events who had completed the standard duration of dual antiplatelet therapy after PCI were randomly assigned to clopidogrel or aspirin monotherapy. The primary endpoint was a composite of all-cause death, myocardial infarction, or stroke. A total of 5,506 patients (1,002 females, 4,504 males) were enrolled in South Korea.
Key results
Females carried a higher risk profile, yet the incidence of the primary endpoint did not differ significantly between females and males (6.7% vs 5.2%; adjusted HR 0.89, 95% CI 0.62–1.28; p=0.537). Compared with aspirin, clopidogrel significantly reduced the primary endpoint in females (4.3% vs 9.4%; adjusted HR 0.45, 95% CI 0.24–0.85; p=0.014) but not significantly in males (4.4% vs 6.0%; adjusted HR 0.77, 95% CI 0.56–1.07; p=0.116). There was no significant interaction between sex and antiplatelet therapy (p for interaction=0.288).
Conclusion
In patients with remote PCI at high ischaemic risk, clopidogrel monotherapy produced a directionally consistent reduction in the composite of death, MI, or stroke in both sexes, with no significant sex-by-treatment interaction, supporting a consistent treatment effect across sexes.
Critical appraisal
Study design
Prespecified sex-stratified analysis nested in a randomised, controlled trial with a hard composite endpoint — a robust framework that preserves randomisation within each sex and reports a formal interaction test rather than relying on separate subgroup p-values.
Clinical relevance
The chronic maintenance phase after PCI increasingly favours a single antiplatelet agent; these data extend the case for clopidogrel over aspirin without signalling that women and men require different agents.
Context
The findings dovetail with this issue's Landmark, HOST-EXAM, which established clopidogrel's superiority over aspirin for long-term maintenance monotherapy; SMART-CHOICE 3 adds a modern, sex-balanced confirmation in a high-risk population.
Limitations
Women were a minority (1,002 of 5,506) and the analysis was not powered for a sex interaction; the striking apparent female-specific benefit rests on relatively few events with a wide confidence interval, and the non-significant interaction term means the eye-catching female result should not be read as a true sex difference. Single-country, East-Asian enrolment limits generalisability.
Bottom line: Clopidogrel monotherapy looks a reasonable maintenance choice after PCI in both women and men, with no convincing evidence that sex should change the agent — but the dramatic female point estimate is hypothesis-generating, not a mandate.
— — Prognostic value of coronary CT angiography-derived FFR (FFR-CT) in a large nationwide cohort
Bell JS et al. · Circulation 2026 · primary source ↗
Structured abstract
Background
The diagnostic value of coronary CT angiography-derived fractional flow reserve (FFR-CT) in stable coronary artery disease is well established, but its prognostic value for future events is less clear, with prior studies limited by small size, variable measurement points, composite outcomes, and short follow-up.
Methods
A nationwide cohort of patients who underwent coronary CT angiography with clinically indicated FFR-CT analysis (HeartFlow) for stable coronary artery disease between 2017 and 2020 across 27 sites in England, with 3-year follow-up. Lowest per-patient stenosis-specific and distal-vessel FFR-CT values were related to individual outcomes: myocardial infarction (MI), cardiovascular death, all-cause death, and revascularisation, and were split into normal (>0.8), borderline (0.71–0.8), reduced (0.51–0.7), and severely reduced (≤0.5).
Key results
Among 7,836 patients (median age 63; 37.4% women), stenosis-specific FFR-CT was associated with future MI (HR 2.08–5.73; P<0.001), cardiovascular death (HR 1.51–3.40; P<0.001), all-cause death (HR 1.01–1.58; P=0.01) and revascularisation (HR 4.79–24.48; P<0.001), with lower values conferring incrementally greater risk. After adjustment for risk factors and CAD-RADS stenosis severity, FFR-CT ≤0.7 remained associated with MI (adjusted HR 2.94–3.39; P<0.001) and ≤0.5 with cardiovascular death (adjusted HR 2.92; P=0.02). Adding FFR-CT improved discrimination for MI or cardiovascular death (C-index 0.783; ΔC-index 0.012; P<0.001), and a value of 0.67 defined the optimal risk threshold. When stenosis-specific FFR-CT was normal, the 3-year rate of MI or cardiovascular death was low (1.5%).
Conclusion
FFR-CT is associated with MI, cardiovascular death, and all-cause death independent of risk factors and stenosis severity, with reduced and severely reduced values carrying the strongest association with future events.
Critical appraisal
Study design
A large, real-world, nationwide observational cohort with individual (not composite) hard outcomes and 3-year follow-up — a substantial advance over the small single-centre datasets that have anchored FFR-CT prognosis to date.
Clinical relevance
It reframes FFR-CT from a gatekeeper to invasive angiography into a prognostic instrument: a normal value identifies a genuinely low-risk group, while severely reduced values mark patients warranting closer attention.
Context
The result sits naturally beside this week's invasive-physiology papers (FFR-guided NSTE-ACS, resting Pd/Pa vs FFR), reinforcing that a physiological signal — however acquired — carries prognostic weight beyond the anatomical picture.
Limitations
Observational design cannot establish that acting on FFR-CT changes outcomes; revascularisation is both an outcome and a treatment influenced by the test itself (confounding by indication), and the incremental discrimination gain, though significant, was small (ΔC-index 0.012). Single-vendor, single-country data.
Bottom line: A normal FFR-CT is genuinely reassuring at 3 years, and a severely reduced value should raise vigilance — but this is prognostic association, not proof that FFR-CT-guided treatment improves outcomes.
SWEDEPAD-1 — Paclitaxel-coated devices in chronic limb-threatening ischaemia with tissue loss
Sellgren F et al. · Circ Cardiovasc Interv 2026 · primary source ↗ · Trial: NCT02051088
Structured abstract
Background
Patients with chronic limb-threatening ischaemia (CLTI) and tissue loss (Rutherford 5–6) are at high risk of major amputation. Drug-coated technologies are effective in coronary interventions, but their impact on limb outcomes in advanced ischaemia remains uncertain.
Methods
A post hoc subgroup analysis of SWEDEPAD-1, a multicentre registry-based randomised trial of endovascular treatment for infrainguinal arterial disease. Adults with CLTI were randomised 1:1 to paclitaxel-coated or uncoated devices; only patients with tissue loss (Rutherford 5–6) were included. The primary endpoint was time to ipsilateral major amputation, assessed at 3 months, 1 year, and 5 years by intention-to-treat.
Key results
Among 1,761 patients (median age 77; 60.1% male; 58.4% diabetes), ipsilateral major amputation within 3 months was more frequent with paclitaxel-coated than uncoated devices (10.4% vs 7.5%; adjusted HR 1.43, 95% CI 1.04–1.96). Amputation rates were similar at 1 year (16.3% vs 14.6%) and through 5 years. Target-vessel reintervention at 1 year was lower with paclitaxel-coated devices (13.6% vs 18.0%; adjusted HR 0.77, 95% CI 0.61–0.97), but this difference attenuated when early limb loss was included in a composite, and no significant differences remained at 5 years.
Conclusion
In CLTI with tissue loss, paclitaxel-coated devices did not improve limb salvage and were associated with a higher early risk of major amputation; the 1-year reintervention benefit was not maintained once the competing risk of limb loss was accounted for.
Critical appraisal
Study design
A post hoc subgroup of a large, pragmatic, registry-based RCT with intention-to-treat analysis and hard limb endpoints out to 5 years — a rigorous setting for a question long clouded by the paclitaxel mortality controversy.
Clinical relevance
For the sickest limbs — those with tissue loss — the "restenosis-reduction" rationale for drug coating does not translate into limb salvage, and an early amputation signal counsels caution in exactly the group where operators may be tempted to reach for these devices.
Context
It nuances the broader paclitaxel debate: the concern here is not late mortality but early limb outcomes in advanced ischaemia, where wound healing and revascularisation durability, not late lumen loss, dominate.
Limitations
Post hoc subgroup analysis; the early amputation excess may reflect baseline imbalance or the biology of the most severe limbs rather than device harm, and mechanism is not established. Reintervention is susceptible to ascertainment and competing-risk effects.
Bottom line: In CLTI with tissue loss, paclitaxel-coated devices offer no limb-salvage advantage and carry an early amputation signal — reserve enthusiasm for less advanced disease until randomised, tissue-loss-specific data mature.
— — FFR-guided revascularisation of non-culprit lesions in NSTE-ACS
Paolucci L et al. · EuroIntervention 2026 · source ↗

This individual-patient-data pooling of five randomised trials (2,493 patients: 1,316 chronic coronary syndrome, 1,177 NSTE-ACS) compared FFR-guided with angiography-guided PCI stratified by coronary-syndrome subtype. The relationship between an FFR-guided strategy and 1-year MACE was similar across groups (CCS: HR 0.78, 95% CI 0.58–1.05; NSTE-ACS: HR 0.86, 95% CI 0.63–1.18; p for interaction=0.792), with concordant findings for death, MI, and repeat revascularisation and out to 5 years. The authors conclude an FFR-guided strategy can safely reduce unnecessary PCI in NSTE-ACS, though deferral of physiologically non-significant non-culprit lesions still demands clinical judgement in the acute setting.

— — Angiography-derived radial wall strain for residual risk after PCI
Huang J et al. · EuroIntervention 2026 · source ↗

Using a novel AI algorithm for automated radial wall strain (RWS) from routine angiography, this blinded post hoc analysis of the TARGET All Comers randomised trial assessed 1,384 non-target vessels in 802 patients over 5 years. A prespecified maximal-RWS cutoff of ≥13% independently predicted the non-target-vessel-oriented composite endpoint (adjusted HR 4.82, 95% CI 3.14–7.40; adjusted AUC 0.73), with the strongest signal for non-target-vessel revascularisation (adjusted AUC 0.92). As a retrospective analysis of deferred vessels, it is hypothesis-generating; prospective RWS-guided trials are ongoing.

SCAI-V — Phenotypes of cardiogenic shock after out-of-hospital cardiac arrest
Roy R et al. · EuroIntervention 2026 · source ↗

This study derived (international cohort, n=627) and externally validated (TTM-2 trial, n=1,781) a new SCAI-V classification of cardiogenic shock after resuscitated out-of-hospital cardiac arrest, combining SCAI grade with lactate (<5 vs ≥5 mmol/L) into four phenotypes. Survival fell stepwise from non-shock (78% and 68% in derivation and validation) through haemodynamic shock (60%/51%) and systemic ischaemia (50%/42%) to haemometabolic shock (20%/27%), with SCAI-V showing better model fit, discrimination and calibration than the 2019/2022 SCAI schemes in the derivation cohort and consistent model-fit advantages (discrimination comparable to SCAI-2022) in TTM-2. By separating haemodynamic from metabolic derangement, the scheme may improve selection for early invasive therapies — though it remains a prognostic framework, not a treatment trial.

IRIS-FFR — Contrasting prognosis of discordance between resting Pd/Pa and FFR
Lim SM et al. · Circ Cardiovasc Interv 2026 · source ↗ · Registry: NCT01366404

From the prospective IRIS-FFR registry, 9,737 medically-managed lesions in 7,376 patients were grouped by resting whole-cycle Pd/Pa (≤0.93) and FFR (≤0.80); discordance was present in 15.9%. Lesions with resting Pd/Pa >0.93 but FFR ≤0.80 had a higher 5-year target-vessel-failure risk (adjusted HR 1.67, 95% CI 1.09–2.55) yet a lower all-cause-death risk (adjusted HR 0.23, 95% CI 0.11–0.52) than the reverse-discordant group. FFR appears to track vessel-specific coronary events while resting Pd/Pa tracks overall survival, arguing for complementary — not interchangeable — use of resting and hyperaemic indices.

Tier 2

Notable

— — Unprotected left main disease: revascularisation versus deferral
Bröchner MM et al. · Catheter Cardiovasc Interv 2026 · source ↗

This Western Denmark Heart Registry study of 4,848 patients with isolated unprotected left main disease and chronic coronary syndrome compared PCI (n=2,819), CABG (n=467), and deferral above or below 50% stenosis. Five-year survival was 89.5% (PCI), 94.1% (CABG), 91.7% (defer ≤50%), and 84.0% (defer >50%), with revascularisation associated with better survival once stenosis exceeded 50%. PCI carried more non-fatal ACS and repeat revascularisation than CABG but similar rates to deferral — real-world support for treating significant unprotected left main disease rather than watching it.

— — QFR versus IVUS versus angiography in intermediate left main disease
Zengin İ et al. · Catheter Cardiovasc Interv 2026 · source ↗

This prospective single-centre study compared conventional angiography (CCA), quantitative flow ratio (QFR), and IVUS in 135 patients with intermediate left main lesions, using IVUS minimum lumen area <6 mm² as the anatomical reference (significant in 68.1%). QFR showed 100% specificity but only 54.3% sensitivity, while CCA offered higher sensitivity (67.4%) with 86.0% specificity; both systematically underestimated vessel dimensions versus IVUS. QFR may serve as a rule-in tool and CCA for triage, but neither replaces IVUS for anatomical characterisation and left-main procedural planning.

— — Planned versus standby V-A ECMO during high-risk PCI
Todurov M et al. · Catheter Cardiovasc Interv 2026 · source ↗

This single-centre retrospective cohort of 74 patients undergoing elective high-risk PCI compared a planned V-A ECMO strategy (n=43, ECMO before PCI) with a standby strategy (n=31, equipment prepared, ECMO only on deterioration). In-hospital MACCE was markedly lower with a planned strategy (14.0% vs 48.4%; adjusted OR 4.20 for standby, 95% CI 1.35–13.10), with shorter ICU and hospital stays. Given the nonrandomised design and the 61% of standby patients who ultimately needed ECMO, the authors frame this as hypothesis-generating rather than proof that pre-emptive support is causally protective.

J-Valve EFS — Transfemoral TAVR for symptomatic severe aortic regurgitation
Kereiakes DJ et al. · J Soc Cardiovasc Angiogr Interv 2026 · source ↗ · Trial: NCT06034028

This prospective single-arm early-feasibility study enrolled 25 high-surgical-risk patients (mean age 80.6; STS-PROM 5.6%) with symptomatic severe native aortic regurgitation for transfemoral J-Valve implantation. Successful implantation occurred in 92% (23/25); the primary composite of death or disabling stroke at 30 days was 8.0%, with 4.0% all-cause mortality at both 30 days and 1 year and none/trace AR in 95.5% at 1 year. In a lesion long resistant to transcatheter therapy, these early results are encouraging but rest on a tiny, non-randomised cohort.

— — Mechanical thrombectomy versus systemic thrombolysis in high-risk PE
Runkana A et al. · J Soc Cardiovasc Angiogr Interv 2026 · source ↗

Using the TriNetX network across 107 organisations, this propensity-matched analysis compared mechanical thrombectomy (n=1,052 after matching) with systemic thrombolysis in haemodynamically unstable, vasopressor-dependent high-risk PE. Mortality was lower with thrombectomy (RR 0.53, 95% CI 0.44–0.63; Kaplan-Meier survival 79.4% vs 61.3%), with numerically fewer haemorrhagic and ischaemic strokes. The signal is striking but hypothesis-generating given the observational design and selection effects; the authors call for the (difficult) randomised trials this question needs.

— — Early aspirin discontinuation after PCI for ACS
Oliveira VMR et al. · J Soc Cardiovasc Angiogr Interv 2026 · source ↗

This reconstructed individual-patient-data meta-analysis pooled five randomised trials (12,984 ACS-PCI patients) comparing standard 12-month DAPT with early aspirin discontinuation (≤1–2 months) followed by P2Y12 monotherapy. Transitioning to monotherapy roughly halved bleeding (HR 0.44, 95% CI 0.34–0.55) including major bleeding (HR 0.40) while preserving ischaemic protection (MACE HR 1.05, 95% CI 0.83–1.32). It strengthens the case for early aspirin withdrawal in selected ACS patients, though "selected" is doing real work — very-short regimens and highest-ischaemic-risk anatomy sit outside the reassuring average.

— — Biodegradable- versus durable-polymer DES at five years
Lee Y et al. · Catheter Cardiovasc Interv 2026 · source ↗

This single-institution propensity-matched analysis compared contemporary biodegradable-polymer (BP) and durable-polymer (DP) drug-eluting stents in 2,197 matched pairs (4,394 patients) undergoing routine PCI. Five-year target-lesion failure was identical (7.9% vs 7.9%; HR 1.01, 95% CI 0.81–1.26), with no differences in stent thrombosis or mortality, though a hypothesis-generating late survival signal favoured BP-DES in the 1–5-year landmark period (HR 0.73, nominal p=0.047). For everyday PCI, polymer type appears not to drive medium-term outcomes.

— — Transcatheter tricuspid interventions after US commercial approval
Rawlley B et al. · JACC Adv 2026 · source ↗

This Vizient database study characterised early real-world adoption of tricuspid edge-to-edge repair (T-TEER, 2,143 procedures at 180 hospitals) and transcatheter tricuspid valve replacement (TTVR, 1,301 at 113 hospitals) from October 2024 to December 2025. Monthly volumes rose for both, with TTVR's share of all interventions climbing from 31.9% to 41.6%; in-hospital mortality was 0.5% (T-TEER) and 2.0% (TTVR), and major bleeding 6.0% versus 12.4%. Baseline comorbidities mirrored pivotal trials, suggesting early adoption has tracked the evidence rather than drifting to unstudied populations.

— — LAA occlusion versus DOACs: network meta-analysis
Hiruma Y et al. · JACC Adv 2026 · source ↗

This network meta-analysis of 11 randomised trials (67,693 patients, 5,735 undergoing LAA occlusion) compared LAAO with direct oral anticoagulants for atrial fibrillation. LAAO and DOACs showed statistically similar risks for the composite of stroke, systemic embolism, and cardiovascular death (HR 1.02, 95% CI 0.88–1.19) and for major bleeding (HR 0.98, 95% CI 0.78–1.23), with Watchman and Amulet each comparable to DOACs. The analysis relies on indirect comparisons — no head-to-head LAAO-versus-DOAC trial anchors it — so it tempers rather than settles the debate.

— — Early versus delayed PCI in NSTEMI with stable heart failure
Hanif M et al. · Catheter Cardiovasc Interv 2026 · source ↗

Using TriNetX, this propensity-matched analysis (2,249 per arm) compared early (<24 h) versus delayed (≥24 h) PCI in NSTEMI patients with stable heart failure, excluding decompensated HF and STEMI. Early PCI was associated with a higher risk of the composite of death, HF exacerbation, recurrent MI, and VT/VF at both 1 month (RR 1.23) and 1 year (RR 1.12), with higher recurrent MI at both time points and similar mortality. The counter-intuitive signal in this understudied subgroup is retrospective and residual-confounding-prone, but flags that reflexive early intervention may not benefit every stable-HF NSTEMI patient.

— — Drug-coated balloon versus stenting for Medina 0.0.1 bifurcation lesions
Gad AA et al. · Catheter Cardiovasc Interv 2026 · source ↗

This prospective observational cohort compared a drug-coated-balloon (DCB) "leave-nothing-behind" strategy (n=81) with stenting (n=80) for isolated side-branch (Medina 0.0.1) bifurcation lesions, allocated by operator judgement. At 1 year the DCB group had lower late lumen loss (0.16 vs 0.48 mm), binary restenosis (7.4% vs 21.3%; NNT 7) and MACE (7.4% vs 18.7%), driven by fewer repeat revascularisations. Because allocation hinged on predilation response, the authors are explicit that this does not establish DCB superiority over DES — but it supports DCB as a viable option after good lesion preparation.

Tier 3

Worth knowing

— — TAVI with large self-expanding valves in large annuli
Alexiou P et al. · Catheter Cardiovasc Interv 2026 · source ↗

This single-centre study compared TAVI outcomes of large 34-mm Evolut valves with regular 23–29-mm valves in 488 patients, using 141 propensity-matched pairs. Procedural technical success (95% vs 94%), 30-day mortality (2.1% vs 1.4%), stroke and new pacemaker rates were comparable, as were 5-year mortality (HR 1.06, 95% CI 0.64–1.76) and reintervention. Large-annulus anatomy, historically a challenging TAVI subgroup, achieved outcomes on par with standard sizes out to 5 years in experienced hands.

— — Percutaneous mechanical aspiration of left-sided intracardiac masses
Patail H et al. · J Soc Cardiovasc Angiogr Interv 2026 · source ↗

This observational single-centre series described transseptal percutaneous mechanical aspiration of mitral-valve masses in 7 prohibitive surgical candidates (mean age 77.9; median STS 19.5%). The primary efficacy endpoint (≥70% mass removal) was met in 100%, with the composite safety endpoint in 14.3% (driven by worsening mitral regurgitation) and 85.7% 30-day survival. This off-label technique is feasible for debulking left-sided masses in inoperable patients, but the tiny sample mandates larger studies to define selection and durability.

— — Tip-detection strategy selection in CTO-PCI
Yamaji K et al. · JACC Adv 2026 · source ↗

This retrospective analysis of 73 tip-detection (TD) CTO-PCI cases compared TD intraplaque tracking (n=37) with TD antegrade dissection and re-entry (n=36). TD success exceeded 97% in both arms; ADR was chosen more often for J-CTO ≥2 (83.3% vs 62.2%) and severe calcification (47.2% vs 2.7%), with shorter crossing time favouring intraplaque tracking (75 vs 93 min) and shorter stent length favouring ADR (38 vs 46 mm). The data help map which TD approach suits which lesion morphology, though single-centre numbers are small.

ICON1 — Angiography-derived microcirculatory resistance in older NSTE-ACS
Magistri M et al. · Int J Cardiol 2026 · source ↗

In this ICON1 analysis, post-PCI angiography-derived index of microcirculatory resistance (IMRangio) was measured in 152 patients aged ≥75 with NSTE-ACS. High IMRangio (≥40 U, in 38 patients) independently predicted the composite of death, MI, revascularisation, readmission, or heart failure at both 1 year (HR 4.82, 95% CI 2.02–11.51) and 5 years (HR 4.18), improving model discrimination (1-year AUC 0.85 vs 0.74). Wire-free microvascular assessment may add prognostic value in elderly ACS, but the cohort is small and drawn from a single study.

BALI — OCT predictors of stent underexpansion in calcified lesions
Kristensen AT et al. · Int J Cardiol 2026 · source ↗

This BALI-trial substudy analysed pre-stent OCT in severely calcified lesions randomised to lesion preparation with or without lithotripsy; underexpansion (stent area ≤80% of reference) occurred in 33.9% (56/165). Embedded calcium >270° (OR 2.8), nodular calcium >90° (OR 4.1) and pre-stent area stenosis ≥20% (OR 4.3) predicted underexpansion, while lithotripsy increased calcium fractures (OR 1.8) and improved expansion at the site of maximal calcification. The work sharpens OCT criteria for adequate calcium modification before stenting.

— — Functional (FFR) optimisation after PCI: IPD meta-analysis
Ziedses des Plantes AC et al. · Clin Res Cardiol 2026 · source ↗

This individual-patient-data meta-analysis pooled eight studies (314 patients) with paired FFR before and after post-PCI optimisation. Median FFR rose significantly from 0.81 to 0.86 after optimisation, with LAD location and higher baseline post-PCI FFR predicting smaller gains, and additional stenting yielding larger increases than post-dilatation alone. It quantifies what optimisation can achieve, but stops short of showing that chasing a physiological target improves clinical outcomes.

SCOPE I — Small aortic annulus and outcomes after TAVR
Cozzi O et al. · Clin Res Cardiol 2026 · source ↗

This post hoc analysis of the SCOPE I randomised trial stratified 732 TAVR patients by CT annular size (small ≤430 mm², larger >430 mm²). Despite higher gradients and more prosthesis-patient mismatch, patients with a small annulus had no difference in 3-year mortality (18.6% vs 25.4%), heart-failure hospitalisation, or patient-reported health status. An exploratory signal suggested worse integrated outcomes when a small annulus was treated with a balloon-expandable valve — a device-selection hypothesis for prospective testing.

— — OCT in below-the-knee endovascular intervention
Li J et al. · J Soc Cardiovasc Angiogr Interv 2026 · source ↗

This prospective, multicentre, core-lab-adjudicated study protocolised angiography, IVUS, and OCT in the tibial arteries of 44 CLTI patients (92.5% Rutherford V–VI). OCT correlated well with IVUS and angiography, defined calcium best, and undiluted contrast gave longer clear imaging (70.2 vs 54.7 mm); greater OCT calcium thickness trended toward more revascularisation and amputation. Tibial OCT is feasible and its calcium characterisation may reshape below-the-knee strategy, though the study is small and correlative.

RPR — Retrograde-guided antegrade puncture reentry for retrograde CTO-PCI
Huang Z et al. · Catheter Cardiovasc Interv 2026 · source ↗

This retrospective study of 336 retrograde CTO-PCI cases evaluated a new retrograde-guided antegrade puncture reentry (RPR) technique against non-RPR and direct-crossing strategies. Despite higher lesion complexity, RPR shortened crossing time (13 vs 25 min), achieved successful reentry in 67% of primary attempts, and did not prolong bailout when it failed, with a 2.3% perforation rate and no in-hospital deaths. Calcification and occlusion length predicted RPR failure — a promising efficiency adjunct requiring prospective validation.

TAVI-TEC — AI-based automated TAVI procedural planning
Zerillo A et al. · Cardiovasc Interv Ther 2026 · source ↗

This study validated TAVI-TEC, a fully automated deep-learning framework for pre-procedural CT analysis in patients receiving SAPIEN 3 Ultra valves. It produced annular and access measurements in about 2–6 minutes with strong agreement to clinicians for annular area (concordance 0.934) and perimeter (0.909), and predicted the implanted valve size with 77.1% accuracy, most errors between adjacent sizes. Automated planning could standardise and speed TAVI workup, but sizing still needs the operator's final call.

— — Textile-based enhanced radiation protection in the cath lab
Angerås O et al. · J Soc Cardiovasc Angiogr Interv 2026 · source ↗

This single-centre cluster-randomised study alternated a textile-based enhanced radiation protection device (Texray MasterPeace) against control shielding by week (6 vs 6), measuring operator and scrub-nurse exposure with a 69-point thermoluminescent dosimetry array over personal protective equipment. The trial addresses a persistent occupational hazard — scatter radiation that lead aprons and ceiling shields leave uncovered — with a comprehensive dosimetry design. It is a practical contribution to cath-lab occupational safety, an under-studied dimension of interventional practice.

— — Mitral regurgitation after TAVR: sex and low-flow low-gradient AS
Warner ED et al. · JACC Adv 2026 · source ↗

This single-centre observational study followed 344 TAVR patients with severe aortic stenosis and moderate-or-greater mitral regurgitation (MR), stratified by classic versus low-flow low-gradient (LFLG) AS. MR improved by ≥1 grade in 62.8% and to mild-or-less in 52.9%, with similar rates in classic and LFLG AS; severe baseline MR, hypertension, and female sex were independently associated with persistent MR. The analysis helps set expectations for MR regression after TAVR, though it is retrospective and single-centre.

Landmark Revisited

HOST-EXAM — Aspirin versus clopidogrel for chronic maintenance monotherapy after PCI
Koo BK et al. (HOST-EXAM investigators) · Lancet 2021 · primary source ↗ · Trial: NCT02044250
Structured abstract
Background
Beyond the initial dual-antiplatelet period, patients who have undergone coronary stenting need indefinite antiplatelet monotherapy — but the optimal agent for the chronic maintenance phase was unknown, with aspirin used by default rather than by evidence.
Methods
An investigator-initiated, prospective, randomised, open-label, multicentre trial at 37 South Korean sites. Patients who had maintained dual antiplatelet therapy without clinical events for 6–18 months after PCI with drug-eluting stents were randomly assigned 1:1 to clopidogrel 75 mg once daily or aspirin 100 mg once daily for 24 months. The primary endpoint was a composite of all-cause death, non-fatal myocardial infarction, stroke, readmission for acute coronary syndrome, and BARC type 3-or-greater bleeding, in the intention-to-treat population.
Key results
Between March 2014 and May 2018, 5,530 patients were enrolled and 5,438 (98.3%) randomised (clopidogrel 2,710; aspirin 2,728). Over 24 months the primary endpoint occurred in 152 (5.7%) of the clopidogrel group versus 207 (7.7%) of the aspirin group (hazard ratio 0.73, 95% CI 0.59–0.90; p=0.0035).
Conclusion
During the chronic maintenance period after PCI with drug-eluting stents, clopidogrel monotherapy was superior to aspirin monotherapy in preventing the composite of adverse clinical events, significantly reducing the composite of death, myocardial infarction, stroke, ACS readmission and major bleeding.
The question & the verdict

For decades "lifelong aspirin" was the reflexive answer for the post-PCI patient who no longer needed dual therapy — a convention resting on the drug's ubiquity and cost, not on a head-to-head contest. HOST-EXAM posed the overdue question directly: once the vulnerable early period has passed, which single agent should a stented patient take for the rest of their life? The verdict was clear and, to many, surprising — clopidogrel beat aspirin on a composite that folded together ischaemia and bleeding in this maintenance setting. It converted a habit into a testable, and then falsified, assumption.

Statistical appraisal

The trial's strengths are its size, near-complete follow-up (98.2% endpoint ascertainment) and a pragmatic net-clinical composite that refuses to privilege ischaemia over bleeding. Its main caveats are equally clear: it was open-label (though the hard components limit ascertainment bias), enrolled a homogeneous East-Asian population whose bleeding and ischaemic profile may not transport to Western cohorts, and enrolled only patients who had already tolerated DAPT event-free, selecting out early failures. Extended follow-up (below) showed lower rates of both thrombotic events and bleeding, an unusual and reassuring pattern, but the absolute risk difference over two years was modest (2.0 percentage points).

Subsequent substudies & follow-ups

• HOST-EXAM Extended (Kang J et al., Circulation 2023): over a median 5.8 years, clopidogrel maintained its edge on the primary composite (12.8% vs 16.9%; HR 0.74, 95% CI 0.63–0.86), with lower thrombotic (7.9% vs 11.9%; HR 0.66) and bleeding (4.5% vs 6.1%; HR 0.74) endpoints and no difference in all-cause death (6.2% vs 6.0%). ↗

• Diabetes subgroup (Rhee TM et al., JAMA Cardiology 2023): clopidogrel's benefit held in the 1,860 patients (34.2%) with diabetes (6.3% vs 9.2%; HR 0.69, 95% CI 0.49–0.96; NNT 37) and without (5.3% vs 7.0%; HR 0.76; p for interaction 0.65), showing the effect was not diabetes-dependent. ↗

Why it still matters: HOST-EXAM turned "lifelong aspirin" from dogma into a choice, and its message runs straight through this week's issue — the SMART-CHOICE 3 sex-stratified analysis extends the clopidogrel-monotherapy case to both women and men. The open question HOST-EXAM leaves — whether its East-Asian result generalises, and whether genotype should guide the pick — is exactly what contemporary trials are now testing.
