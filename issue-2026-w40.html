Tier 1

Practice-changing

DanGer Shock — Coronary complexity and the microaxial flow pump in infarct-related cardiogenic shock
Marquard J et al. · EuroIntervention 2026 (online first) · primary source ↗
Structured abstract
Background
The prognostic weight of multivessel anatomy and lesion complexity in infarct-related cardiogenic shock is poorly characterised, and whether it modifies the benefit of microaxial-flow-pump (mAFP) support is unknown.
Methods
Secondary analysis of the DanGer Shock trial. Multivessel disease was defined as ≥1 non-culprit stenosis ≥70% or an isolated left main culprit; baseline SYNTAX scores were categorised low (≤22), intermediate (23–32) or high (>32), with isolated left main culprits analysed separately. The primary outcome was 180-day all-cause mortality. Of 355 patients, 256 had multivessel disease and 211 had an available baseline SYNTAX score (26% low, 47% intermediate, 27% high); 35 had an isolated left main culprit.
Key results
A high baseline SYNTAX score was associated with higher odds of 180-day mortality versus low and intermediate scores (adjusted OR 3.12, 95% CI 1.17–8.69). Higher baseline SYNTAX predicted a higher residual SYNTAX score (p<0.001), and the residual score independently predicted 180-day mortality. The mAFP effect was not modified by baseline SYNTAX group (p for interaction = 0.31). In the isolated left main culprit subgroup, mAFP use was associated with lower odds of 180-day mortality versus standard care (adjusted OR 0.17, 95% CI 0.03–0.99).
Conclusion
Baseline and residual SYNTAX scores independently predicted 180-day mortality; the mAFP effect was consistent across SYNTAX measures, with a hypothesis-generating benefit signal in the isolated left main subgroup.
Critical appraisal
Study design
Secondary analysis of a randomised trial; the mAFP comparison retains randomisation, but the SYNTAX associations are observational.
Clinical relevance
Anatomical complexity is a strong, independent prognostic axis in shock, yet it did not identify a subgroup that benefits more or less from the pump — supporting selection on shock physiology rather than coronary score.
Context
DanGer Shock (our Issue 2026-w25 Landmark Revisited) established a survival benefit for the microaxial flow pump in STEMI shock; CULPRIT-SHOCK had cautioned against routine immediate multivessel PCI. Residual SYNTAX here reflects completeness of revascularisation.
Limitations
Post-hoc subgroups; the isolated left main analysis rests on only 35 patients with a confidence interval (0.03–0.99) approaching unity; residual SYNTAX is confounded by procedural decisions and patient stability.
Bottom line: In infarct-related shock, coronary complexity powerfully stratifies risk but does not by itself select who benefits from mechanical support — decide on shock severity, and read the left-main signal as hypothesis-generating.
OCTOBER — Stenting technique and outcome in true bifurcation PCI
Andreasen LD et al. · JACC Cardiovasc Interv 2026 · primary source ↗
Structured abstract
Background
The optimal stenting strategy for true coronary bifurcation lesions remains debated, and few analyses use predefined intracoronary imaging.
Methods
Post-hoc analysis of the OCTOBER trial (NCT03171311) including 1,201 patients with true bifurcation PCI. Multivariable Cox regression assessed clinical outcomes and logistic regression identified predictors of a 2-stent technique.
Key results
Acute presentation (adjusted OR 1.73, 95% CI 1.33–2.25), left main bifurcation (OR 2.04, 1.38–3.03) and side-branch disease severity (lesion length OR 1.06 per mm; diameter stenosis OR 1.04 per 1%) predicted 2-stent use. In the as-treated analysis a 1-stent technique had a lower 2-year MACE rate than a 2-stent technique (9.0% vs 13.9%; adjusted HR 1.53, 95% CI 1.04–2.27), with no interaction with OCT (p=0.24). Planned-strategy analyses showed no significant MACE difference. Among DK-crush cases, angiography-guided DK-crush had higher MACE than OCT-guided DK-crush (HR 2.42, 95% CI 1.12–5.24). The lowest event rates were seen with OCT-guided 1-stent PCI.
Conclusion
Clinical indication, left main location and side-branch severity predicted a 2-stent approach; outcomes did not differ by planned strategy, whereas actual 2-stent PCI — likely marking greater complexity — carried higher MACE, and OCT guidance mitigated the risk of DK-crush.
Critical appraisal
Study design
Post-hoc analysis of a randomised OCT-guidance trial; comparisons of 1- versus 2-stent technique are non-randomised.
Clinical relevance
Reinforces a provisional (1-stent) default where anatomy allows, and makes a strong case for OCT guidance when a 2-stent DK-crush is unavoidable.
Context
DKCRUSH-V (w36 Landmark) favoured planned 2-stent DK-crush in true distal left main bifurcations; the planned-strategy neutrality here is consistent once lesion complexity is accounted for.
Limitations
The as-treated signal is confounded by indication — sicker, more complex bifurcations receive 2 stents — so the higher MACE partly reflects baseline risk rather than the technique itself.
Bottom line: Keep it to one stent when the bifurcation permits; when a 2-stent DK-crush is needed, use OCT to optimise it.
MITRA-PRO — Residual MR as a long-term predictor of mortality after mitral TEER
Rottländer D et al. · JACC Cardiovasc Interv 2026 · primary source ↗
Structured abstract
Background
The MITRA-PRO registry previously identified residual mitral regurgitation (MR) as a predictor of 1- and 3-year mortality after mitral transcatheter edge-to-edge repair (M-TEER); its long-term and time-dependent impact was unclear.
Methods
Five-year follow-up of MITRA-PRO (N=1,491). An intraprocedural MitraScore ≤3 defined mild or less residual MR and ≥4 defined relevant residual MR; adjusted hazard ratios characterised time-dependent predictors of mortality.
Key results
Five-year mortality was lower with mild or less residual MR than with more than mild residual MR (MitraScore ≤3: 55.1% vs MitraScore ≥4: 67.7%), with a near-linear relationship. In degenerative MR, 5-year mortality was 53.1% versus 73.2%; in functional MR it was 55.0% versus 64.6%. Predictors were time-dependent: early risk was driven by advanced disease and procedural factors, whereas residual MR became prognostic after 30 days and remained associated with excess long-term mortality.
Conclusion
Residual MR assessed by the intraprocedural MitraScore independently predicts 5-year mortality, underscoring the value of achieving optimal MR reduction and of quantitative multimodal intraprocedural assessment.
Critical appraisal
Study design
Large multicentre registry with 5-year follow-up and a defined intraprocedural grading tool (MitraScore).
Clinical relevance
Reinforces that the operator's on-table result matters for years — residual MR is not a cosmetic endpoint but a durable mortality signal — and favours chasing the best achievable reduction.
Context
The COAPT benefit in secondary MR was tied to the degree of MR reduction; MITRA-PRO extends that principle across degenerative and functional MR at five years.
Limitations
Observational registry data with residual confounding; the MitraScore dichotomy (≤3 vs ≥4) is a simplification of a near-linear relationship; absolute 5-year mortality is high in both groups.
Bottom line: Optimise MR reduction during M-TEER and quantify what you leave behind — residual MR at the table forecasts survival out to five years.
Multivessel TALENT — Standardised versus visual assessment of device success after PCI
Oshima A et al. · EuroIntervention 2026 (online first) · source ↗

This prespecified lesion-level substudy of the Multivessel TALENT trial (1,548 three-vessel-disease patients randomised to Supraflex Cruz or SYNERGY stents; PCI attempted in 4,052 lesions) compared operator visual device success with the EAPCI standardised definition adjudicated by core-lab quantitative coronary angiography (QCA) and angiography-derived FFR. Site-reported device success of 97.4% fell to 93.8% by Δangio-FFR, 88.0% by residual diameter stenosis <20%, and 83.2% by combined QCA+angio-FFR (all p<0.001), and lesions failing the QCA definition had more 1-year lesion-oriented composite events (12.0% vs 9.1%; log-rank p=0.041). Standardised assessment reclassified a substantial share of operator "successes" and identified higher-risk lesions, with no difference between the two stents.

AMTRAC — Contemporary self-expanding valves for TAVR
Levi A et al. · Struct Heart 2026 · source ↗

This multicentre cohort from the AMTRAC registry compared Evolut PRO/PRO+/FX with Navitor self-expanding valves using a 1:1 propensity-matched cohort (1,200 of 2,673 patients) with mixed-effects adjustment for centre clustering. One-year all-cause mortality was similar (Navitor 7.1% vs Evolut 9.2%; HR 0.75, 95% CI 0.47–1.21; p=0.24), as were in-hospital mortality (0.8%), periprocedural stroke (2.3%), pacemaker (14.4% vs 15.9%) and paravalvular leak, while the Evolut platform delivered lower mean transaortic gradients (7.3±3.9 vs 8.6±4.1 mmHg; p<0.001). Early vascular and bleeding advantages for Navitor disappeared after accounting for centre clustering, supporting an anatomy- and institution-driven approach to valve selection.

RITUAL — CABG versus PCI for isolated LAD chronic total occlusion
Sural S et al. · J Am Heart Assoc 2026 · source ↗

This retrospective, four-centre study compared CTO-PCI with coronary artery bypass grafting for isolated left anterior descending (LAD) chronic total occlusion in 297 patients (184 CTO-PCI, 113 CABG). Adjusted procedural success favoured CTO-PCI (88.6% vs 83.2%; adjusted HR 1.33, 95% CI 1.01–1.75; p=0.043), 3-year death and MACCE were comparable, and the periprocedural composite safety outcome was lower with PCI (20.7% vs 35.4%; adjusted HR 0.63, 95% CI 0.40–0.99; p=0.047). For isolated LAD CTO, PCI and surgery reached similar medium-term outcomes with fewer in-hospital complications for PCI, particularly in less complex (J-CTO <3) lesions.

Tier 2

Notable

— — Real-world comparison of balloon-expandable valves for TAVR
Talmor-Barkan Y et al. · Struct Heart 2026 · source ↗

This international registry compared Sapien 3/Ultra and MyVal balloon-expandable valves in 1,903 TAVR patients (Sapien 1,676; MyVal 227), with a propensity-matched cohort of 743. Two-year all-cause mortality and major procedural complications were similar, but MyVal had higher permanent pacemaker rates (26.6% vs 10.1%; p<0.001) and more-than-mild paravalvular leak (9.8% vs 2.1%; p<0.001), while more Sapien recipients had a mean gradient >10 mmHg (44.0% vs 32.3%; p=0.019). These platform-specific trade-offs should inform valve choice pending longer follow-up.

SUBLIME — LIRA method for TAVR sizing in bicuspid anatomy
Bellini B et al. · JACC Cardiovasc Interv 2026 · source ↗

This multicentre retrospective study assessed the bicuspid-dedicated LIRA (level of implantation at the raphe) sizing method in 215 bicuspid aortic valve patients undergoing TAVR at 6 centres, with blinded core-lab reproducibility testing. LIRA led to prosthesis downsizing in 74.4% of cases, with 30-day device success of 93%, moderate-or-greater paravalvular leak in 4.6%, new pacemaker in 10.7%, stroke in 1.9%, and 1-year mortality of 5.7%; sizing agreement was excellent (κ=0.85). The method showed good cross-centre reproducibility and favourable outcomes, supporting it as a bicuspid-specific sizing strategy.

NAViV — Intra-annular self-expanding valve for valve-in-valve TAVR
Chavez J et al. · J Am Heart Assoc 2026 · source ↗

The NAViV registry retrospectively evaluated 200 valve-in-valve TAVR procedures using the Portico/Navitor intra-annular self-expanding platform, a high-risk cohort in which 86% had small surgical bioprostheses (internal diameter ≤21 mm). Technical success was 96% with 2% in-hospital mortality; device success was 73.5% (high residual gradients being the main failure mode), mean residual gradient was 14 mmHg, and 2-year survival was 85.6%. Intra-annular self-expanding valve-in-valve TAVR showed a favourable safety profile and encouraging mid-term survival, warranting comparative study.

— — TAVI complications under VARC-3: systematic review and meta-analysis
Giacobbe F et al. · J Am Heart Assoc 2026 · source ↗

This meta-analysis pooled 57 studies and 73,588 patients reporting TAVI complications using VARC-3 definitions (mean age 81.7 years, STS-PROM 4.1%). Technical and 30-day device success were 95% and 90%, 30-day early safety was 66%, and 12-month all-cause mortality was 10.2%; the commonest complication was permanent pacemaker (13%), followed by type ≥2 bleeding (9.7%), stage 3–4 acute kidney injury (4.7%), major vascular events (4.3%) and stroke (3.0%). Acute kidney injury, type ≥2 bleeding, paravalvular regurgitation, non-structural valve dysfunction and higher STS score were associated with higher mortality.

— — TAVR and anti-cancer therapy continuity in severe AS
Zheng HJ et al. · Catheter Cardiovasc Interv 2026 · source ↗

This retrospective, inverse-probability-weighted study compared TAVR (n=101) with surgical AVR (n=82) in cancer patients with severe aortic stenosis. TAVR was associated with higher 90-day anti-cancer therapy resumption (83.7% vs 56.3%; OR 3.51), shorter time to resumption (28 vs 75 days), fewer cardiac-related interruptions (6.9% vs 17.1%) and lower 3-year cardiovascular mortality (4% vs 12%), with each 30-day treatment delay raising cancer mortality by 15% (HR 1.15). The authors suggest TAVR may be preferred when timely neoadjuvant therapy is needed, pending prospective validation.

— — Valve replacement in small aortic annuli: network meta-analysis
Chiabrando JG et al. · J Invasive Cardiol 2026 · source ↗

This network meta-analysis synthesised 20 studies and 8,843 patients with small aortic annuli across surgical and transcatheter strategies. Supra-annular self-expanding valves had less severe patient-prosthesis mismatch than intra-annular devices or surgery; balloon-expandable valves showed more hemodynamic structural dysfunction (RR 4.44), smaller indexed orifice area and higher gradients, while surgery had the worst hemodynamics but lower paravalvular leak, pacemaker and vascular complications. Despite hemodynamic differences, 1-year mortality did not differ between strategies.

SPARTAN — Drug-coated balloon versus drug-eluting stent in calcified de-novo lesions
Natarajan R et al. · Am J Cardiol 2026 · source ↗

This registry retrospectively compared drug-coated balloon (DCB, n=506) and drug-eluting stent (DES, n=551) strategies for angiographically calcified de-novo coronary lesions over a median 3.7 years. Target lesion failure was similar (11.5% DCB vs 10.9% DES; adjusted HR 1.07) and target lesion revascularisation did not differ (6.7% vs 6.4%), while target-lesion myocardial infarction was lower with DCB (1.6% vs 3.4%; HR 0.38, p=0.025). These exploratory data suggest a stentless DCB strategy is feasible in calcified disease but require randomised confirmation.

REC-CAGEFREE II — Stepwise DAPT de-escalation after DCB angioplasty in bifurcations
Yan Y et al. · Int J Cardiol 2026 · source ↗

This prespecified subgroup of REC-CAGEFREE II (1,948 ACS patients undergoing DCB-only angioplasty) compared stepwise DAPT de-escalation with 12-month standard DAPT, with 884 (45.4%) having bifurcation lesions. In the bifurcation subgroup, 12-month net adverse clinical events were similar (11.5% de-escalation vs 9.4% standard; HR 1.15) with no treatment-by-subgroup interaction, while de-escalation lowered BARC type 3 or 5 bleeding (0.5% vs 1.8%; HR 0.15, p=0.048). Stepwise de-escalation appears safe and bleeding-sparing for ACS patients with bifurcation lesions treated by DCB.

— — Location and outcomes of LAD chronic total occlusion PCI
Sural S et al. · Catheter Cardiovasc Interv 2026 · source ↗

This three-centre registry classified 644 LAD CTO PCIs as ostial (81), proximal (144) or mid/distal (419) and compared complexity and outcomes. Ostial CTOs required a retrograde approach far more often (43.2% vs 20.1% vs 14.1%) and had lower technical success (77.8% vs 89.6% vs 92.6%), with ostial location independently predicting lower success after adjustment for the J-CTO (OR 0.43) or EuroCTO (OR 0.41) score. Ostial LAD CTOs are intrinsically harder than conventional scores capture, with similar in-hospital major events.

— — No-reflow as a predictor of contrast-induced AKI and mortality in STEMI
Raimoglu U et al. · Catheter Cardiovasc Interv 2026 · source ↗

This prospective observational study of 299 STEMI patients undergoing primary PCI examined the link between the no-reflow phenomenon and contrast-induced acute kidney injury (CI-AKI). CI-AKI occurred in 14.04% (42 patients), no-reflow was far more frequent in that group (73.8% vs 15.2%) and independently predicted CI-AKI (OR 10.925, 95% CI 3.709–32.181), and CI-AKI was associated with higher all-cause mortality (21.4% vs 7.4%; p=0.008). No-reflow flags patients at combined renal and mortality risk after primary PCI.

— — Triglyceride-glucose index for risk stratification after PCI
Li B et al. · Catheter Cardiovasc Interv 2026 · source ↗

This retrospective cohort of 23,592 post-PCI patients stratified by ASCVD risk tested the triglyceride-glucose (TyG) index against 3-year cardiovascular events over a median 3.1 years. Elevated TyG independently predicted events only in the very-high-risk group (adjusted HR 1.51 for top vs bottom tertile, 95% CI 1.10–2.08), improving reclassification and discrimination, with no association in lower-risk strata. TyG refines risk specifically in the very-high-risk post-PCI population.

— — Photon-counting CT for coronary in-stent restenosis
Aramouni K et al. · Cardiovasc Revasc Med 2026 · source ↗

This single-centre diagnostic-accuracy study evaluated photon-counting detector CT against invasive angiography for significant in-stent restenosis (ISR) in 52 patients with 69 stents. Per-stent sensitivity was 75.0%, specificity 83.0%, negative predictive value 91.7% and AUC 0.79, with patient-level AUC 0.86 and numerically better performance in stents ≥3 mm. The high negative predictive value suggests photon-counting CT could help rule out ISR, pending larger prospective multicentre validation.

Tier 3

Worth knowing

— — RV–PA coupling after M-TEER in primary and atrial MR
Paulus M et al. · Int J Cardiol 2026 · source ↗

This study assessed right ventricular–pulmonary artery (RV–PA) coupling by TAPSE/PASP before and one month after M-TEER in 190 patients with primary or atrial MR. Coupling improved in 41.1% of patients, and preserved coupling (TAPSE/PASP >0.33 mm/mmHg) at one month identified a lower-risk group for death or heart-failure hospitalisation (HR 0.46, 95% CI 0.26–0.81; p=0.008). Persistent RV–PA uncoupling marks a high-risk subgroup after M-TEER.

— — T-TEER in patients with transvalvular CIED leads
Moscato L et al. · Am J Cardiol 2026 · source ↗

This meta-analysis of 8 observational studies (2,779 patients, 820 [29.5%] with transvalvular CIED leads) examined tricuspid transcatheter edge-to-edge repair (T-TEER) by lead status. Procedural success (RR 1.00), early safety and residual tricuspid regurgitation were comparable, but 1-year all-cause mortality was higher in patients with CIED leads (RR 1.33, 95% CI 1.03–1.72). Lead presence does not impair the procedure but marks a worse prognosis — a discordance between valve-level and patient-level success.

— — PASCAL versus PASCAL-ACE: 3D mitral geometry after TEER
Abusharekh M et al. · Clin Res Cardiol 2026 · source ↗

This single-centre retrospective study compared the PASCAL (n=238) and PASCAL-ACE (n=128) devices using 3D echocardiographic remodelling in 366 M-TEER patients. ACE was used in more complex anatomies with larger baseline orifice areas, yet relative 3D orifice-area reduction was similar (ACE −44.5% vs PASCAL −45.9%; p=0.066) and procedural success was high in both (100% vs 99.6%). Post-procedural orifice area was dictated by baseline anatomy rather than device configuration, supporting anatomy-guided device selection.

— — Peripheral vessel tortuosity and prognosis in transfemoral TAVI
Alvarez-Covarrubias HA et al. · Clin Res Cardiol 2026 · source ↗

This single-centre retrospective study measured a CT-derived peripheral vessel tortuosity index in 2,002 transfemoral TAVI patients, dichotomised at 1.5 per EVAR criteria. Older age, female sex, diabetes, peripheral artery disease and higher creatinine were associated with tortuosity, and a high index (>1.50) was independently associated with increased 6-month mortality (HR 1.679, 95% CI 1.098–2.567; p=0.017). Tortuosity may flag a higher-risk transfemoral population, warranting validation.

— — Building a multidisciplinary cardiogenic shock programme in Latin America
Jimenez-Franco V et al. · J Soc Cardiovasc Angiogr Interv 2026 · source ↗

This retrospective single-centre cohort described 67 cardiogenic shock patients (27 pre-team, 40 post-team) before and after implementing a dedicated shock team at a Mexican tertiary hospital. SCAI stage E was the commonest presentation (33%) among post-team patients and 80% required mechanical circulatory support; 30-day mortality fell from 63% to 25% after the team was established. The experience supports the feasibility and impact of structured shock programmes in middle-income settings.

— — Mechanical circulatory support in HCM with cardiogenic shock
Sivakumar N et al. · Cardiovasc Revasc Med 2026 · source ↗

This National Inpatient Sample analysis (2016–2022) examined 4,610 obstructive hypertrophic cardiomyopathy hospitalisations complicated by cardiogenic shock, of whom 280 (6.1%) received temporary mechanical circulatory support (mostly VA-ECMO). MCS recipients were younger and less often female, and in-hospital mortality was higher with MCS (42.9% vs 22.0%; adjusted OR 2.88, 95% CI 1.46–5.69) alongside substantially higher charges. The signal likely reflects greater illness severity in patients selected for support rather than treatment-related harm.

— — Hybrid DES-DCB versus two-stent PCI for distal left main bifurcation
Saboe A et al. · Cardiovasc Revasc Med 2026 · source ↗

This single-centre registry described 1-year outcomes in 83 patients undergoing PCI for true distal left main bifurcation disease — 52 with a hybrid drug-eluting-stent-plus-drug-coated-balloon strategy and 31 with a two-stent strategy (intravascular imaging in 92.8%). The 1-year composite of cardiac death or clinically driven target-lesion revascularisation was similar (5.8% vs 6.5%; exploratory p=1.000). These descriptive data are hypothesis-generating; larger left-main-specific studies are needed.

— — Resting full-cycle ratio versus nicorandil-based FFR
Shin Y et al. · Catheter Cardiovasc Interv 2026 · source ↗

This prospective single-centre registry compared the resting full-cycle ratio (RFR) with nicorandil-based FFR in 300 patients (399 lesions) with intermediate stenoses. Threshold-based agreement was 85.0% (κ=0.684, AUC 0.932); RFR exceeded FFR by +0.067, and agreement was high away from the FFR 0.80 boundary (91.6–92.8%) but fell to 72–73% in the flanking strata. Disagreement clustered within RFR 0.87–0.91, an exploratory gray zone requiring outcome-based validation.

— — ATP- versus nicorandil-induced FFR: long-term outcomes
Tanaka N et al. · Catheter Cardiovasc Interv 2026 · source ↗

This prospective single-centre registry followed 363 patients (543 vessels) whose treatment was guided by adenosine-triphosphate-induced FFR or intracoronary nicorandil-induced FFR over a mean 6.4 years. Across ischaemic and non-ischaemic strata, rates of acute and chronic coronary syndrome events did not differ significantly between the two hyperaemic agents, and Kaplan-Meier analyses were concordant. Intracoronary nicorandil is a practical, effective alternative hyperaemic agent for FFR.

DEFINE-FLOW — Hemodynamics of FFR–iFR discordance
Alan Y et al. · Catheter Cardiovasc Interv 2026 · source ↗

This secondary analysis of DEFINE-FLOW applied reservoir-excess pressure analysis to 249 intermediate lesions in 223 patients to explain FFR–iFR discordance. FFR-negative/iFR-positive lesions showed higher excess pressure and diastolic rate constant, and aortic excess pressure independently predicted this discordance (OR 1.230, 95% CI 1.026–1.473), whereas higher coronary flow reserve predicted the opposite FFR-positive/iFR-negative pattern. Systemic aortic reservoir behaviour helps explain why the two indices disagree.

— — CT-angiography plaque and hemodynamics for in-stent restenosis
Gao W et al. · Catheter Cardiovasc Interv 2026 · source ↗

This retrospective self-controlled study assessed pre-PCI coronary CT-angiography plaque and hemodynamic features in 52 patients with 120 stented lesions (63 with in-stent restenosis, 57 without). Lesions with ≥2 adverse plaque and ≥3 adverse hemodynamic characteristics had markedly higher restenosis risk (HR 11.02, 95% CI 1.63–16.32), and adding these features raised the model C-index from 0.762 to 0.858 (p=0.017). Non-invasive CTA features may improve prediction of in-stent restenosis in stable patients.

LIMUS FLOW — Sirolimus- versus paclitaxel-coated balloons for femoropopliteal disease
Rammos C et al. · J Am Heart Assoc 2026 · source ↗

This investigator-initiated randomised proof-of-concept trial assigned 70 patients with severe peripheral artery disease to sirolimus- or paclitaxel-coated balloons for femoropopliteal intervention. At 12 months, sirolimus-coated balloons produced greater endothelial-function improvement (40% vs 22%; p=0.023) and reduced inflammatory markers at 1 month, with no difference in primary patency or freedom from target-lesion revascularisation. The mechanistic signal did not translate into a clinical-outcome difference in this small trial.

PreHEART 3 — Prehospital chest-pain triage score, randomised
Sagel DC et al. · Heart 2026 · source ↗

This pragmatic, open-label, blinded-endpoint non-inferiority trial randomised 5,170 adults with undifferentiated chest pain attended by emergency medical services to PreHEART-score-guided conveyance versus usual care. PreHEART-guided care was non-inferior for 3-day serious adverse events (win ratio 1.01, 95% CI 0.89–1.16), increased non-conveyance (34.6% vs 24.5%), reduced 30-day emergency presentations and costs, and was associated with more ACS diagnoses and angiography in women. A validated prehospital triage score can safely guide conveyance decisions.

Landmark Revisited

CLOSE — Patent Foramen Ovale Closure or Anticoagulation vs. Antiplatelets after Stroke
Mas JL et al. · N Engl J Med 2017 · PMID 28902593 · primary source ↗
Structured abstract
Background
Earlier PFO-closure trials had been inconclusive; CLOSE asked whether patients with cryptogenic stroke and high-risk echocardiographic features would benefit from PFO closure or from anticoagulation, compared with antiplatelet therapy.
Methods
Multicenter, randomized, open-label trial. Patients aged 16–60 years with a recent stroke attributed to a PFO associated with an atrial septal aneurysm or a large interatrial shunt were assigned 1:1:1 to transcatheter PFO closure plus antiplatelet therapy, antiplatelet therapy alone, or oral anticoagulation; patients with a contraindication were routed to the non-contraindicated alternatives. The primary outcome was occurrence of stroke.
Key results
A total of 663 patients were followed for a mean of 5.3±2.0 years. In the closure-versus-antiplatelet comparison, no stroke occurred among the 238 patients in the PFO-closure group versus 14 of 235 in the antiplatelet-only group (hazard ratio 0.03, 95% CI 0–0.26; P<0.001). Procedural complications occurred in 5.9%, and atrial fibrillation was more frequent with closure (4.6% vs 0.9%; P=0.02). In the anticoagulation-versus-antiplatelet comparison, stroke occurred in 3 of 187 versus 7 of 174 patients.
Conclusion
Among patients with cryptogenic stroke attributed to a PFO with an atrial septal aneurysm or large shunt, PFO closure plus antiplatelet therapy markedly reduced recurrent stroke versus antiplatelet therapy alone, at the cost of more atrial fibrillation.
The question & the verdict

By 2012–13 three trials (CLOSURE I, PC, and the initial RESPECT analysis) had failed to show a clear benefit of device closure over medical therapy, leaving guidelines cautious. CLOSE reframed the question around anatomy: by enrolling only patients whose PFO carried an atrial septal aneurysm or large shunt — the features most likely to make the PFO causal — it isolated a population in which closure could plausibly work. The verdict was unusually clean: zero recurrent strokes in the closure arm against 14 on antiplatelets, published alongside the positive long-term RESPECT and REDUCE trials in the same issue, together turning the field decisively toward closure in well-selected young patients.

Statistical appraisal

The trial was open-label but used blinded outcome adjudication, and its three-way design with nested randomizations handled patients who had contraindications to anticoagulation or closure, pooling data to power each pairwise comparison. The near-complete event separation (0 vs 14) produced a dramatic but imprecise point estimate (HR 0.03) with a confidence interval reaching 0.26 — convincing in direction, less so in magnitude. Deliberate enrichment for high-risk anatomy maximised the effect size but limits generalisability to patients with an atrial septal aneurysm or large shunt, and the anticoagulation arm was underpowered to position oral anticoagulation against either comparator. The excess of (mostly early, often self-limited) atrial fibrillation is the principal safety trade-off and must be weighed against a low but non-trivial annual stroke risk accrued over years in young patients.

Subsequent substudies & follow-ups

• Pooled individual-patient analysis (Kent DM et al., JAMA 2021): across randomized trials, device closure lowered the annualized stroke rate to 0.47% from 1.09% with medical therapy (adjusted HR 0.41, 95% CI 0.28–0.60), with a larger benefit in patients with a high RoPE score. ↗

• Systematic review and sequential meta-analysis (Turc G et al., JAHA 2018): PFO closure reduced recurrent stroke versus antithrombotic therapy (RR 0.36, 95% CI 0.17–0.79), with the effect largest in patients with an atrial septal aneurysm or large shunt and an attendant increase in atrial fibrillation. ↗

Why it still matters: CLOSE, with RESPECT and REDUCE, made transcatheter PFO closure standard care for cryptogenic stroke in younger patients with high-risk anatomy — and its anatomy-based, RoPE-informed selection is exactly how heart-team and neurology discussions now choose who to close, while atrial fibrillation remains the trade-off to counsel.
