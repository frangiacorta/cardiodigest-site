Congress Highlights — ESC 2026

The ESC Congress 2026 Hot Lines (Munich, 28–31 August; 12 sessions, 59 trials) dominated this week's simultaneous-publication wave. Most interventional and structural late-breakers were published on presentation and appear in the tiers below with their PubMed links — TAVI-PCI/Stähli, AIR-STEMI, SWITCH-SWEDEHEART, PREMIUM, LIBREXIA-ACS, A-CLOSE and TARGET-CTCA in Tier 1–2, plus the newly added EPIDAURUS, ACASA-TAVI, NOTION-4 and TRIC-I-HF. The item below is the one interventional late-breaker still awaiting peer-reviewed publication, verified here against the congress source rather than the deterministic PubMed gate.

POPular ACE TAVI — Routine protamine reduces minor bleeding after TAVI, without touching major bleeding or death
Overduin C et al. · ESC 2026 late-breaker (Hot Line 7) · source ↗

[ESC 2026 — late-breaker, pre-publication] This investigator-initiated, double-blind, placebo-controlled randomized trial assigned 1,000 patients undergoing transfemoral TAVI (mean age 81 years; 41.2% women; more than half at high or very high bleeding risk) to routine versus selective protamine to reverse procedural heparin before sheath removal. The primary composite of all-cause death and bleeding was lower with routine protamine (18.5% vs 29.4%; P<0.001), driven by VARC-3 type 1 (minor) bleeding (15.6% vs 26.5%), with no difference in major bleeding (2.9% vs 2.9%) or major vascular complications. These figures are congress-reported (presented at ESC Congress 2026) and not yet peer-reviewed — only the trial's rationale-and-design paper is indexed on PubMed so far.

Tier 1

Practice-changing

TAVI PCI — Timing of PCI in patients undergoing transcatheter aortic-valve implantation
Stähli BE et al. · N Engl J Med 2026 · primary source ↗
Structured abstract
Background
About half of patients undergoing TAVI have concomitant coronary artery disease, and PCI is often performed before the valve, but the preferred sequence has not been established.
Methods
International, open-label, randomized noninferiority trial at 48 European centres. Patients with severe aortic stenosis and coronary artery disease were assigned 1:1 to TAVI before PCI (TAVI-first) or PCI before TAVI (PCI-first). The primary end point was a 1-year composite of death from any cause, nonfatal MI, ischemia-driven revascularization, rehospitalization related to the valve/procedure/heart failure, or life-threatening/disabling/major bleeding; the noninferiority margin was 6.6 percentage points.
Key results
986 patients were randomized (498 TAVI-first, 488 PCI-first). A primary event occurred in 105 patients (22.2%) with TAVI-first and 112 (24.2%) with PCI-first (risk difference −2.0 percentage points; 95% CI −7.4 to 3.4; P<0.001 for noninferiority). Serious adverse events occurred in 264 vs 273 patients.
Conclusion
Among patients with severe aortic stenosis and coronary disease, a TAVI-first strategy was noninferior to a PCI-first strategy for the 1-year composite end point.
Critical appraisal
Study design
Adequately powered, multicentre randomized noninferiority design directly answering a sequencing question that has until now rested on observational data and expert opinion.
Clinical relevance
Establishes that operators can prioritise the valve when the coronary lesion is not the acute problem, deferring PCI to after TAVI without a penalty — useful when access, contrast load, or haemodynamics favour treating the valve first.
Context
Complements this week's EuroIntervention state-of-the-art on TAVI and coronary interventions; guidelines have carried no firm recommendation on order of treatment.
Limitations
Open-label design; the composite pools hard events with softer ones (rehospitalization) and with major bleeding; the 6.6-percentage-point margin is wide; follow-up is only 1 year and the population is European.
Bottom line: In severe AS with coronary disease, treating the valve before the coronary is a safe default at one year — sequence PCI to the clinical situation rather than to dogma.
AIR-STEMI — Complete revascularization guided by functional coronary angiography in STEMI
Biscaglia S et al. · N Engl J Med 2026 · primary source ↗
Structured abstract
Background
Complete revascularization is recommended in STEMI with multivessel disease, but the best way to decide which non-culprit lesions warrant treatment remains uncertain.
Methods
International randomized trial in STEMI patients with multivessel disease whose culprit lesion had been successfully treated. Complete revascularization was guided either by functional coronary angiography (physiology-guided group) or by conventional angiography. The primary outcome was a composite of death, MI, cerebrovascular accident, or ischemia-driven revascularization; the primary safety outcome was contrast-associated acute kidney injury or major bleeding.
Key results
1823 patients were randomized (913 physiology-guided, 910 angiography-guided); median age 66 years, 24% women. At a median 17.9 months, a primary event occurred in 81 patients (8.9%) with physiology guidance versus 125 (13.7%) with angiography (HR 0.62; 95% CI 0.47–0.83; P<0.001). The safety composite occurred in 42 (4.6%) vs 65 (7.1%) patients (HR 0.63; 95% CI 0.43–0.93; P=0.02).
Conclusion
Functional-angiography-guided complete revascularization lowered the risk of the ischaemic composite — and the safety composite — compared with angiography-guided treatment.
Critical appraisal
Study design
Large multicentre randomized trial with a clinically meaningful, adjudicated composite and a concurrent safety end point; effect sizes are substantial and internally consistent.
Clinical relevance
Extends the physiology-versus-angiography debate into the STEMI setting, where prior complete-revascularization trials (e.g. COMPLETE) leaned on angiography; angiography-derived functional assessment avoids extra wire passes and contrast.
Context
Sits alongside FAME-era data showing physiology-guided lesion selection reduces unnecessary stenting; here the safety benefit (less contrast injury/bleeding) is as notable as the ischaemic one.
Limitations
Non-culprit assessment in the acute phase can be confounded by microvascular disturbance; the specific functional index and its thresholds should be examined in the full report, and blinding of an angiography-based strategy is inherently limited.
Bottom line: In multivessel STEMI, let angiography-derived physiology — not the eye — choose which non-culprit lesions to treat: fewer hard events and less procedural harm.
SWITCH-SWEDEHEART — Prasugrel versus ticagrelor in acute coronary syndromes
Omerovic E et al. · N Engl J Med 2026 · primary source ↗
Structured abstract
Background
Whether prasugrel or ticagrelor should be the preferred P2Y12 inhibitor after an acute coronary syndrome remains uncertain.
Methods
Registry-based, open-label, stepped-wedge, cluster-randomized trial in Sweden in adults who had undergone PCI for ACS. Seven regions in three clusters switched from ticagrelor to prasugrel as the default P2Y12 inhibitor in a randomized sequence across four 9-month periods (2021–2024). The primary end point was a composite of death from any cause, fatal or nonfatal MI, or fatal or nonfatal stroke through 1 year, ascertained from national registries.
Key results
17,095 patients were enrolled (9444 under a default ticagrelor policy, 7651 under prasugrel); mean age 69.8 years, 36.8% at least 75, 27.8% female; 39.4% STEMI, 43.7% NSTEMI, 16.8% unstable angina. At 1 year a primary event had occurred in 11.8% (ticagrelor policy) vs 11.1% (prasugrel policy) — adjusted OR 0.90 (95% CI 0.77–1.06). Major bleeding was 4.4% vs 4.2% (adjusted OR 0.80; 95% CI 0.64–0.99).
Conclusion
In an unselected post-PCI ACS population, a default prasugrel policy did not lower ischaemic events compared with ticagrelor.
Critical appraisal
Study design
A pragmatic, policy-level cluster-randomized trial embedded in SWEDEHEART — its real-world, all-comers population is a strength that head-to-head enrolment trials lack.
Clinical relevance
Argues against a blanket switch to prasugrel for ischaemic protection; the choice can rest on tolerability, adherence, cost and bleeding profile rather than a presumed efficacy gap.
Context
Contrasts with ISAR-REACT 5, which favoured prasugrel in a conventionally randomized ACS trial; the policy-level estimate here is diluted by real-world crossover and non-adherence.
Limitations
Open-label; a policy (intention-to-treat by default drug) rather than patient-level randomization, so on-treatment effects are attenuated; registry end-point ascertainment; the bleeding estimate is exploratory.
Bottom line: At the population level prasugrel and ticagrelor are a wash for ischaemic events after ACS — pick the agent that best fits the patient in front of you.
NATURE — Stent-retriever thrombectomy in myocardial infarction
Valgimigli M et al. · J Am Coll Cardiol 2026 · source ↗

This open-label, multicentre superiority trial randomized 156 STEMI patients with large angiographic thrombus burden (TIMI thrombus grade ≥3) undergoing primary PCI within 8 hours to stent-retriever thrombectomy plus conventional PCI or PCI alone. Infarct size by CK-MB area under the curve was lower with the stent retriever (median 3965 vs 5250 IU/L·h; difference −1359, 95% CI −2231 to −522; p=0.001), and CMR infarct size was 17% vs 28% of the left ventricle. The trial is a proof-of-concept for a device-based approach to the high-thrombus subgroup, but it was powered for infarct size rather than clinical events (MACE was minimal in both arms).

LIBREXIA-ACS — Milvexian with antiplatelet therapy after an acute coronary syndrome
Gibson CM et al. · N Engl J Med 2026 · source ↗

This phase 3 trial randomized 14,194 patients to the oral factor XIa inhibitor milvexian 25 mg twice daily or placebo added to standard antiplatelet therapy within 7 days of an ACS event. After a planned interim analysis the trial was terminated for futility: a primary event (CV death, MI, or ischemic stroke) occurred in 5.4% with milvexian vs 5.1% with placebo (HR 1.05; 95% CI 0.91–1.21; P=0.50). Milvexian did not reduce ischaemic events, though BARC 3c/5 bleeding was identical (0.3% each) — leaving factor XIa inhibition unproven at this dose in this setting.

PREMIUM — Aspirin omission at the time of primary PCI in STEMI
Takahashi K et al. · N Engl J Med 2026 · source ↗

This multicentre, open-label Japanese trial randomized 2216 STEMI patients undergoing primary PCI to low-dose prasugrel monotherapy or dual antiplatelet therapy (aspirin plus low-dose prasugrel) for 12 months, testing noninferiority (margin 1.50). Death, stroke, or MI at 12 months occurred in 11.0% with monotherapy vs 8.5% with DAPT (HR 1.34; 95% CI 1.02–1.75; P=0.40 for noninferiority — noninferiority not met), while major bleeding was lower with monotherapy (5.6% vs 8.4%; HR 0.66; 95% CI 0.47–0.91). Dropping upfront aspirin traded a real reduction in bleeding for more ischaemic events, so aspirin should not be omitted at the front end of primary PCI.

PRAGUE-26 — Catheter-directed thrombolysis in intermediate-high-risk pulmonary embolism
Kroupa J et al. · N Engl J Med 2026 · source ↗

This multicentre, open-label trial randomized 558 patients with intermediate-high-risk acute PE (haemodynamically stable, RV dysfunction plus elevated troponin or natriuretic peptide) to catheter-directed thrombolysis with alteplase plus anticoagulation or anticoagulation alone. The 7-day composite of death, recurrent PE, or cardiorespiratory decompensation/collapse occurred in 0.7% vs 6.8% (relative risk 0.10; 95% CI 0.02–0.44; P<0.001), driven mainly by less decompensation. Clinically relevant bleeding was similar (4.6% vs 5.0%), with 2 intracranial haemorrhages in the thrombolysis arm and none in standard care — a randomized signal that CDT prevents early deterioration in this risk band.

HALO-SCE — Half-dose rivaroxaban vs antiplatelet therapy after left atrial appendage occlusion
Wang K et al. · Eur Heart J 2026 · source ↗

This multicentre randomized trial assigned 164 patients with successful LAAO (confirmed at 45 days) to half-dose rivaroxaban 10 mg daily or antiplatelet therapy, with serial diffusion-weighted MRI and cognitive testing. New silent cerebral embolic lesions occurred in 12.2% with rivaroxaban vs 31.7% with antiplatelet therapy (P=0.005), and at 365 days both MMSE and MoCA scores favoured rivaroxaban. The composite of death, thromboembolism, and major bleeding was 2.4% vs 11.0% (P=0.057) — a hypothesis-generating case that anticoagulation-eligible LAAO patients may do better on low-dose rivaroxaban than on antiplatelets.

TRIC-I-HF — Transcatheter tricuspid-valve repair in severe tricuspid regurgitation
Hausleiter J et al. · N Engl J Med 2026 · ESC 2026 Hot Line · source ↗

This multicentre trial randomized 360 patients with symptomatic severe tricuspid regurgitation (mean age 80.3 years, 56.4% women) to transcatheter tricuspid-valve repair plus medical therapy (n=237) or medical therapy alone (n=123). The first primary end point — a hierarchical composite of death, heart-failure hospitalization, and quality-of-life improvement at 1 year — favoured repair with a win ratio of 2.42 (95% CI 1.76–3.33; P<0.001); the second primary end point, 3-year freedom from death or heart-failure hospitalization, was 52.4% with repair versus 21.0% with medical therapy (HR 0.40; 95% CI 0.29–0.55; P<0.001), with 30-day major adverse events in 5.9%. Transcatheter tricuspid repair improved hard clinical outcomes — not just symptoms — over medical therapy alone in this randomized trial.

Tier 2

Notable

FUSION — CT-derived FFR in the diagnostic pathway of stable CAD
Sharma SP et al. · J Am Coll Cardiol 2026 · source ↗

This investigator-initiated multicentre RCT randomized 528 patients with 50–90% stenosis on coronary CT angiography to FFRCT-guided care or usual care. The rate of invasive angiography without obstructive CAD at 90 days fell from 33% to 18% (odds ratio 0.46; 95% CI 0.31–0.69; P<0.001), and overall invasive angiography use dropped from 51% to 39% (P=0.004). Revascularization rates were identical (20% vs 20%), so FFRCT acts as a gatekeeper that spares negative catheterisations without denying needed treatment.

ViVA — Vericiguat for coronary vasospasm
Namba HF et al. · J Am Coll Cardiol 2026 · source ↗

This double-blind, placebo-controlled crossover trial gave 57 ANOCA patients with epicardial and/or microvascular vasospasm 10 weeks each of the soluble-guanylate-cyclase stimulator vericiguat and placebo. Vericiguat did not improve peripheral vasodilator function during acetylcholine iontophoresis (effect −1221 APU·s; 95% CI −4237 to 1796; p=0.42), and the posterior for daily angina episodes slightly favoured placebo (OR 0.84). It was well tolerated with no treatment-related serious adverse events, but showed no efficacy signal in vasospastic angina.

BRIDGE — Protocol-based early intensive lipid management after ACS
Oyama K et al. · J Am Coll Cardiol 2026 · source ↗

This cluster-randomized trial assigned 10 centres (329 ACS patients) to a protocol-based algorithm — in-hospital high-intensity statin, ezetimibe and PCSK9 inhibitors with 4-week reassessment — or standard lipid care. LDL-C <70 mg/dL at 6 months was achieved in 86.4% vs 73.7% (between-group difference 12.6 percentage points; 95% CI 3.8–21.5; P=0.005), and LDL-C <55 mg/dL in 60.8% vs 34.5% (P<0.001). A structured, front-loaded pathway markedly improves attainment of guideline LDL targets that routine care leaves unmet.

A-CLOSE — Clopidogrel monotherapy or extended DAPT in high-ischaemic-risk patients
Lee SJ et al. · N Engl J Med 2026 · source ↗

This open-label Korean noninferiority trial randomized 3203 high-risk patients 12 months after drug-eluting stent implantation to clopidogrel monotherapy or extended clopidogrel-plus-aspirin. Net adverse clinical events at 24 months were 5.0% vs 5.1% (risk difference −0.1 percentage points; 90% CI −1.3 to 1.2; P=0.001 for noninferiority), but the ischaemic composite was higher with monotherapy (3.7% vs 1.6%; HR 2.33) while bleeding was lower (1.8% vs 4.1%; HR 0.43). Monotherapy is noninferior for the net outcome but re-balances risk from bleeding toward ischaemia — a decision to individualise.

TARGET-CTCA — Targeted CT coronary angiography in acute chest pain
Lee KK et al. · N Engl J Med 2026 · source ↗

This UK multicentre trial randomized 3170 emergency-department patients in whom MI had been ruled out but troponin indicated intermediate risk to outpatient CT coronary angiography-guided care or standard care. After a median 3.0 years, MI or cardiac death occurred in 7.1% vs 7.3% (adjusted HR 0.95; 95% CI 0.73–1.23; P=0.71). Routine CTCA after a ruled-out MI did not lower subsequent events, arguing against reflex downstream imaging in this group.

— — Drug-coated balloon vs drug-eluting stent for bypass-graft lesions
Aikawa T et al. · JACC Cardiovasc Interv 2026 · source ↗

This nationwide Japanese J-PCI OUTCOME registry analysis compared DCB-only (30.2% of 610 PCIs) with DES for coronary bypass-graft lesions. One-year MACE (all-cause death, ACS, stroke) did not differ (10.3% DES vs 8.6% DCB; P=0.69), and after adjustment the DCB-only strategy carried no significant difference in risk (HR 1.01; 95% CI 0.58–1.76). A stentless approach appears a reasonable option for degenerated grafts, pending randomized confirmation.

— — Geographic heterogeneity of IVUS- vs angiography-guided PCI
Bulluck H et al. · Eur Heart J 2026 · source ↗

This meta-analysis of 17 randomized trials (14,033 patients) compared IVUS- with angiography-guided drug-eluting-stent PCI, pre-specifying geography as effect modifier. Pooled cardiac death fell with IVUS (RR 0.71; 95% CI 0.53–0.94), but the benefit was concentrated in East Asian trials (RR 0.56) versus non-Asian trials (RR 1.23; interaction P<0.001), whereas the reduction in definite stent thrombosis (RR 0.39) was consistent across regions. Differences in how IVUS findings are used to optimise implantation — not the imaging itself — likely explain why some European trials read neutral.

SPECTRUM — Primary PCI guided by high-definition IVUS
Groenland FTW et al. · Catheter Cardiovasc Interv 2026 · source ↗

This prospective single-centre study performed protocol HD-IVUS pullbacks in 200 haemodynamically stable STEMI patients undergoing primary PCI. IVUS-guided optimization was performed in 41.1%, raising the proportion meeting predefined optimal-PCI criteria from 52.1% to 65.5% (P<0.001), and target-vessel failure at 12 months was low at 3.0% (95% CI 1.1–6.4). The first dedicated primary-PCI IVUS study shows the technique is feasible and safe in the acute setting, generating a hypothesis for a randomized trial.

SHIELD-MI — Intravenous dexrazoxane for haemorrhagic myocardial infarction
Vora KP et al. · Eur Heart J 2026 · source ↗

This single-centre, non-randomized, placebo-controlled sequential-cohort phase IIa study gave 25 reperfused STEMI patients intravenous dexrazoxane around primary PCI and matched them to 25 placebo-treated comparators, with a blinded CMR core lab. Dexrazoxane was associated with higher LVEF (39.8% vs 34.7%; P=0.048), lower intramyocardial-haemorrhage volume (2.0% vs 6.3% of LV; P=0.004), and smaller infarcts (29.1% vs 43.8%; P=0.002). These exploratory findings nominate intramyocardial haemorrhage as a druggable target and justify a randomized trial.

NL-CFT — Long-term outcomes of invasive coronary function testing in ANOCA
Crooijmans C et al. · Eur Heart J 2026 · source ↗

This analysis of the NL-CFT registry followed 1355 patients with angina and non-obstructive coronary arteries who underwent invasive coronary function testing, comparing coronary vasomotor dysfunction with normal testing. Testing was abnormal in 79.8%, and over a mean 33 months the composite of MACE and emergency angina visits was nearly doubled with vasomotor dysfunction (31.2% vs 16.8%; P<0.001), driven by emergency care for angina, while hard events (death, MI, stroke, revascularization) were low and similar. Vasomotor dysfunction carries a heavy symptom burden even where prognosis for hard events is benign.

EPIDAURUS — Potent P2Y12 inhibitors with a DOAC in atrial fibrillation and acute coronary syndrome
Rizas KD et al. · Nat Med 2026 · ESC 2026 Hot Line · source ↗

This open-label trial randomized patients with atrial fibrillation and an acute coronary syndrome undergoing PCI to dual antithrombotic therapy pairing a DOAC with either a potent P2Y12 inhibitor or clopidogrel, with efficacy (recurrent ischaemic events) and safety (death and major bleeding) co-primary endpoints assessed by win–loss analysis at 6 weeks. The trial was stopped early by the data-safety board after 602 of a planned 1,474 patients: the potent-P2Y12 arm had more Bleeding Academic Research Consortium type ≥2 and ≥3 bleeding without a clear reduction in ischaemic events. The findings argue against pairing prasugrel or ticagrelor with a DOAC in this population — clopidogrel remains the P2Y12 inhibitor of choice.

ACASA-TAVI — NOAC vs aspirin monotherapy after TAVI
Dodgson CS, Lie ØH et al. · JAMA 2026 · ESC 2026 Hot Line · source ↗

This Norwegian open-label, blinded-endpoint trial randomized 360 patients aged 65–80 (mean 74.5 years, 37% women) without another anticoagulation indication to 12 months of factor Xa inhibitor NOAC monotherapy or aspirin monotherapy after TAVI. The primary efficacy endpoint — hypoattenuated leaflet thickening (HALT) on blinded 4D-CT at 12 months — occurred in 16.2% with NOAC versus 28.6% with aspirin (risk ratio 0.55; 95% CI 0.37–0.82; P=0.004), while the safety composite (VARC-3 bleeding, thromboembolism, death) was 7.5% vs 10.6% (risk difference −3.3%; 95% CI −9.5 to 2.8; P<0.001 for noninferiority). In selected younger, lower-risk TAVI recipients, NOAC monotherapy cut subclinical leaflet thrombosis and was noninferior for bleeding.

NOTION-4 — Three-month DOAC vs single antiplatelet therapy for leaflet thrombosis after TAVR
Jørgensen TH, De Backer O et al. · J Am Coll Cardiol 2026 · ESC 2026 Hot Line · source ↗

This randomized trial assigned 347 evaluable TAVR patients without an oral-anticoagulation indication to lifelong single antiplatelet therapy (SAPT) or 3 months of a direct oral anticoagulant followed by SAPT. HALT at 3 months was far less common with the DOAC (12.1% vs 31.8%), but by 1 year — after the DOAC had been stopped — the difference had vanished (28.3% vs 32.2%; risk difference −3.9%; 95% CI −14.4 to 6.6; P=0.54), while the composite of death, stroke, or major/life-threatening bleeding was higher in the DOAC arm (8.2% vs 2.3%; risk difference 5.9%; 95% CI 1.2 to 10.6). Read against ACASA-TAVI, the message is that a short DOAC course only defers leaflet thrombosis at a bleeding cost, whereas continued anticoagulation is what durably suppresses it.

Tier 3

Worth knowing

— — Long-term stroke recurrence after PFO closure (Lucerne registry)
Möller DF et al. · Catheter Cardiovasc Interv 2026 · source ↗

This consecutive registry of 331 patients undergoing percutaneous PFO closure (80% for prior cryptogenic stroke) reported 99% technical success and a stroke-recurrence rate of just 0.39 per 100 patient-years over a median 3.7 years. Double-disc devices achieved higher effective closure than suture-based closure (97% vs 68%; P<0.001), and atrial fibrillation was newly documented in 5.4%. Residual shunt and post-procedure AF remain the outcomes to watch.

— — Neointimal healing after extraplaque vs intraplaque CTO recanalization
Selwanos PPS et al. · Catheter Cardiovasc Interv 2026 · source ↗

This prospective single-centre OCT study assessed vascular healing in 50 patients (51 CTOs) recanalized by extraplaque or intraplaque technique, using a 6-month Neointimal Healing Score. Healing scores were statistically comparable (18 ± 7.4 extraplaque vs 14.1 ± 8.4 intraplaque; P=0.51), as was target-vessel failure (27% vs 22%; P=0.73). Contemporary extraplaque tracking shows only a non-significant numerical trend toward poorer healing in a small sample.

— — Real-world use of intracoronary pressure-wire assessment (BCIS)
Bashar H et al. · Catheter Cardiovasc Interv 2026 · source ↗

This British Cardiovascular Intervention Society database study covered 303,453 PCIs (2012–2020), of which only 10.74% used pressure-wire assessment, rising modestly from 9.1% to about 13%. Pressure-wire use was associated with lower in-hospital MACCE (adjusted OR 0.73; 95% CI 0.62–0.87) and fewer procedural complications (adjusted OR 0.90). Physiology remains under-used in UK practice, and its outcome association is hypothesis-generating rather than causal.

— — Operator and institutional PCI volume and outcomes
Batchelor RJ et al. · Cardiovasc Revasc Med 2026 · source ↗

This Victorian Cardiac Outcomes Registry analysis of 117,237 PCIs (2015–2024) classified operators and institutions by annual volume and assessed in-hospital mortality. High-volume operators performed 72.0% of procedures and managed more high-risk presentations, yet after adjustment neither operator nor institutional volume was independently associated with mortality — although quality metrics (radial access, imaging use, door-to-balloon time) were better at high volume. The findings suggest performance monitoring may matter more than raw volume thresholds.

— — Glucocorticoids to prevent conduction injury after TAVI
Suruagy-Motta RFO et al. · Cardiovasc Revasc Med 2026 · source ↗

This systematic review and meta-analysis pooled 9 studies (8841 patients) of peri-procedural glucocorticoids versus standard care in TAVI. Steroids did not reduce permanent pacemaker implantation (RR 0.91), new left bundle-branch block (RR 0.94), or mortality (RR 0.94), and were associated with more bleeding (RR 1.39) and vascular complications (RR 1.86). The data argue against routine anti-inflammatory prophylaxis for conduction disturbance.

— — Two femoral closure strategies during TAVI
Roussel T et al. · Catheter Cardiovasc Interv 2026 · source ↗

This prospective single-centre study compared a hybrid closure strategy (one Perclose ProStyle plus one Angio-Seal) with the conventional double-Perclose technique in 633 transfemoral TAVI procedures. VARC-3 vascular complications were similar (hybrid 4.8% vs non-hybrid 5.0%; P=1.000), with no difference in major complications (2.2% vs 1.9%). Pre-procedural antithrombotic therapy — not the closure device choice — was the main predictor of complications.

— — Acurate Neo vs Neo 2 and the impact of midframe under-expansion
Crusius L et al. · Catheter Cardiovasc Interv 2026 · source ↗

This study compared 124 Acurate Neo with 133 Acurate Neo 2 TAVI recipients, assessing VARC-3 device success and early safety. Neo 2 achieved higher device success (94.7% vs 86.3%; P=0.020) and early safety (87.2% vs 74.2%; P=0.008), with less mild/moderate paravalvular leak. Midframe under-expansion occurred in 13.3% and was linked to worse 1-year outcomes regardless of valve generation — a modifiable procedural target.

— — Single vs double Perclose after large-bore transfemoral access
Thakurathi P et al. · Cardiovasc Revasc Med 2026 · source ↗

This systematic review and meta-analysis of 14 studies (6487 patients) compared single- with the default double-Perclose strategy for large-bore transfemoral closure. Device success (RR 1.02), overall vascular complications, major bleeding, and 30-day mortality (RR 0.99) did not differ, but single-Perclose was associated with a lower risk of arterial dissection (RR 0.32; 95% CI 0.15–0.70; P=0.004). A single-device approach appears comparable for most outcomes and may reduce dissection.

— — ZephyrCS covered stent for transcatheter closure of sinus venosus defects
Sagar P et al. · Catheter Cardiovasc Interv 2026 · source ↗

This retrospective single-centre study analysed 100 consecutive transcatheter sinus venosus defect closures using the balloon-expandable ZephyrCS covered stent. Procedural success was 98% (two embolizations required surgery), a single-stent strategy sufficed in 79%, and 73.5% had no residual shunt at 1-year transoesophageal echocardiography. Asymptomatic intraluminal stent thrombosis (10.2%) and minor fabric leak (11.2%) were not clinically significant but warrant surveillance.

— — Vasomotor dysfunction in residual or recurrent angina after PCI
Crooijmans C et al. · EuroIntervention 2026 · research correspondence · source ↗

First carried as a qualitative pointer in Issue 2026-w33, this EuroIntervention research correspondence is now covered from its own text. Drawing on the NL-CFT programme, it characterises patients undergoing coronary function testing for angina after prior PCI — where residual or recurrent angina occurs in up to half of patients — and compares them with ANOCA patients who have not had PCI. Its companion NL-CFT outcomes analysis (see Tier 2 above) shows that confirmed vasomotor dysfunction in this population carries a substantial angina-driven event burden.

— — Wall shear stress in eruptive vs non-eruptive calcified nodules
Gallo D et al. · EuroIntervention 2026 · research correspondence · source ↗

First carried as a qualitative pointer in Issue 2026-w33, this EuroIntervention flashlight is now covered from its own text. The authors built a computational-fluid-dynamics model fusing coronary CT angiography and OCT data from two stable patients — one with an eruptive and one with a non-eruptive calcified nodule — to compare wall-shear-stress metrics (time-averaged wall shear stress and the topological shear variation index, a marker of flow disturbance linked to plaque instability). In this proof-of-concept, the eruptive nodule showed higher blood velocities in the nodular region, suggesting the two nodule phenotypes create distinct local haemodynamic environments.

Landmark Revisited

DKCRUSH-V — Double Kissing Crush versus Provisional Stenting for Left Main Distal Bifurcation Lesions
Chen SL et al. · J Am Coll Cardiol 2017 · primary source ↗
Structured abstract
Background
Provisional stenting (PS) is the most common technique for distal left main (LM) bifurcation lesions, but the planned double-kissing (DK) crush 2-stent technique — proven superior to culotte in LM and to PS in non-LM bifurcations — had never been compared with PS in LM bifurcations.
Methods
482 patients from 26 centres in 5 countries with true distal LM bifurcation lesions (Medina 1,1,1 or 0,1,1) were randomized to PS (n=242) or DK crush (n=240). The primary end point was 1-year target lesion failure (TLF): cardiac death, target-vessel MI, or clinically driven target-lesion revascularization, with routine 13-month angiographic follow-up.
Key results
TLF at 1 year occurred in 26 patients (10.7%) with PS versus 12 (5.0%) with DK crush (HR 0.42; 95% CI 0.21–0.85; p=0.02). DK crush also lowered target-vessel MI (2.9% vs 0.4%; p=0.03) and definite/probable stent thrombosis (3.3% vs 0.4%; p=0.02), with numerically lower clinically driven TLR (7.9% vs 3.8%; p=0.06) and angiographic restenosis within the LM complex (14.6% vs 7.1%; p=0.10). Cardiac death did not differ.
Conclusion
A planned DK crush 2-stent strategy reduced 1-year TLF versus provisional stenting for true distal LM bifurcation lesions.
The question & the verdict

By the mid-2010s, PCI had become an accepted alternative to surgery for many patients with unprotected left main disease, but the true distal bifurcation — where the lesion involves the ostia of both the LAD and circumflex — remained the technical crux. The default philosophy was minimalism: stent the main vessel and treat the side branch only if it declared itself (provisional stenting). DKCRUSH-V asked whether, for genuine two-branch (Medina 1,1,1 / 0,1,1) disease, a disciplined, planned two-stent DK crush would beat that reflex. The verdict was unambiguous within its population: planned DK crush halved 1-year target lesion failure and, strikingly, nearly abolished the early stent-thrombosis and target-vessel-MI signal that has always haunted complex bifurcation PCI.

Statistical appraisal

The primary end point was a device-oriented composite (TLF) analysed as time-to-event, powered for a superiority comparison; the hazard ratio of 0.42 was driven predominantly by revascularization and target-vessel MI rather than by mortality, which the trial was never sized to move. Randomization was concurrent and multinational, but the trial was open-label — unavoidable given the procedures differ — so the softer, operator-adjudicated component (clinically driven TLR) is the most susceptible to bias, and it reached only borderline significance on its own (p=0.06). The result is also a technique-and-operator result: DK crush is demanding, and these were experienced bifurcation operators using mandated intravascular-imaging-informed protocols, which limits generalizability to occasional operators. The routine angiographic follow-up (a per-protocol "oculostenotic" prompt) can inflate revascularization counts in both arms, and the modest sample (482) yields wide confidence intervals on the individual components.

Subsequent substudies & follow-ups

• DKCRUSH-V 3-year outcomes (Chen X et al., JACC Cardiovasc Interv 2019): the benefit persisted and widened — TLF 8.3% with DK crush versus 16.9% with PS (p=0.005), driven by less target-vessel MI (1.7% vs 5.8%) and target-lesion revascularization (5.0% vs 10.3%). ↗

• EBC MAIN (Hildick-Smith D et al., Eur Heart J 2021): a European randomized trial reached the opposite emphasis — a stepwise provisional strategy was not inferior to systematic dual stenting, with a 12-month composite of death/MI/TLR of 14.7% vs 17.7% (HR 0.8; 95% CI 0.5–1.3; P=0.34) — reflecting different anatomy, operators and a "provisional-first" ethos. ↗

Why it still matters: DKCRUSH-V is the trial that legitimised a planned two-stent strategy for true distal left-main bifurcations — but read alongside EBC MAIN it defines the real lesson: reserve DK crush for genuine two-branch disease and experienced hands, and default to provisional elsewhere.
